# Faculty guide: The Missing CBC

## At a glance

Allow 22 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: separate a verified result from successful report delivery; trace the same result through the lis, message queue and chart; justify the first local check. PIER: 1.4.

## Expected answers


## Learning objective

By the end of the lesson, the learner should be able to identify the hardware, software, network connections, and interfaces involved in electronic result reporting.

## Case mechanism

- The ED sees the CBC as pending and does not know whether testing is complete.
- The hematology lab checks the LIS and finds a final result verified at 09:42.
- The result reached the interface engine, where it joined a growing outbound queue.
- The interface server and application are running.
- A hospital firewall change blocks the connection from the interface server to the vendor-hosted EHR.
- hospital IT corrects the firewall setting. The LIS team checks the queue and confirms that reporting resumes.
- The laboratory confirms that delayed and new reports reached the correct patient charts and reconciles results communicated during downtime.

## Discussion points

Ask learners what finding clears each earlier part of the path. A final result in the LIS clears neither the interface nor the EHR. A running interface application also does not prove that it can connect to the receiving system.

The lesson distinguishes the analyzer's analytical work from the postanalytic result-reporting pathway. The network is essential to delivering the report, but it is not part of the analyzer's analytical process.

Do not imply that every institution organizes support in the same way. The LIS team may be part of hospital IT or may be housed in the pathology department. The roles shown here describe the work that must occur, not a mandatory reporting structure.

The technical terms are drawn from the API Pathology Informatics Introductory Course sessions on computer hardware, software, networking, and laboratory information systems. The case and artifacts are original.

## Suggested facilitation

1. Before opening the LIS record, ask what the ED actually knows.
2. During the map, ask learners to name the comparable systems at their institution.
3. In the evidence section, ask what each finding rules out before opening the next check.
4. After repair, ask why checking only the original CBC would be inadequate.
5. Use the hosting discussion to compare local support arrangements without claiming that one model is inherently safer or more reliable.

The debrief may be opened without completing every interaction. This lesson is formative practice, not a scored assessment.

## Common misconceptions

- “Restart the interface before calling.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? Use the approved downtime communication process, obtain read-back when required, and document the call while the delivery failure is investigated.
- “Wait for the next scheduled report.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? Use the approved downtime communication process, obtain read-back when required, and document the call while the delivery failure is investigated.
- “Use server uptime as proof of delivery.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? A successful acknowledgment establishes receipt at one handoff; it does not prove the clinician can view the complete report.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: separate a verified result from successful report delivery.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: trace the same result through the lis, message queue and chart, because a successful acknowledgment establishes receipt at one handoff; it does not prove the clinician can view the complete report.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes an LIS analyst and a bench technologist and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Draw a result path with five timestamps and identify how urgent results are communicated during an interruption. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.

## PIER coverage

- 1.4: Build the reporting path and identify the connection that prevents the ED receiving a final result.
