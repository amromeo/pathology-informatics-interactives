## Debrief

**A result can be final in the LIS and still be unavailable to the clinical team.** The investigation must follow the result through the entire reporting path.

In this case, the analyzer completed the CBC and the LIS released it. The result then waited in the interface queue because a hospital firewall rule blocked the connection to the vendor-hosted EHR. This was a failure in the result-reporting pathway, not a problem with the analytical performance of the analyzer.

## Who was involved

The **LIS team** usually begins by determining whether the result left the LIS and where transmission stopped. **Hospital IT** manages the network and firewall. The **EHR vendor or EHR team** helps confirm whether the receiving system accepted the message.

The laboratory remains responsible for making sure its reports reach the correct patient charts. After the connection was restored, the laboratory did not check only this CBC. Staff also checked the queued reports, a new CBC, a report from another laboratory section using the same connection, and the record of results communicated during downtime.

## Local and hosted systems

Local and vendor-hosted systems fail in different places. The laboratory should know who runs each part, how failures are detected, and how results will be reported during an outage.

You cannot tell whether a system is more reliable just because it is local or vendor-hosted. You have to look at how it is connected, monitored, backed up, and supported.
