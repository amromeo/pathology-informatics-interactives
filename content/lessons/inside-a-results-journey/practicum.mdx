# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet an LIS analyst and a bench technologist.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Trace the same result through the LIS, message queue and chart.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Draw a result path with five timestamps and identify how urgent results are communicated during an interruption.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: draw a result path with five timestamps and identify how urgent results are communicated during an interruption. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “use server uptime as proof of delivery” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Optional local practicum

Review a recent interface outage or delayed-result incident with the LIS team. Determine:

- how the problem was recognized;
- which systems and support groups were involved;
- how results were communicated during the interruption; and
- how delayed reports were reconciled after service was restored.

Do not include patient identifiers in notes or teaching materials. Follow local policy and complete this activity under appropriate supervision.
