# Trace one result at your institution

## Who to meet

An LIS analyst and a bench technologist. Ask them to pick a result you are allowed to review, or one from a reporting problem that has already been fixed.

## Time

30–60 minutes.

## Steps

Draw the result's path from the analyzer to the chart. At each handoff, write down the system, the record that shows the result arrived or was released, and the person who can look at that record. Use the timestamps, but ask whether the systems' clocks agree before you read anything into a delay.

Have the analyst show you what a waiting message and an acknowledgment look like. Say what each one proves, and what you would still need to check in the chart. Then read the local procedure for getting urgent results to clinicians when electronic reporting is down.

Ask the analyst to show you the destination the sender is set to use and the settings of the receiving service. Find the IP address and port in each. Ask how the team checks that the receiving service is listening, and how a settings change is tested before it goes into use.

## Bring back

A one-page diagram, and a short note on how staff would check the delayed reports once the connection was fixed. Include one question you could not answer and who could answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

Which record would show that a result reached the chart, and which would only show that an earlier system had handled it?

## Privacy

Keep patient identifiers and screenshots in the hospital's own systems.
