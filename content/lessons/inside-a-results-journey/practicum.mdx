# Trace one result at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet an LIS analyst and a bench technologist. Ask them to select a result you are authorized to review or a suitable record from a resolved reporting incident.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes.

## Steps

Draw its path from the analyzer to the clinical chart. At each handoff, write down the system, the record that shows arrival or release, and the person who can inspect that record. Use the available timestamps; ask about differences in clock settings before interpreting a delay.

Have the analyst show you what a waiting message and an acknowledgment look like. Explain what each establishes and what still requires checking in the chart. Then review the local procedure for communicating urgent results while electronic reporting is unavailable.

Ask the analyst to show you the sender's configured destination and the receiving service's settings. Identify the IP address and port in each. Have the analyst explain how the team checks that the receiving service is listening and how a settings change is tested before routine use.

## Bring back

Bring back a one-page diagram and a short explanation of how staff would account for delayed reports after restoration. Include one unanswered question and the person who can help resolve it.

## Discuss with faculty

Which record would show that a result reached the chart, and which record would only show that an earlier system handled it?

## Privacy

Keep patient identifiers and screenshots in the institution's approved systems.
