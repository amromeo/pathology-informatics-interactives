# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet a terminology steward and section director.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Compare specimen, units, method and timing.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Prepare a terminology review for one assay with its specimen, method, units and intended use.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: prepare a terminology review for one assay with its specimen, method, units and intended use. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “choose the most common code from a search” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum

With the LIS analyst or terminology specialist, choose one numeric result from an approved local catalog. Record its local identifier, specimen, property, scale, time, method, and unit. Find a candidate on the LOINC website and compare all six dimensions. Record the term status, release, lookup date, code, and rationale; identify the source documentation for the local definition.

Have an assay owner review the proposal. Include one plausible alternative and explain why it does not fit. If the definition is incomplete, specify what must be obtained before approval. Describe how you would validate the receiving display and investigate previously transmitted results if the current mapping were wrong. Do not change a production mapping as part of this exercise.
