# Map one local test at your institution

## Who to meet

The LIS analyst or terminology specialist, and the director of the section that runs the test.

## Time

30–60 minutes.

## Steps

1. With your supervisor, choose one numeric result from the approved local test list.
2. Write down its local code, specimen, property, scale, time, method and unit, and where that definition comes from.
3. Find a candidate code on the LOINC website and compare all six parts. Record the code's status, the LOINC release, the date you looked it up, the code and your reasons.
4. Note one other code that looks plausible and explain why it does not fit. If the local definition is incomplete, list what has to be found out before approval.
5. Ask the section director to review your proposal, and ask how the mapping would be tested in the receiving system and who would approve it.

## Bring back

A one-page comparison of the local test and the LOINC code, part by part, with your reasons and the plausible alternative. Include one open question and the person who can answer it. Do not change a live mapping as part of this exercise.

## Discuss with faculty

What would make you change your choice of code? Why might picking the most common code from a search mislead the team? If the current mapping turned out to be wrong, how would you find the results already sent?

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor allows you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots in the hospital's own systems, and bring back a description with no patient identifiers.
