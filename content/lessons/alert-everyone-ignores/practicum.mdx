# Plan how to evaluate one alert at your institution

## Who to meet

A quality lead and a clinical informatics analyst.

## Time

30–60 minutes.

## Steps

1. With your supervisor, choose one existing laboratory-related alert or suggestion. Get its specification and a summary report you are allowed to see.
2. For each row of the report, work out what it counts: a time the message was shown, a click, an order decision, a patient or an encounter.
3. Describe how a message could be linked to what the clinician actually did next.
4. Plan a review of a sample of both accepted and overridden suggestions. Looking only at acceptances misses good overrides and harmful agreement.
5. Before calculating any change, decide which decisions count, which time periods you will compare, whether there is a comparison group, and what the denominators are. Include whether decisions were clinically appropriate, the extra work for staff and any needed tests that were delayed. Note anything that differs between the periods or groups and could explain a difference.

## Bring back

A one-page plan naming the clinical reviewer, the person who maintains the alert, how often it will be reviewed, and when it should be paused, changed or switched off. Include one open question and the person who can answer it. Show it to faculty before asking for any local change.

## Discuss with faculty

What result would change your view of the alert? Why would counting dismissed alerts as prevented repeats mislead the team?

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor allows you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots in the hospital's own systems, and bring back a description with no patient identifiers.
