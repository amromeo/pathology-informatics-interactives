# Evaluating a Repeat-Test Alert

You are reviewing hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) ordering guidance with the laboratory director and the clinical decision support (CDS) committee. The August report lists 12,000 message presentations and 400 clicks on “Accept.” Staff describe frequent interruptions. The committee asks whether to redesign the guidance and how to decide if the redesign helps.

The message appears for every HbA1c order and can reappear when the order panel is reopened. Collection staff also receive it. “Accept” closes the message; cancelling a test is a separate action. The report therefore does not tell you how often ordering changed or whether a change was appropriate.

First, compare the report with the linked audit. Then retarget the guidance and replay ten events. Finally, examine a four-week ward comparison and a delayed-test review before recommending what should happen next.

The review criteria are the same throughout this case: show a usable final report collected within 90 days or a pending order, and preserve the option to proceed for a clinical reason. This is the institution's review specification, not a universal testing interval.


## What you will be able to do

- Compare pilot changes with concurrent changes elsewhere.
- Define eligible decisions and balancing measures.
- Explain why “count dismissed alerts as prevented repeats” is an incomplete approach.

Start by reading the case records. Make a choice before checking the explanation; you can revise it and test again.
