# Evaluating a Repeat-Test Alert

You are reviewing hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) ordering guidance with the laboratory director and the clinical decision support (CDS) committee. The August report shows the message was displayed 12,000 times, and “Accept” was clicked 400 times. Staff say they are interrupted constantly. The committee asks whether to redesign the guidance, and how to tell whether a redesign helps.

The message appears for every HbA1c order, and can come back each time the order panel is reopened. Collection staff get it too. Clicking “Accept” just closes the message; cancelling a test is a separate step. So the report cannot tell you how often ordering changed, or whether a change was the right one.

First, compare the report with the linked order audit. Then change who gets the guidance and when, and replay ten events. Finally, look at a four-week comparison between two wards and a review of a delayed test before recommending what to do next.

The criteria stay the same throughout this case: show a usable final report collected within 90 days, or a pending order, and always let the clinician go ahead for a clinical reason. These are this hospital's review criteria, not a general rule for how often to test.


## What you will be able to do

- Compare a change on a trial ward with what happened elsewhere over the same period.
- Decide which decisions count, and what to measure to catch side effects.
- Explain why counting dismissed alerts as prevented repeats gives the wrong answer.
