### Compare the pilot with a ward that did not change

The committee piloted revised guidance on one ward at the beginning of week 3. Weeks 1–2 are the baseline period; weeks 3–4 are the pilot period. Another ward kept the existing guidance. Each ward had 1,000 eligible HbA1c order decisions per week, where “eligible” means all decisions in the evaluation population, whether or not a message was shown.

Reviewers used the same clinical criteria to classify avoidable repeat orders in both wards and periods. The table and trend show those counts per 1,000 decisions. The evaluation includes retained and cancelled decisions; it does not select only users who clicked a message. These observed ward data remain fixed when you change the replay configuration.

The pilot ward falls from 80 to 40 avoidable repeats per 1,000. The comparison ward falls from 60 to 50. The difference between their changes is −30 per 1,000: (40 − 80) − (50 − 60). This comparison accounts for the observed change in the other ward, but the wards were not randomized. Different patients, staffing, concurrent initiatives, review practices, or chance could still explain part of the difference. Four weeks are insufficient to establish a sustained effect.

### Look for harm from following the advice

Read safety record SR-14 before making a recommendation. A clinician cancelled an indicated test after accepting the suggestion. A structured field described the earlier report as usable, while a report comment contained a limitation relevant to the current decision.

**Automation bias** means relying too readily on a system's suggestion. Here, the review must examine both the missing information in the display and how the clinician interpreted the advice. Telling users to “pay more attention” leaves the data and display defect unresolved.

A lower test-use rate measures utilization, not patient health. Include a process measure, a clinically reviewed utilization outcome, a burden measure, and a measure of delayed or missed indicated testing. The single known delay requires investigation; its patient consequence is still under review.
