# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet the privacy office or research data steward.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Ask which disclosure method was approved.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Compare an approved data request with the permitted fields and document how reidentification keys are controlled.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: compare an approved data request with the permitted fields and document how reidentification keys are controlled. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “delete names and send the remaining file” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum

With a supervisor, obtain your institution's policy for classifying and releasing clinical data for research, quality improvement, or education.

1. Classify the dataset described in this lesson under the local policy.
2. Identify which retained fields—such as exact dates, five-digit ZIP codes, rare diagnoses, or a linkage code—change the classification or required safeguards.
3. Determine who decides whether the requested fields are necessary for the stated question.
4. Identify who may approve a release and which agreement or documented review is required for the intended recipient.
5. Record where a linkage key would be stored and which role would be permitted to use it.

Use only the lesson's example. Do not copy local patient data, linkage keys, or restricted policy details into teaching notes.
