# Faculty guide: Not Anonymous Enough

## At a glance

Allow 25 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: distinguish removing names from satisfying a disclosure method; ask which disclosure method was approved; justify the first local check. PIER: 3.2, 3.4.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Activity | Ask the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Read the case and records | What decision has the laboratory been asked to make? |
| 5–18 | Make, test and revise a choice | Which record supports your decision? What could fail? |
| 18–25 | Debrief and retrieval questions | How would you verify this in your own laboratory? |

## Expected answers


Ask learners to explain how the combination of fields—not just one field—changes reidentification risk. Removing the linkage key is a real safeguard, but it does not make detailed dates, geography, and a rare cohort suitable for public release.

Start with Breast carcinoma: three records remain. Adding ZIP 02108 isolates NR-001. Generalizing ZIP and diagnosis together raises the smallest group to four. Because every record shares the outage admission date, generalizing the date does not change k in this table. Safe Harbor still requires removing date elements other than year, and the cohort definition itself reveals the date to anyone who knows the study. Sequestering the key alone leaves that group size unchanged. Ask what research detail each transformation removes and whether the approved research question still can be answered.

Run the public-release shortcut and inspect its partial credit: separating the key passes one check but leaves four failures. For the controlled release path, the exercise must also document purpose, recipient, and agreement. A passing result means the proposal is ready for documented privacy review; it does not authorize release. Reset and repeat with one safeguard missing to show why selecting the preferred narrative answer alone is insufficient.

Make the regulatory distinction explicit: the HIPAA Privacy Rule recognizes Safe Harbor and Expert Determination as deidentification methods. A limited data set remains protected health information and requires an appropriate purpose, recipient, and data use agreement. Local policies and legal review remain authoritative; this exercise is educational and not legal advice.

## Common misconceptions

- “It meets Safe Harbor without review.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? Safe Harbor removes all date elements except year and all five-digit ZIP codes. Limited three-digit ZIP retention has a population condition; other disclosure methods require their own review.
- “A study number alone makes it anonymous.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? Safe Harbor removes all date elements except year and all five-digit ZIP codes. Limited three-digit ZIP retention has a population condition; other disclosure methods require their own review.
- “Delete names and send the remaining file.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? The fields and safeguards depend on the authorized method; a data use agreement is not a substitute for satisfying that method.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: distinguish removing names from satisfying a disclosure method.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: ask which disclosure method was approved, because the fields and safeguards depend on the authorized method; a data use agreement is not a substitute for satisfying that method.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes the privacy office or research data steward and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Compare an approved data request with the permitted fields and document how reidentification keys are controlled. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.
