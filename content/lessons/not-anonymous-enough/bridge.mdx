### Count groups before and after changing fields

The transformation controls operate on a copy of all 18 rows, including rows hidden by your filters. The smallest-group calculation groups rows with identical **birth year, sex, geography, admission period and diagnosis**, counts each group and reports the smallest count. Record labels and linkage codes are excluded from that calculation, so a larger count cannot tell you whether the key is safe.

For example, after broadening ZIP and diagnosis, NR-001 through NR-004 all have birth year 1988, sex F, geography 021**, admission date 2026-04-18 and diagnosis Malignant neoplasm. That group has four rows. The other groups contain four, five and five rows, so the minimum is **4**. Open the [transformed preview](#transformed-privacy-preview) to compare the actual rows.

Changing the date to year leaves the count unchanged because all 18 admission dates were already the same. Separating the key also leaves it unchanged because key access is outside the five-field calculation. These actions can matter even when the counter does not move.

The three controls remove different details: exact timing within the year, location within a ZIP prefix, and breast-specific diagnosis. Priya and the investigator must decide which detail the research needs. The exercise's complete three-change proposal is one conservative proposal for review; it is not a universal legal requirement for every research file.

### What would permit a release?

Under **Safe Harbor**, specified identifiers must be removed and the holder must have no actual knowledge that the remaining information can identify a person. Dates directly related to a person generally retain only the year; ages over 89 need additional handling. Full ZIP codes cannot remain. Three-digit prefixes require a combined area population greater than 20,000 using current Census information, with 000 used when the condition is not met. This workbench abbreviates ZIP values but does not perform that population check. A retained reidentification code also has conditions on its derivation and use; its key cannot be disclosed.

**Expert Determination** requires a qualified expert to assess and document a very small identification risk for the anticipated recipient and available information. The group counter alone performs neither method. Everyone who knows this file was selected from April 18 admissions already knows its date, even if the visible column says only 2026. That context must be part of Priya's review. [HHS deidentification guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html) explains the two methods and their conditions.

A **limited data set** can retain dates and some geography, including ZIP codes, after specified direct identifiers are excluded. It remains PHI and can be used for research, public health or health care operations with a data use agreement. This may preserve detail needed for the study while limiting recipients and uses. A general promise of confidentiality does not by itself complete that pathway. The [HHS research guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/research/index.html) explains limited data sets and other research disclosure pathways.

For this proposed controlled research release, document the purpose, recipient and required agreement, and restrict the key. Selecting these controls records a proposed plan in the exercise.
