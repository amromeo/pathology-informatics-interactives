## Debrief: explain what the remaining fields reveal

### What happened in this case

Jordan removed names and MRNs but left a lookup key in the shared folder. That key can link an exported code back to the patient. In the [original export](#raw-privacy-table), someone who knows a patient's diagnosis and ZIP can also pick out NR-001 from the other 17 rows.

So the original file is coded and can be reidentified. Narrowing it to one row shows that someone with outside knowledge could make a link; it does not show that a real person has been identified. Breast carcinoma appears in three rows of this file. That says nothing about how common it is among the hospital's patients.

After broadening ZIP and diagnosis, the groups have four, four, five and five rows. The [transformed preview](#transformed-privacy-preview) shows why the smallest is four. Changing the shared admission date to the year alone leaves that count the same, and so does locking down the key. Each step deals with a different part of the problem.

The full proposal passes the five checks in the exercise only once the field changes, the locked-down key, the purpose, the recipient and the agreement are all recorded. Priya still has to review the whole file, the fact that everyone in it was admitted on April 18, the ZIP population rules and the study codes. If the study needs exact dates or locations, she can consider sharing a limited data set, which keeps more of that detail. A bigger group count cannot choose the method or approve the file on its own.

Jordan prepares the approved fields and locks down the key. The investigator explains which details are needed to study test completion and phoned results. Priya and the research office decide which review and agreement are needed, with whoever is responsible for the data. The patient fields shown here still need the outcome fields added before the study question can be answered.

A researcher may need dates to follow a patient's course, and a diagnosis to pick the right cases. Combined with location, those same fields can single out a small group. Look at the proposed file as a whole, including who can use any key that links it back to patients.

Images bring more places to check. A whole-slide image file can include a picture of the slide label, information stored inside the file, and a file name based on the accession number. Renaming the file still leaves the other places to check. Staff need a way to check the prepared files and hold back any that still need work.

The privacy and research teams can help keep information useful while they review the release. The small-group counter in this exercise answers one question about this table; approval also depends on how the data may be used and how they will be shared.
