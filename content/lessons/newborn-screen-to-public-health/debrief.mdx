## Debrief: match standards to the question they answer

### What happened in this case

The original test code and free-text answer could be stored, but the program's routing rules could not use them. Coding the overall result correctly, with coded answers, gets NB-01, NB-02 and NB-03 to the right place.

The other five messages each test something different: the infant's identity, one record for a repeat send, linking a corrected report to the original, keeping unknown answers in view, and reviewing preliminary results. The original settings match five of the eight actions; the full fix matches all eight. Removing the identity or version checks causes failures even when the codes are right.

The laboratory and the program agree on what each result means and what follow-up it needs. Interface staff build and watch the connection. Standards help with that agreement; they do not replace it, and they do not show that the program's follow-up process works.

You chose the test code and its allowed answers from an agreement you were given. The next lesson asks why laboratories need shared test codes at all, and has you check proposed LOINC codes against a local test list instead of accepting a code just because it exists.

<summary>A closer look: ask what happens after the message arrives</summary>

A program's system can store a message without putting it on the worklist staff use for follow-up. Laboratory and public health staff need to agree on what result is being reported, which answers are allowed and what happens for each answer.

Follow a corrected newborn screening report through that agreement. The program has to recognize which earlier result it replaces and whether the follow-up has to change. A repeat send of the same report also has to stay distinguishable from a new result.

Shared message formats and codes help both teams describe what they need. The acceptance test still has to show what the program actually does with the report, including an unfamiliar answer and an infant who cannot be matched.

**At the joint review:** After each test message, which worklist or follow-up record would you ask the program to show you?

### Who does what

The laboratory's interface analyst and the program's interface lead build and test the connection and record the results. Laboratory and program staff agree on what each result means and confirm that reports reach the right follow-up worklist.
