# Who Needs to Be at the Table?

> **Synthetic case:** No patient data or proprietary system display is used.

## Monday is go-live

The chemistry laboratory is scheduled to start a new high-sensitivity troponin assay on Monday. The analyzer is connected, and test messages reach the EHR. At the final huddle, however, several people are using the words *IT*, *pathology informatics*, *clinical informatics*, and *laboratory administration* as if they mean the same thing.

You are the pathology resident at the huddle. Your job is to decide who leads each part of the work, where the pathologist must be involved, and whether the laboratory can approve go-live.

## The terms

- **Enterprise information technology (IT)** runs shared infrastructure such as servers, networks, enterprise accounts, security services, and communications.
- **The laboratory IT team**, often called the **LIS team**, supports the laboratory applications. Its analysts build tests and rules in the LIS, configure interfaces, manage access, test changes, and troubleshoot the systems with laboratory staff.
- **Pathology informatics** applies laboratory knowledge to the design and use of information systems and data. It helps set laboratory-wide practices for orders and results, reviews how the LIS and EHR are built and tested, uses laboratory data to improve practice, and works on laboratory-related CDS.
- **Clinical informatics** works across patient care and the EHR. It may consult on difficult ordering or result-display problems and often has a central role in clinical decision support (CDS) and other uses of laboratory data in the EHR.
- **Laboratory administration** manages staffing, procedures, training, resources, accreditation, and day-to-day operations.
- **The pathologist or laboratory director** is responsible for the laboratory service. The laboratory owns the laboratory report, including its content and how it is validated in the EHR.

These groups work together, and one person may wear more than one hat. The names still matter because the responsibilities are different.

## What you need to do

1. Assign the responsible role or group to each part of the go-live work.
2. Triage problems involving the laboratory report, downstream EHR displays, and CDS.
3. Review the open items and decide whether the laboratory can approve go-live.

## Key point

- Enterprise IT runs shared technology. The LIS team builds and supports the laboratory applications. Informatics decides how the technology and data should support laboratory and clinical work.
- Pathology informatics focuses on laboratory orders, specimens, testing, results, and laboratory workflow. Clinical informatics works across the patient's care and focuses on encounters rather than specimens.
- Pathology informatics and clinical informatics can both work on CDS. Clinical informatics can also help with difficult EHR problems, but it does not own the laboratory report. The laboratory does.
