# Who Does What in Laboratory Informatics?

You are the chemistry pathology resident at Friday's meeting about a new high-sensitivity troponin assay. The laboratory plans to start patient testing on Monday.

> **Case:** Test results from the new analyzer already pass through the laboratory information system (LIS) into the electronic health record (EHR). What is left is checking the report and getting the chemistry section ready to run the test.

The laboratory director asks you to go through the remaining work. Who will make each change? Who will check it? Would you start on Monday?

## The people at the meeting

- **Hospital information technology (IT)** staff keep the network and the hospital's shared systems running.
- **The LIS team** adds the test to the LIS and checks the connections that carry orders and results between systems. These connections are called interfaces.
- **Pathology informatics** helps chemistry decide how the test is ordered and reported, so that it follows the same naming and reporting conventions as the laboratory's other tests.
- **Clinical informatics** looks after how clinicians use the EHR, including the alerts and other tools that draw on laboratory results.
- **Laboratory operations** staff get the section ready to run the assay: written procedures, training, supplies and enough trained staff on every shift.
- **The pathologist or laboratory director** approves how the test is ordered and what the complete report says, including the version clinicians see in the EHR.

Your hospital may use different job titles. The LIS team may sit in pathology or in hospital IT, and one person may do several of these jobs.

## What needs checking in the chart?

A report is more than the number. It includes the test name, units and reference interval, and any comments, flags or corrections, and all of these have to reach the clinician. The laboratory checks the complete report in one agreed place in the EHR, because other views, such as flowsheets and summaries, may show only part of it.

The EHR also has an alert that combines the troponin result with the patient's diagnoses and medications. This is **clinical decision support (CDS)**: software that uses patient information to help someone make a care decision. Pathology and clinical informatics need to review the alert together with chemistry and the clinicians who will receive it.

## What you will be able to do

- Match the test build, report review and bench preparation to the people who do that work.
- Explain why the laboratory has to check the complete report in the EHR.
- Decide who should lead when a report, trend graph or alert is wrong.
- Use the meeting notes to decide whether Monday's start should wait.
