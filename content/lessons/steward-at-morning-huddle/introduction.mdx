# Who Does What in Laboratory Informatics?

> **Synthetic case:** No patient data or proprietary system display is used.

## Is the laboratory ready?

A new high-sensitivity troponin assay is due to go live Monday. The analyzer is talking to the LIS and the result reaches the EHR. Now the team has to decide whether the test is actually ready for patient use.

## IT, the LIS team, and informatics

- **Hospital IT** supports the hospital-wide systems and keeps the network and shared systems available.
- **The LIS team** is the laboratory-facing technical group that builds tests, maintains interfaces, and helps troubleshoot laboratory systems.
- **Pathology informatics** helps make laboratory systems work the way the laboratory needs them to—from ordering and specimen handling through testing, reporting, and use of the report.
- **Clinical informatics** focuses on how the EHR and other clinical information systems are configured and used to support patient care.
- **Laboratory operations** makes sure the procedures, staffing, training, supplies, and support plan are ready before patient testing begins.
- **The pathologist or laboratory director** is responsible for the laboratory service and approves the order and complete result report, including how the report appears in the EHR.

Institutions organize their LIS teams differently. Some are part of pathology, while others are part of hospital IT; the latter arrangement is becoming more common.

How these groups are organized varies by institution, and one person may serve more than one role.

## Your role at the go-live meeting

Work through the final go-live checklist. Decide who does each piece of work, who must approve it, and what still needs to be fixed.

## Before you start

The laboratory's product is the complete result report, not just the result value. The laboratory is responsible for the report and for how the report appears in the EHR. Because the EHR may reuse parts of the report in other places, the laboratory identifies a specific report location where the complete report is verified to be correct.

Responsibility for CDS is not cleanly divided between pathology informatics and clinical informatics. Laboratory-related CDS may be led by pathology informatics, but its design and review often involve clinical informatics, the laboratory, and the clinical services affected by it.
