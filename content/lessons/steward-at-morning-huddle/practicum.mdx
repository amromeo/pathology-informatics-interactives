# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet a laboratory director and LIS analyst.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Compare the LIS report with its verified chart view.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Trace one report through specimen, result and patient records, naming each responsible team.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: trace one report through specimen, result and patient records, naming each responsible team. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “change the display before reviewing the source” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum

With a faculty mentor, select one current or recent laboratory test build.

1. Identify who handled the analyzer/LIS/EHR connection, the laboratory workflow, report content, training, and final approval.
2. View the report in the LIS and in the EHR. Confirm who reviewed the EHR display and what evidence was retained.
3. Identify one CDS rule, dashboard, or other EHR function that uses the result. Record the role of clinical informatics and the role of pathology.
4. Describe one issue that needs the specimen-centered view and one that needs the patient-centered view.
5. Note any local job titles that differ from the role names used in this lesson.
