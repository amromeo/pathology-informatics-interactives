# Faculty guide: Who Does What in Laboratory Informatics?

## At a glance

Allow 22 minutes. Ask residents for their initial Monday recommendation before they assign the work. Keep the meeting notes visible so they can return to the missing comment, alert review and unfinished bench preparation.

This lesson covers PIER 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 and 8.1. Residents should be able to name who builds the test, who checks the report, and why sending a result is insufficient preparation for patient testing.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Activity | Ask the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Read Friday's meeting notes and recommend whether to start Monday | What would the clinician see if we started now? |
| 4–11 | Assign the six jobs | Who can make this change? Who needs to check the finished report? |
| 11–17 | Work through the three calls | What do we need to trace before choosing who should fix it? |
| 17–22 | Revisit Monday and discuss the checks | Which unfinished job changed your recommendation? |

## Expected answers

The six assignments are:

1. **Network and shared systems: hospital IT.** The LIS depends on those services even when its staff belong to pathology.
2. **LIS and interface build: LIS team.** Analysts add the test and check the messages that carry orders and results.
3. **Order and complete result report: pathologist or laboratory director.** The laboratory checks and approves the report in the EHR as well as the LIS.
4. **Naming and reporting across the laboratory: pathology informatics.** This group helps chemistry fit the new assay into the laboratory's usual practices.
5. **Troponin alert: pathology and clinical informatics.** Review the laboratory result and the diagnoses and medications used by the alert with the affected clinicians.
6. **Procedures and shift preparation: laboratory operations.** The section needs staff who can run the test, use the procedure and obtain help.

For the calls, the laboratory director leads the incorrect-report review. Pathology informatics leads the comparison of assays on the trend graph. Clinical informatics coordinates review of the repeated alert. For that alert, the LIS team must first check whether the correction replaced the original observation. Sending it as a new result calls for a repair on the laboratory side.

**Hold Monday's start.** The interpretive comment is cut off, pathology has not reviewed the alert, and procedures, training and staffing are unfinished. Residents should identify the specific jobs still needed, rather than simply saying the laboratory is not ready.

## Common misconceptions

- **“IT and informatics are the same job.”** Ask: who keeps the network running, and who decides what the troponin report should say?
- **“The problem is in the EHR, so the laboratory can hand it over.”** Ask: who will check the complete report after the EHR analyst changes it?
- **“Clinical informatics should handle every alert.”** Ask: who can judge whether the troponin result is being used correctly? Who understands the other chart data in this alert?
- **“The interface passed, so Monday is fine.”** Ask: can the clinician read the whole interpretive comment now?

## Discussion prompts

1. Why should chemistry review a trend graph outside the LIS? *A strong answer:* combining different assays can make a method change look like a change in the patient. Chemistry must judge comparability, and the EHR analyst may need to change the graph.
2. A correction triggers another alert. What would you ask the analyst to show you? *A strong answer:* the original result, correction message and resulting chart entry. Check whether the correction updated the same observation before changing the alert.
3. What would you need at a second meeting to recommend starting? *A strong answer:* the full comment displayed correctly, the alert reviewed by the relevant teams, and completed procedures, training and shift coverage. The laboratory director gives final approval.

## Group variant

Give residents the jobs of chemistry pathologist, LIS analyst, bench supervisor and clinician receiving the alert. Each explains what they need to see before Monday. Ask who else needs to join the discussion.

## Assess transfer

Have the resident draw one local test's route from the analyzer to the chart. Look for the complete report location and the people who change and check it. Local job titles may differ from the lesson's labels.
