# Faculty guide: Who Needs to Be at the Table?

## PIER R5 coverage

- **1.1:** Learners distinguish IT, pathology informatics, and laboratory administration by assigning actual go-live work.
- **1.2:** Learners identify the laboratory and EHR data needed to manage a corrected result and decide whether the available go-live evidence is enough for clinical use.
- **1.3:** Learners compare the specimen-centered laboratory view with the patient-centered EHR view and distinguish pathology informatics from clinical informatics.

## Expected reasoning

The expected assignments are:

1. **Interface connection and access controls — IT/application team.** The laboratory then validates accurate result transmission.
2. **Laboratory report content and required EHR display — pathologist/laboratory.** Analysts perform the build; the laboratory owns and approves the report.
3. **End-to-end laboratory workflow and test plan — pathology informatics.** This role connects laboratory work with the technical build under laboratory medical oversight.
4. **Patient-facing EHR alert using several kinds of clinical data — clinical informatics working with the clinical service.** Pathology must review the laboratory data and its intended use. Clinical informatics does not own the laboratory report.
5. **SOPs, staffing, training, and go-live coverage — laboratory administration.**

Learners should hold the go-live. Transmission passed, but the laboratory report is not displayed correctly, the proposed CDS use has not had pathology review, and operations are not ready.

## Watch for

- Describing IT and informatics as synonyms.
- Treating pathology informatics and clinical informatics as competing owners rather than related fields with different scopes.
- Giving ownership of the laboratory report to IT, the EHR team, or clinical informatics.
- Excluding clinical informatics from CDS and broader EHR uses of laboratory data.
- Assuming that a successful interface test is enough to approve go-live.

Ask learners who performs each task at their institution and who gives the final clinical approval. Job titles vary. Keep the discussion focused on responsibilities, not titles.

The cited CAP member resource identifies **GEN.48500, Interface Result Integrity**, as the applicable checklist requirement and describes accurate transmission of laboratory results into the EHR as a laboratory responsibility. This lesson summarizes that principle and does not reproduce checklist wording.
