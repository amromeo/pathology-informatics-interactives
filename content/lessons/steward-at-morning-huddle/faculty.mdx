# Faculty guide: Who Does What in Laboratory Informatics?

## PIER R5 coverage

- **1.1:** Learners distinguish hospital IT, the LIS team, pathology informatics, clinical informatics, and laboratory operations by assigning work for a new assay.
- **1.2:** Learners identify where laboratory data are created, reported, and reused in the EHR. They also decide what the laboratory must review before a result report is released for patient care.
- **1.3:** Learners decide who should lead three common problems involving a result report, an EHR trend display, and an EHR alert.

## Expected reasoning

The expected assignments are:

1. **Network and shared systems — Hospital IT.** Hospital IT keeps the network, servers, accounts, security services, and other shared systems available.
2. **LIS and interface build — LIS team.** The LIS team builds the approved test in the LIS, changes the interface, and performs technical testing before laboratory review. In some hospitals the LIS team is part of IT; in others it sits in the pathology department. The work is the same even when the reporting structure differs.
3. **Order and complete result report — pathologist or laboratory director.** The laboratory approves the order and the complete result report, including how the report appears in the EHR.
4. **Consistent laboratory build practices — pathology informatics.** Pathology informatics helps the section fit the assay into the practices used across the laboratory for naming, reporting, corrections, and EHR display.
5. **EHR alert — pathology and clinical informatics.** CDS work is not cleanly divided between the two fields. Pathology informatics may lead laboratory-related CDS. Clinical informatics, the laboratory, and the affected clinical services are often involved when the rule uses other EHR data or changes clinical work.
6. **Procedures and daily operation — laboratory operations.** Laboratory operations makes sure procedures, staffing, training, supplies, and the support plan are ready before patient testing starts.

The expected support-ticket answers are:

1. **The complete result report is wrong — pathologist or laboratory director.** The laboratory leads because it owns the complete result report and its appearance in the EHR. The LIS team and hospital IT may help make the correction.
2. **Two assays are grouped incorrectly in an EHR trend — pathology informatics.** This is laboratory data presented with the wrong meaning. Pathology informatics should coordinate the review with the section and the EHR team.
3. **An EHR alert fires again after a corrected result — clinical informatics.** This is primarily a broader EHR and CDS workflow problem. Clinical informatics should coordinate with pathology informatics, the laboratory, and the affected clinical service.

Learners should hold the go-live. Transmission passed, but the complete result report is clipped in the EHR, the proposed use in CDS has not had pathology review, and procedures and training are incomplete.

## Watch for

- Describing IT and informatics as synonyms.
- Treating hospital IT and the LIS team as the same function.
- Treating pathology informatics and clinical informatics as competing owners rather than related fields with different scopes.
- Giving ownership of the laboratory report to IT, the EHR team, or clinical informatics.
- Assigning all CDS to clinical informatics and overlooking the role of pathology informatics in laboratory-related CDS.
- Excluding clinical informatics or the clinical service when CDS uses broader EHR data or changes their workflow.
- Assuming that a successful interface test is enough to approve go-live.

How these groups are organized varies by institution, and one person may serve more than one role. Ask learners who performs each task locally and who gives final laboratory approval. The assignments identify who should coordinate the work, not everyone who must participate.

The laboratory produces and owns the complete result report: the report name, result fields, units, reference information, comments, flags, corrections, and required appearance in the EHR. Because the EHR may reuse parts of that report in several places, laboratories usually identify one specific report location where the complete report has been checked and approved. Other EHR views must still be reviewed when they could change the clinical meaning.

The cited CAP member resource identifies **GEN.48500, Interface Result Integrity**, as the applicable checklist requirement and describes accurate transmission of laboratory results into the EHR as a laboratory responsibility. This lesson summarizes that principle and does not reproduce checklist wording.
