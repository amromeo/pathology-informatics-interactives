# Faculty guide: Who Needs to Be at the Table?

## PIER R5 coverage

- **1.1:** Learners distinguish IT, pathology informatics, and laboratory administration by assigning actual go-live work.
- **1.2:** Learners identify the laboratory and EHR data needed to manage a corrected result and decide whether the available go-live evidence is enough for clinical use.
- **1.3:** Learners triage problems involving the laboratory report, downstream EHR use, and CDS to distinguish pathology informatics from clinical informatics and identify shared work.

## Expected reasoning

The expected assignments are:

1. **Shared infrastructure — enterprise IT.** Enterprise IT provides the servers, network, identity and access services, security controls, and secure connections used by laboratory systems.
2. **LIS and interface configuration — laboratory IT/LIS team.** The LIS team enters approved test definitions, configures interfaces and rules, performs technical testing, and supports the application. It does not independently set the report's clinical content.
3. **Laboratory report content and required EHR display — pathologist/laboratory.** Analysts perform the build; the laboratory owns and approves the report.
4. **Laboratory-wide order and result build standards — pathology informatics.** Pathology informatics knows the general practices used across laboratory sections and checks that the proposed names, fields, units, reference information, comments, and correction handling are consistent. The laboratory owns the clinical content, and the technical teams configure the systems.
5. **EHR alert using the result with diagnoses and medications — shared work.** Laboratory-related CDS is within the scope of pathology informatics. Clinical informatics and the clinical service are also involved when the rule uses broader EHR data or changes patient-care workflow. The laboratory remains responsible for how its result is interpreted and used.
6. **SOPs, staffing, training, and go-live coverage — laboratory administration.**

The expected support-ticket answers are:

1. **Incorrect laboratory report — pathology/laboratory.** The laboratory owns the report and determines the correction.
2. **Correct report grouped incorrectly in an EHR trend graph — clinical informatics/EHR team.** The error is in a downstream patient-chart view. Pathology confirms the result identity and intended representation.
3. **Corrected result causes an EHR alert to fire again — both groups.** The laboratory explains the corrected result; pathology informatics, clinical informatics, and the clinical service determine the CDS behavior.

Learners should hold the go-live. Transmission passed, but the laboratory report is not displayed correctly, the proposed CDS use has not had pathology review, and operations are not ready.

## Watch for

- Describing IT and informatics as synonyms.
- Treating enterprise IT and the laboratory IT/LIS team as the same function.
- Treating pathology informatics and clinical informatics as competing owners rather than related fields with different scopes.
- Giving ownership of the laboratory report to IT, the EHR team, or clinical informatics.
- Assigning all CDS to clinical informatics and overlooking the role of pathology informatics in laboratory-related CDS.
- Excluding clinical informatics or the clinical service when CDS uses broader EHR data or changes their workflow.
- Assuming that a successful interface test is enough to approve go-live.

Ask learners who performs each task at their institution and who gives the final clinical approval. Job titles vary. Keep the discussion focused on responsibilities, not titles.

The cited CAP member resource identifies **GEN.48500, Interface Result Integrity**, as the applicable checklist requirement and describes accurate transmission of laboratory results into the EHR as a laboratory responsibility. This lesson summarizes that principle and does not reproduce checklist wording.
