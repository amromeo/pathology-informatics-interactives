## Debrief: what still needs to happen before Monday?

The assay should wait. The result reaches the EHR, but part of the interpretive comment is cut off. Pathology has not reviewed the troponin alert. And the section still has procedures, training and shift coverage to finish.

### Who finishes the work?

The LIS team builds the test and checks the interface. Hospital IT keeps the network and shared systems running. Pathology informatics helps chemistry make sure the new assay is ordered and reported like the laboratory's other tests. The laboratory director approves the order and the complete report.

The bench supervisor and laboratory operations staff get the section ready to run the test on every shift. Pathology and clinical informatics review the alert with the clinicians who will receive it. A result reaching the chart does not tell you that any of this work is done.

### Who leads when something goes wrong in the chart?

The three calls after go-live need different people to lead:

- **The corrected report is still wrong in the EHR.** The laboratory director should lead. The LIS and EHR teams trace the correction and fix the display, and the laboratory then checks the complete corrected report where clinicians read it.
- **Two troponin assays appear on one trend graph.** Pathology informatics should lead, with chemistry and the EHR analyst. Only the laboratory can say whether results from the two assays can be compared, so that has to be settled before they are plotted together.
- **The alert fires again after a correction.** Clinical informatics should coordinate the review of the alert. But first, pathology informatics and the LIS team check whether the correction replaced the original result or arrived as a second one.

That last check decides who fixes what. In a Health Level Seven version 2 (HL7 v2) message, status C marks a corrected result on the same observation. If the laboratory sent a new result instead, the laboratory team fixes that first. If the correction arrived properly, the team can look at why the alert fired again.

### Check the report clinicians will read

The laboratory has to check the complete report in its agreed place in the chart. A correct number in a flowsheet would not have shown that the interpretive comment was cut off. Before the director signs off, ask whoever is reviewing the report to show you the comment on screen.
