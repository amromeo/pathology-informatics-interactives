## Debrief

**Key point:** A working interface does not settle who is responsible for the report or make a new laboratory service ready for use.

IT builds and runs the connection. Pathology informatics helps set laboratory-wide practices for building orders and results and checks that laboratory sections use those practices consistently. Laboratory administration gets the people, procedures, and daily operation ready.

The laboratory owns the laboratory report. That includes the report name, result fields, units, reference information, comments, flags, corrections, and the required display in the EHR. The pathologist or laboratory director must be able to review and approve the report as clinicians will see it.

Pathology informatics and clinical informatics can both work on CDS. Pathology informatics brings the laboratory workflow and the meaning of the result. Clinical informatics and the clinical service bring the broader patient-care and EHR workflow. Clinical informatics can also advise on difficult ordering and display problems, but it does not take ownership of the laboratory report.

The specimen-centered and patient-centered views are connected. The laboratory needs the specimen record to know what was collected, tested, verified, corrected, and reported. The clinical team needs the patient record to use that result with the rest of the patient's care. Difficult problems often require both views.
