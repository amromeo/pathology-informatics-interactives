# Debrief

## Three different questions

Can the message cross the interface? Is the result report correct? Can the laboratory safely offer the test? The interface test answers only the first question.

The result reaches the EHR, but the result report, CDS review, procedures, training, and staffing are not ready. The assay should not be used for patient testing until that work is complete and approved.

## Different groups, different responsibilities

- **Hospital IT** keeps the network and shared systems available so the LIS and instrument connections can operate.
- **The LIS team** builds the new test in the LIS, makes the required interface changes, and completes technical testing before laboratory review.
- **Pathology informatics** works with the laboratory and the technical teams to make sure the systems support the test from ordering through reporting.
- **Clinical informatics** focuses on how the EHR and other clinical information systems are configured and used to support patient care.
- **Laboratory operations** makes sure the procedures, staffing, training, supplies, and support plan are ready before patient testing begins.
- **The pathologist or laboratory director** approves the order and complete result report, including how the report appears in the EHR.

The technical teams configure the systems. The laboratory director decides what should be reported and whether the test is ready for patient use.

Institutions organize their LIS teams differently. Some are part of pathology, while others are part of hospital IT; the latter arrangement is becoming more common. How these groups are organized varies by institution, and one person may serve more than one role.

## The complete result report

The laboratory's product is the complete result report, not just the result value. The laboratory is responsible for the report and for how the report appears in the EHR.

The EHR may show pieces of a result report in flowsheets, summaries, alerts, and other parts of the chart. The laboratory therefore identifies a specific report location where clinicians can find the complete result report exactly as the laboratory approved it.

## Who should lead when the problem appears in the EHR?

Where the problem appears does not necessarily tell you who should lead. The important question is whether the problem concerns the laboratory test and report or the broader EHR workflow.

- **The result report is wrong in the EHR:** The laboratory should lead. It is responsible for the complete result report and must verify that the corrected report appears properly in the EHR.
- **Results from different troponin assays are combined in a trend graph:** Although the error appears in the EHR, pathology informatics should lead because the problem is how two laboratory tests are represented in the patient's chart. Test identity, method changes, and whether results are sufficiently comparable to be trended together are laboratory questions. An EHR analyst may be needed to make the configuration change.
- **An EHR alert fires again after a corrected result:** The alert firing is an EHR and CDS workflow problem, so clinical informatics should lead. Pathology contributes the laboratory information needed to set the rule correctly.

Responsibility for CDS is not cleanly divided between pathology informatics and clinical informatics. Laboratory-related CDS may be led by pathology informatics, but its design and review often involve clinical informatics, the laboratory, and the clinical services affected by it.
