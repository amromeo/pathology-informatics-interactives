### Understand what the score and cost total include

The preference score is the weighted mean of three ratings on a 1–5 scale: add each rating multiplied by its selected weight, then divide by the sum of weights. The controls normalize the weights, so they do not need to sum to 100. With all weights zero, no score is calculated.

The opening weights favor usability, then analytics, then training. Alder scores highest under those weights. Try emphasizing training and compare the ranking. No preference weighting changes the evidence for the three mandatory requirements.

The cost cards show USD thousands over implementation plus five annual service charges. Compare the same included categories for all vendors. The initial total includes only upfront and service charges; selecting the additional-cost control adds interfaces, migration, and exit. An omitted cost is not a free service.

Alder's interface amount is a planning allowance, while the other values are proposal amounts. None is a signed contract. Totals exclude internal staffing, tax, inflation, discounting, and other local costs. Before budgeting, assign responsibility for interfaces and data conversion, obtain binding terms, and test assumptions such as additional interfaces or a longer overlap period.

### Plan the relationship after selection

A vendor recommendation begins contract and implementation work. Specify deliverables, acceptance evidence, responsibilities, support hours, change notification, recovery arrangements, data access, and termination assistance.

A hosted subscription, often called software as a service (SaaS), shifts some infrastructure work to the supplier. It does not remove the laboratory's responsibility to plan downtime, verify reports, manage access, reconcile work, and maintain usable historical records.
