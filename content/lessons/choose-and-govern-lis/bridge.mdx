### What the score and the totals include

The preference score combines the panel's three ratings according to the weights you set. Each rating runs from 1 to 5, so the score does too. The weights do not have to add up to 100, because the score is divided by whatever total you choose. If every weight is zero, there is no score.

The starting weights put usability first, then reports and analytics, then training. With those weights, Alder comes out on top. Move most of the weight to training and watch the order change. No setting of the sliders changes whether a vendor showed the three requirements working.

The cost cards are in thousands of US dollars: the installation price plus five years of annual service fees. At first, the totals leave out interfaces, moving the old data across (often called data migration), and the cost of leaving the vendor at the end of the contract. Tick the box to add them. A cost that is missing from a total still has to be paid.

Compare the same items for every vendor. Alder did not quote for interfaces, so its interface figure is the committee's own estimate; the other figures come from the vendors' proposals. None of them is a signed price. The totals also leave out your own staff time, tax and inflation. Before anyone builds a budget, find out who will build the interfaces and move the old data, and get those prices in writing.

### After you choose

Choosing a vendor starts the contract talks; it does not finish them. The contract should spell out:

- what the vendor will deliver, and how the laboratory will check it before accepting it;
- who does which part of the installation;
- when support is available, and how much notice the laboratory gets before changes;
- what happens during an outage;
- how the laboratory gets its records back if it leaves.

Some vendors run the system on their own servers and charge a yearly fee, often called software as a service (SaaS). The vendor then looks after the servers. The laboratory still plans for downtime, checks its reports, controls who has access, accounts for work done on paper during outages, and keeps its old records usable.
