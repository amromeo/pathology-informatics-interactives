# Faculty guide: Selecting and Maintaining a Laboratory Information System

## At a glance

Allow 40 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: use workflow evidence to compare system proposals; agree on requirements and decision authority; justify the first local check. PIER: 8.3, 8.4.

## Outcomes and facilitation

This lesson addresses PIER 8.3 and 8.4 through selection, implementation, maintenance, and information governance. Allow 40 minutes. Ask learners to recommend a vendor from the initial scorecard, then require them to revise or defend that recommendation using the demonstration records.

Emphasize that the ratings are preferences, not measured patient outcomes. The four-hour recovery and fifteen-minute reconciliation limits are case-specific requirements, not regulatory thresholds.

## Evidence key and calculations

Alder: correction fails, recovery passes, export unverified. Birch: all three pass. Cedar: correction unverified, recovery passes, export fails. A patch promise is not a retest; an unfinished demonstration is not a demonstrated failure.

At weights 50/30/20, scores are Alder 4.8, Birch 3.2, Cedar 3.8. Training-only weights produce 4, 4, and 3. All-zero weights produce no score. Neither ranking establishes mandatory eligibility.

Five-year base totals are 180 + 5×80 = 580, 240 + 5×65 = 565, and 160 + 5×70 = 510, in USD thousands. Adding interfaces, migration, and exit produces 695, 695, and 720. Ask which numbers are allowances versus proposals and what costs remain outside scope.

The exercise prevents a learner from approving a vendor merely by misclassifying its evidence. Strong reasoning still requires the learner to identify why the failed or missing evidence matters.

## Recommendation and transfer

Birch may proceed to contract and local acceptance review when the evidence is classified accurately, the scoped costs included, and all lifecycle actions addressed. Deferral can also be justified; ask for specific outstanding questions, an owner, and a review date. Neither response is procurement authorization.

The expected lifecycle actions are local testing and training, documented change assessment with testing and approval, downtime operation plus reconciliation, and verified historical retrieval before retirement.

Ask learners to add one institution-specific requirement and a demonstration script that could falsify a vendor claim. Have them identify the approver, technical maintainer, and evidence retained for a future inspection or internal review.

## Common misconceptions

- “Average the failure into the total score.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? A high aggregate preference score cannot compensate for an unmet mandatory safety requirement. Require evidence and an acceptable plan before selection.
- “Treat every demonstration criterion as optional.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? A high aggregate preference score cannot compensate for an unmet mandatory safety requirement. Require evidence and an acceptable plan before selection.
- “Choose the vendor with the most features.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? The team needs explicit must-haves, weighted preferences and responsibility for approving tradeoffs before viewing demonstrations.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: use workflow evidence to compare system proposals.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: agree on requirements and decision authority, because the team needs explicit must-haves, weighted preferences and responsibility for approving tradeoffs before viewing demonstrations.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes a laboratory director and procurement or project lead and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Write a requirements-and-evidence table plus contract questions about support, changes and data export. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.
