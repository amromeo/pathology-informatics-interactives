# Selecting and Maintaining a Laboratory Information System

You are the pathology resident on the committee choosing a new laboratory information system (LIS). The contract for the current system runs out next year. Over the past month, laboratory staff, information technology (IT), purchasing and the clinical services watched demonstrations from three vendors: Alder, Birch and Cedar.

The panel rated Alder highest. Before the committee recommends it, the laboratory director asks you to look past the ratings at three things. Can each system correct a report properly? Can the laboratory get back to work after an outage? Can the laboratory still pull up its old reports after it leaves the vendor? She also points out that the price comparison leaves out interfaces, moving the old data across, and the cost of leaving the vendor at the end of the contract.

Your job is to separate what the laboratory cannot do without from what the panel simply liked, and to decide what each demonstration actually showed. You will also compare five-year costs on the same basis and decide what has to happen once the system is installed.

Start with the purchasing summary and the requirements the committee agreed on before any vendor came in. These prices and recovery limits apply to this comparison. Your laboratory would set its own limits and request current prices.

## What you will be able to do

- Tell a demonstration that failed from one that was never finished.
- Explain why a high rating from the panel cannot make up for a correction that does not work.
- Compare vendors' costs once every total includes the same items.
- Say what the laboratory must test or keep when the system is installed, upgraded and finally replaced.
