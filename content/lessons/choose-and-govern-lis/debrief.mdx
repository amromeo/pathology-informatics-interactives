## Debrief: a high rating cannot make up for a correction that fails

Alder had the best ratings, but when its corrected report reached the chart it arrived as a second final report. The old report was still on display, and nothing linked the two, so a clinician could act on the wrong result. Alder also said it could export the laboratory's records but never showed an export.

Cedar recovered fastest from the outage, but its export held only the latest text of each report, without the earlier versions or the accession numbers. Its correction demonstration also stopped before the report reached the chart, so that requirement has not been shown either way.

Birch was the only vendor that showed all three requirements working. That makes Birch the one to take forward to contract talks and to testing on the laboratory's own systems. It does not make Birch the best system for every hospital. Waiting would also be reasonable, as long as the committee writes down exactly what it still needs to see, who will get it, and when it will decide.

The costs tell a similar story. On the vendors' opening figures, Cedar looked cheapest: 510 thousand dollars over five years, against 565 for Birch and 580 for Alder. Once interfaces, moving the old data and the cost of leaving were added, Cedar became the most expensive at 720, and Alder and Birch tied at 695. Even these totals leave out staff time and other local costs.

### After the contract is signed

The work continues for as long as the laboratory uses the system.

- **Installation.** Before go-live, test the system with the laboratory's own interfaces, test definitions and staff, on a busy shift and during a downtime drill. Include corrections. The vendor's demonstration covered none of this.
- **Upgrades.** An upgrade the vendor has already tested can still change how your reports look. Find out what it changes, test those parts, and have the change approved and written down before it goes in.
- **Outages.** Getting the system back is only half the job. Afterward, the laboratory has to account for every order and report it handled on paper while the system was down.
- **Retirement.** Before the old server is switched off, make sure the laboratory can still pull up old reports, including corrected ones, and that the right people can still reach them.

Keep written procedures for running the system, its interfaces and reports, user access, outages and changes. Review them with the laboratory's quality staff against the accreditation checklist. An inspector will ask for the laboratory's own procedures and test records, and a signed contract or a good demonstration will not stand in for them.
