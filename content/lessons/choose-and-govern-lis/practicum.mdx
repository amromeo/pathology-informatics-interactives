# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Your laboratory director, and whoever runs LIS projects or purchasing at your hospital.

## Time

About an hour, with a faculty member or supervisor.

## Steps

1. Pick one LIS module or upgrade your laboratory is planning, or one it finished recently.
2. Write three things the laboratory could not do without, each tied to a real task on the bench or at sign-out, and three things that would simply be nice to have.
3. For each must-have, write what you would watch for at a demonstration to know it works.
4. If your supervisor can share a proposal or project plan, go through it and mark each claim: someone saw it working, the vendor only said so, it failed, or nobody knows yet.
5. Ask how the laboratory would get its reports and their earlier versions back at the end of the contract, and who would check them.

## Bring back

A one-page table of your must-haves and nice-to-haves, what you would watch for, and what you found. Add one question nobody could answer yet and the name of the person who should answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

Which of your must-haves did people disagree about, and who settled it? If a vendor failed one of them at a demonstration, what would it take for you to consider that vendor again?

## Privacy

Use only documents your supervisor says you may review. Leave contracts, prices and any patient information in the hospital's own systems, and bring back your table without them.

## If you have more time

Choose one test or panel your laboratory is retiring, such as an immunohistochemistry panel nobody orders anymore. Find out which order sets, reports and interfaces still use it, and who has to approve removing it. Do not change anything in the live system.
