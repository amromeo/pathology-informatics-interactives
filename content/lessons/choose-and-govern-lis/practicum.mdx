# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet a laboratory director and procurement or project lead.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Agree on requirements and decision authority.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Write a requirements-and-evidence table plus contract questions about support, changes and data export.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: write a requirements-and-evidence table plus contract questions about support, changes and data export. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “choose the vendor with the most features” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum: write requirements that can be checked

With faculty, choose an LIS module or planned upgrade. Write three mandatory requirements and three preferences, each connected to a named laboratory workflow. Add a test script and expected result for every mandatory item.

Review a permitted proposal or internal project plan. Separate observed evidence, supplier statements, demonstrated failures, and unanswered questions. Compare the same cost categories over a defined period, identifying allowances and exclusions.

Choose one dictionary or order-set maintenance issue, such as an obsolete immunohistochemistry panel. Propose a controlled review of its users, names, components, procedure, downstream reports, approval, testing, and retirement. Do not change production settings as part of this exercise.

Present a short implementation and lifecycle plan identifying local acceptance tests, staff training, support, change control, downtime/reconciliation, historical access, and responsibilities at termination.
