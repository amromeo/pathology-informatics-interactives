## Read the comparison before judging it

In the [original summary table](#tat-original-summary), each case contributes one turnaround time (TAT). The mean adds those times up and divides by the number of cases. The median lines the times up in order and takes the middle one; with an even number of cases, it averages the two middle values.

Here is why the two can disagree. Take five times: 2, 2, 3, 3 and 10 working days. They add up to 20, so the mean is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 days. The middle value is 3, so the median is 3. If the longest case took 20 days instead, the mean would rise to 6 days, but the median would stay at 3.

Now read the post-go-live row: 245 cases, with a mean of 3.8 days and a median of 2.5. Before go-live, the mean was 3.3 and the median 2.7. The lower median says the typical case got a little faster. The higher mean says some cases took much longer. Neither number tells you when those long cases happened.

When a few cases take much longer than the rest, the times are said to be **right-skewed**. A gap between the mean and the median is a reason to look at the cases; on its own, it does not prove the shape. Look at the weeks one by one before deciding what to tell the director.

The pre-go-live weeks also vary: the last two have higher medians than the first four. A before-and-after comparison describes what happened in each period. It cannot, by itself, show that the new LIS caused a change.
