## Read the comparison before judging it

In the [original summary table](#tat-original-summary), each case contributes one turnaround time (TAT). The mean adds those times and divides by the number of cases. The median puts the times in order and identifies the middle. With an even number of cases, it averages the two middle values.

To see why they can disagree, consider five example times: 2, 2, 3, 3 and 10 working days. Their sum is 20, so the mean is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 days. The middle value is 3 days, so the median is 3. If the longest case takes 20 days instead, the mean becomes 6 days while the median stays 3.

Now read the post-go-live row. It includes 245 cases, with a mean of 3.8 days and a median of 2.5. The pre-go-live mean is 3.3 and median is 2.7. The lower median suggests that the middle of the distribution moved down. The higher mean suggests that longer cases deserve attention. Neither statistic tells you when those cases occurred.

A distribution with a group of long times stretching beyond most cases is called **right-skewed**. The mean/median difference is a reason to inspect the cases, not proof of a particular shape. Look at the weeks separately before deciding what to tell the director.

The baseline also varies: the last two pre-go-live weeks have higher medians than the first four. A before/after comparison describes these periods; by itself, it cannot show that the LIS caused any later difference.
