# Faculty guide: Can We Trust This Report?

Allow 32 minutes. Learners review a surgical pathology spreadsheet after an LIS replacement, trace a missing structured time to its source and revise the comparison without discarding delayed cases. This addresses PIER 2.1 and 1.2.

## Run of show

Spend 6 minutes on the report definitions and mean/median example, 10 minutes on weekly and source records, 8 minutes on the data-quality checks and 8 minutes revising the report and discussing its limits.

## Start with the comparison

Ask learners to name the start event, endpoint and periods before interpreting the summary. TAT runs from accession to the initial final report in working days. Amendments do not create another case row. The export was run on April 20, after all cases accessioned through April 3 had an initial final report.

The [original summary](../../lessons/can-we-trust-this-report/#tat-original-summary) contains 210 pre-go-live cases and 245 post-go-live cases. Read one row aloud. Use the five-case example to show why changing a long time can move the mean without moving the median.

The appropriate first action is to inspect the weeks. The higher post-go-live mean and lower median are a reason to investigate, not a choice between two competing verdicts.

## Follow the case into the spreadsheet

Cutover week contains 35 cases. The first eight were accessioned in the LIS. After the interruption began at 08:30 Tuesday, 27 were recorded on paper. Their original times were not entered into the structured field during later data entry.

Ask the learner to open the requisition and identify the original time. Paper accessioning occurred at receipt, as confirmed by the accessioning supervisor. The coordinator checked that event definition before using the paper time in the spreadsheet.

Use the [case-list table](../../lessons/can-we-trust-this-report/#tat-case-table) to distinguish a missing LIS field from a missing TAT calculation. Both the blank field and the source time remain visible. Replacing the original time with later data entry would make TAT artificially short.

## Explain the seven checks

Accuracy compares a populated value with the source event; completeness asks whether a required value exists. Validity concerns the format and permitted sequence. Consistency concerns a shared definition across periods. Uniqueness prevents duplicate accessions. Timeliness asks whether records are current for the report date. Fitness for purpose asks whether the comparison supports the intended use.

The categories can overlap. For the report-date check, the focus is whether the export is current enough to include late-finalized cases. Do not teach that waiting indefinitely is always the solution: an earlier report could instead identify pending cases explicitly.

## Explain the revised conclusion

Keep 210 pre-go-live, 35 cutover and 210 later cases. The means are 3.3, 8.4 and 3.0 working days; medians are 2.7, 9.5 and 2.5. Removing the 27 cases with blank structured fields would selectively remove known delays whose original times were available.

The report shows a substantial temporary delay during cutover and somewhat shorter overall TAT in the later period. It does not prove that replacing the LIS improved TAT. The last two baseline weeks already differ from the earlier baseline. Case mix and other workflow changes may also need investigation.

Ask the learner to summarize both the cutover delay and the later pattern in two sentences, then identify a remaining question. That is a better check of understanding than reciting the seven terms.

## Local work and further reading

The practicum asks learners to trace one report's fields, count included and excluded cases, and inspect any unusual interval. Have them identify who prepares the report and who approves its use.

The [Government Data Quality Framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-government-data-quality-framework/the-government-data-quality-framework) explains quality dimensions and choosing checks for a purpose. The lesson uses six commonly described dimensions plus the broader question of fitness for purpose.

[API teaching slides](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/teaching-slide-sets), Harrison Session 0, slides 18–20, and Session 2, slides 5–12 and 24–26, support discussion of how data are recorded, selected and used. PIER R5 provides the curriculum objectives. The lesson's counts and TAT values come from its editable case dataset.
## Common misconceptions

A blank LIS field does not mean the original time is unknowable: check the paper source and event definition. A lower median does not erase the long delays. Showing cutover separately does not mean removing its cases. Ask learners to point to the records supporting each distinction.
