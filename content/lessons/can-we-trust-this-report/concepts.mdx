## Seven ways to check data quality

Now that you have seen the spreadsheet and one affected accession, use these seven questions to examine the report.

- **Accuracy:** the data correctly describe what happened. *Example: the accession time in the LIS agrees with the original requisition.*
- **Completeness:** the required data are present. *Example: every included case has an accession time and an initial final-report time.*
- **Validity:** the data follow the expected format and rules. *Example: finalization does not occur before accession.*
- **Consistency:** the same item is defined and handled the same way wherever it is used. *Example: the pre- and post-go-live reports use the same TAT start and end points.*
- **Uniqueness:** each real case is represented once. *Example: an amendment does not add a second accession to the case count.*
- **Timeliness:** the data are current enough for the intended use. *Example: late cases are finalized before the monthly report is run.*
- **Fitness for purpose:** the data and method are adequate for the question being asked. *Example: a comparison used to judge go-live performance includes comparable groups of cases.*

The seven dimensions separate questions that are easy to mix together. A time may be valid because it has the right format, yet inaccurate because it is not the time written on the requisition. A dataset may contain no duplicate rows and still be incomplete.

### How the spreadsheet gets its data

In the usual workflow, accessioning staff enter the accession time in the LIS. The LIS analyst copies that field into the spreadsheet together with the initial final-report time.

During cutover, 27 accession times were written on requisitions and in the paper cutover log but were not entered into the structured LIS field. The quality coordinator used those source documents to complete the TAT calculation in the spreadsheet. That kept the delayed cases in the report, but the report must identify where those times came from and confirm that they describe the same accession event used for the other cases.

The director of surgical pathology and the quality group then decide whether the report supports the statement that TAT changed after go-live.
