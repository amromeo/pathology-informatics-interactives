# Debrief

The original report compared 210 pre-go-live cases with 245 post-go-live cases. The pre-go-live mean was 3.3 working days and the median was 2.7. After go-live, the mean increased to 3.8 while the median decreased to 2.5. Those two statistics seemed to point in opposite directions because the post-go-live TAT values were skewed by a smaller group of very long cases.

The weekly review showed that the long cases were concentrated during cutover. The 35 cutover cases had a mean TAT of 8.4 working days and a median of 9.5. During the six weeks after cutover, the 210 cases had a mean of 3.0 and a median of 2.5. Showing cutover week separately explains both findings: the laboratory experienced a severe temporary delay during cutover, followed by a modest improvement in routine TAT.

The cutover cases should not disappear from the report. The revised report keeps all 455 cases and shows three periods: pre-go-live, cutover week, and post-go-live after cutover. The weekly graph also makes the temporary spike visible.

The investigation identified a second issue. Twenty-seven cutover accession times were documented on requisitions and in the paper cutover log but were not entered into the structured LIS field. The quality coordinator used those source documents to calculate TAT, so the delayed cases remained in the report. The report should disclose that source and confirm that it represents the same accession event used for the other cases. Accessioning and the LIS team should also make sure the structured field is completed in future workflows.

The quality coordinator can prepare the report, the LIS analyst can explain the fields and reporting process, and the resident can review the cases and calculations. The director of surgical pathology is responsible for deciding whether the report is clinically sound and ready for use.
