# Review one laboratory server with the people who support it

## Who to meet

Meet the LIS analyst or system administrator, a laboratory supervisor and a security team member.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes. Ask for the approved system diagram, access procedure and latest recovery-test record.

## Steps

1. Choose one middleware or instrument-support server. Identify which orders, results and settings it stores and which clinical work stops if it is unavailable.
2. Ask who checks power and cooling, who approves account access and who maintains the network connections.
3. Follow one vendor support request: approval, named access, work performed, recorded changes and disconnection.
4. Review the last restoration test. Find what was restored and how staff checked queued orders, results and routing settings.
5. Pick one remaining concern. Describe its confidentiality, integrity or availability effect, the proposed control, who would perform the test and who would review the clinical consequence.

## Bring back

Prepare a one-page account of that concern and its test. Include the job titles responsible for the control and approval, plus one question the available records could not answer.

## Discuss with faculty

How would you tell whether a named account is also appropriately restricted? If the network route failed, which part of the recovery plan would restore connectivity and which would account for laboratory work?

## Privacy

Use only records authorized by your supervisor. Keep passwords, network addresses, patient identifiers and confidential security details in approved hospital systems. Bring back a description of the work and job titles.
