# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet a laboratory manager and security officer.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Review the approved access and risk assessment.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Record one system risk, its confidentiality/integrity/availability effects and an agreed control.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: record one system risk, its confidentiality/integrity/availability effects and an agreed control. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “count passwords without reviewing access” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum

With the laboratory manager or LIS team, identify one system that stores or routes laboratory orders or results outside the main laboratory information system (LIS).

1. Record where the system is physically or virtually hosted and who is responsible for its power, cooling, operating system, and network connection.
2. Identify which laboratory staff, IT staff, and vendor personnel can sign in. Determine whether they use named accounts and whether access is limited to the work each person performs.
3. Ask how vendor support sessions are approved, ended, and reviewed.
4. Find the date of the most recent backup-restoration test. Record what service and data were restored and how the laboratory confirmed that queued orders and results remained correct.
5. Identify the laboratory leader who reviews these controls before installation or a major change.

Do not record passwords, network addresses, patient information, or other security-sensitive details in teaching notes. Follow local policy and perform the review under appropriate supervision.
