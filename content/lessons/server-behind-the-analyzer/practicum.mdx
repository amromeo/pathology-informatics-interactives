# Review one laboratory server with the people who support it

## Who to meet

The LIS analyst or system administrator, a laboratory supervisor and someone from the security team.

## Time

30–60 minutes. Ask beforehand for the approved system diagram, the access procedure and the record of the last restore test.

## Steps

1. Choose one middleware or instrument-support server. Find out which orders, results and settings it holds, and what clinical work stops if it goes down.
2. Ask who checks power and cooling, who approves account access and who looks after the network connections.
3. Follow one vendor support request from start to finish: approval, the named account used, the work done, the changes recorded and the disconnection.
4. Look at the last restore test. Find out what was restored and how staff checked the waiting orders, results and routing settings.
5. Pick one concern that is still open. Describe its effect on confidentiality, integrity or availability, the proposed fix, who would test it and who would review what it means for patient care.

## Bring back

A one-page account of that concern and its test. Include the job titles of the people responsible for the fix and for approving it, and one question the records could not answer.

## Discuss with faculty

How would you tell whether a named account is also properly restricted? If the network route failed, which part of the recovery plan would get the connection back, and which part would account for the laboratory work done in the meantime?

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor has approved. Keep passwords, network addresses, patient identifiers and confidential security details in the hospital's own systems. Bring back a description of the work and job titles only.
