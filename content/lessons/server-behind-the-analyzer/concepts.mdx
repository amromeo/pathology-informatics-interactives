### Ask three questions about every finding

First ask who could see patient information that they should not see. Protecting information from unauthorized disclosure is called **confidentiality**. The unrestricted shared login and persistent vendor connection both raise that question for orders, results, and patient identifiers.

Next ask whether an unauthorized or unattributed person could change a routing rule, message queue, result, or recovery file. Protecting information and configuration from improper change is called **integrity**.

Finally ask whether the middleware will be available when the laboratory needs to route orders and results. Protecting dependable access to the service is called **availability**. Heat, power loss, ransomware, and an unusable backup can all interrupt patient-care work.

One finding may threaten more than one property, and one control rarely addresses every finding. The control plan exercise therefore asks what the laboratory can implement now, what risk remains, and what must be completed before approval.

### What the rules require for this server

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Security Rule requires administrative, physical and technical safeguards for electronic protected health information. Risk analysis identifies where access, integrity and availability can fail. For this server, named user IDs, access review, audit records and secure transmission support those obligations. A vendor that accesses patient information while supporting the server needs an appropriate business associate agreement; the research data use agreement in the export lesson serves a different purpose.

The privacy office assesses suspected unauthorized access to unsecured patient information. When breach notice is required, affected individuals must be notified without unreasonable delay and within 60 days of discovery. HHS reporting requirements depend on the number affected; media notice also applies when more than 500 residents of a state or jurisdiction are affected. Properly encrypted data that meet HHS guidance are not unsecured information, but the incident still needs investigation.

Records covered by 42 CFR Part 2 and some state protections, including protections for HIV results, need additional review. Accreditation also examines laboratory system security and access practices. Keep the evidence of risk analysis, approvals and access review with the server record.

### Check whether the controls work

Periodic vulnerability scans identify known weaknesses; patching addresses them after testing the laboratory workflow. Authorized penetration tests investigate whether controls can be bypassed. Phishing exercises and multifactor sign-in address different paths into the system. None replaces tested backups. Plan secure emergency access so protecting an account does not leave the laboratory unable to report an urgent result.
