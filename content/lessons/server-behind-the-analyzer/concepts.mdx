### Work through one finding

Suppose someone uses the shared login to open a patient's waiting result and change where it is sent. Looking at that result without permission breaks **confidentiality**. Changing the routing setting is an **integrity** problem. And because the login is shared, Jordan has no way to tell who did it. This finding is about viewing and changing records; it does not describe the service stopping.

For each of the other findings, ask what record or service is involved, what could happen to it, and which of the three properties that affects. Heat or a sudden loss of power can stop routing (**availability**) and cut off a write to disk, leaving a queue incomplete or damaged (**integrity**). A backup needs both checks: can staff get the service running again, and are the restored orders, results and settings complete and correct?

The map scores the effects the case describes for each finding. Other effects may be possible. If you think one applies, be ready to explain how it would happen, rather than ticking every box.

### What each fix changes

Jordan can replace the shared login with named accounts and limit who can change routing settings. The audit log can then tie each change to a person. Vendor work also needs an approved start, an agreed task, a record of what was changed and an end to the connection.

The network team can let instrument messages through a restricted boundary while blocking access to the server's files. This is network segmentation. A second network route solves a different problem: if construction cuts the only cable, segmentation cannot supply another connection. A backup can bring back saved data after a loss, but it cannot reconnect a cut cable either.

Each fix in the control builder has a cost in budget points and in staff-effort points. Add up each kind separately and stay within **15 budget points and 11 staff-effort points**. These limits exist only for this exercise, to make you choose. For example, protected hosting costs 4 budget and 1 effort; named accounts cost 2 and 2. Together they use 6 budget and 3 effort. Look at what is still unprotected before spending the rest.

In the [five event checks](#stress-test-title), **Blocked** means your fixes stop the event as described. **Partially mitigated** means the harm is reduced, but some interruption or way in is left. **Not mitigated** means none of your fixes touches that event. The summary counts both blocked and partial results as covered, so read each explanation before recommending the plan.

### Which review is needed?

HIPAA's Security Rule sets out administrative, physical and technical safeguards for ePHI. For this installation, the hospital's risk analysis should link each problem found to the access controls, audit records and recovery procedures that deal with it. The [HHS Security Rule summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html) explains these requirements, and separates the rule now in force from proposed changes.

The vendor will handle PHI while supporting the server, so the hospital has to review the business associate agreement as well as the technical access. A signed agreement and an approved, limited support session do different jobs. The research data use agreement in the later privacy case covers a different kind of disclosure.

If an incident may have exposed patient information, bring in the privacy office quickly to assess whether it is a breach and whether anyone must be notified. Getting the server running again does not end that review. Records covered by **42 CFR Part 2**, the federal rules protecting certain substance use disorder records, and by state law need extra review. A diagnosis alone does not decide whether Part 2 applies; what matters is which program the records came from.

In the review questions later in the lesson, choose the review that answers the question asked. Several requirements can apply to one installation. An access log, for example, can be used for security work and for an accreditation visit; the question tells you which one is being asked for.
