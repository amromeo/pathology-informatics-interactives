# Faculty guide: The Server Behind the Analyzer

## At a glance

Allow 25 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: match a security finding to its laboratory consequence; review the approved access and risk assessment; justify the first local check. PIER: 3.1, 3.2.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Activity | Ask the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Read the case and records | What decision has the laboratory been asked to make? |
| 5–18 | Make, test and revise a choice | Which record supports your decision? What could fail? |
| 18–25 | Debrief and retrieval questions | How would you verify this in your own laboratory? |

## Expected answers


Do not allow the discussion to collapse into password advice. Ask how each finding affects confidentiality, integrity, availability, or more than one.

Begin with one incomplete threat mapping and ask the learner to explain the missing property using the actual patient data, configuration, or service at risk. The scored map represents the stated scenario; accept a reasoned discussion of additional consequences rather than treating its labels as an exhaustive threat model.

Have learners run a small control plan, inspect the unmitigated events, and revise it within both limits: 15 budget points and 11 staff-effort points. Named accounts alone block shared-credential misuse in the simulation but only partially mitigate unapproved vendor changes. Neither a keyboard cover nor a policy memo mitigates a modeled event by itself.

Compare two affordable plans:

- Controlled hosting, named accounts, hardening/encryption, controlled vendor sessions, and a restoration drill cost 14 budget points and 11 effort points. Ransomware traversal remains partially mitigated.
- Environmental monitoring/shutdown, named accounts, network segmentation, controlled vendor sessions, and a restoration drill cost 13 budget points and 11 effort points. The heat and power event remains partially mitigated because result routing stops during shutdown.

Ask which residual risk the learner would bring to the laboratory director and security team, what further work is needed, and how result delivery would continue during an interruption. Coverage includes partial mitigation; it is not an installation approval. “Blocked” describes the particular simulated event, not a guarantee against every attack or outage. The exercise does not test every finding, including all disclosure risks for stored patient data.

Close by naming who implements each selected control and who reviews its clinical consequences. Include vendor-access terms and applicable institutional agreements in the local discussion. A restoration drill must verify laboratory work and configuration as well as server startup.

Have residents support their answer with a specific record or result from this lesson before comparing it with the slides.

## Common misconceptions

- “Share the supervisor login temporarily.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? The access arrangement needs individual authorization, appropriate safeguards and review of the business-associate relationship where applicable. Convenience does not remove these obligations.
- “Permit unrestricted access until repair ends.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? The access arrangement needs individual authorization, appropriate safeguards and review of the business-associate relationship where applicable. Convenience does not remove these obligations.
- “Count passwords without reviewing access.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? The laboratory and security team must understand what the vendor can access and how that access is monitored and removed.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: match a security finding to its laboratory consequence.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: review the approved access and risk assessment, because the laboratory and security team must understand what the vendor can access and how that access is monitored and removed.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes a laboratory manager and security officer and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Record one system risk, its confidentiality/integrity/availability effects and an agreed control. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.
