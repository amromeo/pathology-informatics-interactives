# Faculty guide: The Server Behind the Analyzer

## At a glance

Allow 25 minutes. Residents review a March 2026 middleware proposal with Dr. Chen and Jordan, before installation. Dr. Ruiz reviews the clinical consequences. Teach PIER 3.1 and 3.2 through a threat map, a constrained control plan and five review questions.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Work | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Case, visible terms and shared-account example | Which record is read or changed? |
| 5–11 | Map six findings | How could the described event affect more than one property? |
| 11–19 | Build and revise a plan | Which event is only partly addressed? |
| 19–25 | Review obligations and debrief | Who must show that the control works before approval? |

## Reasoning key

The findings combine a room inspection, account proposal, network diagram, vendor arrangement and backup record. They are observations from the installation review, not results of an attack.

- Heat and power: availability because routing stops; integrity because an interrupted storage write can damage a queue.
- Shared login: confidentiality because patient results can be opened; integrity because settings can be changed without attribution.
- Flat network: all three, because a compromised workstation can read or alter files and interrupt routing.
- Persistent vendor access: confidentiality and integrity through continuing access and unapproved changes.
- Untested backup: availability and integrity because service recovery and completeness of restored records remain unverified.
- Stored PHI: confidentiality is the described concern; review storage access and encryption.

The map uses the effects described in each finding. Ask a resident who proposes another effect to explain the intervening event. Changing a selection clears that finding's previous check.

## Compare two affordable plans

Budget and staff effort are separate exercise limits: 15 and 11 points. Protected hosting plus named accounts uses 6 budget and 3 effort.

- Protected hosting, named accounts, hardening/encryption, controlled vendor sessions and a restore drill use **14 budget, 11 effort**. The ransomware event remains partially mitigated.
- Environmental monitoring/shutdown, named accounts, segmentation, controlled vendor sessions and a restore drill use **13 budget, 11 effort**. The environmental event remains partially mitigated because shutdown stops routing.

The coverage total includes partial results. Have the resident name the remaining interruption, the person responsible for additional work and the test that would demonstrate improvement. A keyboard cover and a memo alone do not address any of the five modeled events. These five events are a focused comparison, not the entire installation review.

The later outage is caused by construction damage to the network. Segmentation limits connections; a separate route supplies redundancy. A restore drill checks recovery from saved copies and the surrounding procedures. Neither segmentation nor a backup repairs a severed cable. Preserve that distinction when teaching the connected cases.

## Review-question key

1. **Shared account:** security safeguards and risk analysis guide the account change.
2. **Vendor handling PHI for the hospital:** review the business associate agreement and technical access.
3. **Possible ransomware disclosure:** involve the privacy office in breach assessment while technical recovery proceeds.
4. **Records from a substance use treatment program:** determine whether Part 2 and state protections apply.
5. **Accreditation visit:** prepare the access-review record requested by the inspector. It can also support routine security work; the choices describe overlapping activities.

## Common misconceptions

A successful backup job records that a copy operation completed. Ask for a restoration record showing the application started and that orders, results, settings and queues were checked. Named accounts identify users, but vendor sessions still need limited access and approved work. Encryption protects stored information; it does not prevent a permitted user from making an unauthorized routing change.

For discussion, assign Jordan, the security team, facilities, vendor management and Dr. Ruiz. Ask each person to name one check they perform and the record they hand to the next person. The local practicum asks for the same concrete chain of work.

## Sources and boundaries

Use the [HHS Security Rule summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html) for current safeguard requirements and [business associate guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html) for the vendor relationship. The Security Rule proposal is identified as proposed by HHS; do not teach its proposed provisions as current requirements.

For an actual incident, use the [HHS breach guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html) with the privacy office. Required individual notices have a maximum of 60 days after discovery and must be sent without unreasonable delay. HHS notice differs at **500 or more** affected people; media notice uses **more than 500** residents of a state or jurisdiction. Smaller breaches have a different HHS reporting schedule. These are notification deadlines, not permission to delay reporting an incident internally.

The [HHS Part 2 fact sheet](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/fact-sheet-42-cfr-part-2-final-rule/index.html) describes the rule with a February 16, 2026 compliance date. Check applicability to the source program and records rather than inferring it from a diagnosis. No specific accreditation clause or state-law rule is asserted by this exercise.
