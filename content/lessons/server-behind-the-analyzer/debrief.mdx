## Debrief

**Informatics principle:** Security and availability must be designed together.

The threat map connects six installation findings to confidentiality, integrity, and availability. A shared account permits disclosure and unattributed changes; a flat network also creates a route for interruption of result routing. A successful backup job does not establish that restored orders, results, and configuration will be complete and correct.

If you built a portfolio, compare the five stress-test results with the controls you selected. Named accounts address shared-credential misuse, but leave vendor work only partially controlled without approved, time-limited sessions. Protected hosting addresses the local heat and power event; it does not demonstrate recovery from backup.

**Five scenarios covered does not mean five events prevented.** The score includes partial mitigation. One affordable plan leaves ransomware traversal partially mitigated; another protects the network but still requires shutdown during an environmental event. Explain the remaining interruption or disclosure risk before recommending a plan. The budget and staff points are teaching constraints, not estimates for an actual installation, and these five scenarios do not constitute a complete security assessment.

Facilities, security, LIS, and vendor-management teams implement controls. The laboratory director retains responsibility for approved procedures, access, continuity, and clinical use.
