## Debrief: match a security finding to its laboratory consequence

### What happened in this case

The middleware server needs protecting from people who should not get in, and it needs a tested way to keep results moving during an outage.

The map links six installation findings to confidentiality, integrity and availability. A shared login lets someone see results and change settings without anyone knowing who did it. An open network also gives an infection a way to stop result routing. A backup job that reports success does not show that restored orders, results and settings will be complete and correct.

If you built a control plan, compare the [five event results](#stress-test-title) with the fixes you chose. Named accounts deal with misuse of a shared login, but vendor work stays only partly controlled until sessions are approved and time-limited. Protected hosting deals with the heat and power event; a restore drill is what checks recovery from backup.

The coverage score counts partial results. The plan in the faculty key, at 14 budget and 11 effort points, only partly handles the ransomware event. The other plan, at 13 budget and 11 effort, restricts the network but still means shutting down during a heat or power event. Explain what interruption or exposure is left before recommending a plan. Dr. Ruiz and the supporting teams also have to look at risks outside these five events.

Heat or power loss shows why one finding can hit two properties: routing stops, and a write cut off halfway can damage records. So a restore drill has to check both that the service comes back and that its queues and settings are complete.

In the outage later in the case, construction cuts the network cable. Segmentation would limit which computers can reach each other; a second route would give another connection. Because the restore drill was never done, staff are less ready to check the recovered service and match it against the paper records. Keep those effects separate from the cause of the cable damage.

<summary>A closer look: follow one vendor support session</summary>

Suppose the vendor needs to look into a problem with instrument messages. The LIS analyst should know who connected, when the session started and what was changed. A shared login makes it hard to tell who did the work. A connection left open afterward can still be used long after that support task is over.

Named accounts, approved sessions and a record of changes each deal with a different part of that problem. The network team also needs to check which other systems the middleware server can reach. Separately, staff need to restore a backup and look through the recovered orders, results and settings before counting on that way of recovering.

**During the installation review:** What would you ask the vendor, the security team and the LIS analyst to show you about a finished support session?
