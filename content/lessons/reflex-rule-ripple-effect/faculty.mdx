# Faculty guide: Updating a Thyroid Reflex Rule

## At a glance

Allow 30 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: check dependent reports after changing a reflex rule; list every result that depends on the reflex assay; justify the first local check. PIER: 4.3, 4.5, 4.2, 8.2.

## Expected answers


Ask learners to list every dependent object that should enter change scope: display, reflexes, calculations, decision support, interfaces, reports, validation cases, procedures, and training.

Ask learners to explain how TH-01 can have a correct displayed interval and an
incorrect downstream order. A complete answer identifies the separate display
definition, reflex revision 8, its 0.40 threshold, and the order created by that
revision. The reference interval did not itself place the FT4 order.

Expected configuration: lower 0.27, upper 4.20, strict outside-boundary comparison,
adult TSH-with-reflex scope, final numeric results, and duplicate prevention for
pending/completed FT4s on the same accession. The original rule matches 10/13
actions and creates five orders; the correction matches 13/13 and creates two.
An inclusive comparison fails TH-04 and TH-06. Removing duplicate prevention
fails TH-08 and TH-09; bypassing scope fails TH-11 and TH-12; allowing preliminary
numeric results fails TH-13. Missing TSH still requires review.

Require the learner to identify both removed orders and retained orders. Ask why
a fall in order volume would also occur if all reflexes were disabled, and why
that would not demonstrate success. Ask for the denominator: 13 selected records,
including nine eligible final adult reflex results, two review exceptions, and
two outside the protocol. This is not a random sample or a full-day utilization
study.

Discuss clinical approval separately from software maintenance. Reference
intervals do not automatically define optimal reflex thresholds. The case
explicitly states that the director approved these order criteria for the new
assay and adult protocol. Do not generalize this sequence to all patients,
pregnancy, pediatric testing, or suspected pituitary disease.

The final handoff should name the approved revision, every dependent object,
expected and observed actions, affected production accessions, monitoring
denominators, and the person responsible for follow-up. Completed FT4 results
are not discarded solely because the order rule was incorrect.

Harrison/API: Session 3, slides 7–13; Session 7, slides 17 and 27; Session 9,
slides 7–10. Clinical context: [American Thyroid Association, Thyroid Function
Tests](https://www.thyroid.org/thyroid-function-tests/). The exact thresholds and
workflow here belong to the case's approved protocol, not an ATA reflex mandate.

## Common misconceptions

- “Only the reflex order count.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? Removing an order does not automatically remove calculations or interpretations that depended on its result. Test the complete report and unaffected pathways.
- “Only the analyzer test menu.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? Removing an order does not automatically remove calculations or interpretations that depended on its result. Test the complete report and unaffected pathways.
- “Hide the unexpected comment in the chart only.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? The dependency list tells the team which calculations, comments and reports need regression testing.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: check dependent reports after changing a reflex rule.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: list every result that depends on the reflex assay, because the dependency list tells the team which calculations, comments and reports need regression testing.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes the assay director and LIS analyst and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Draw a reflex dependency map and define one quality indicator with a denominator. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.
