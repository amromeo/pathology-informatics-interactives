# Faculty guide: Updating a Thyroid Reflex Rule

Ask learners to list every dependent object that should enter change scope: display, reflexes, calculations, decision support, interfaces, reports, validation cases, procedures, and training.

Ask learners to explain how TH-01 can have a correct displayed interval and an
incorrect downstream order. A complete answer identifies the separate display
definition, reflex revision 8, its 0.40 threshold, and the order created by that
revision. The reference interval did not itself place the FT4 order.

Expected configuration: lower 0.27, upper 4.20, strict outside-boundary comparison,
adult TSH-with-reflex scope, final numeric results, and duplicate prevention for
pending/completed FT4s on the same accession. The original rule matches 10/13
actions and creates five orders; the correction matches 13/13 and creates two.
An inclusive comparison fails TH-04 and TH-06. Removing duplicate prevention
fails TH-08 and TH-09; bypassing scope fails TH-11 and TH-12; allowing preliminary
numeric results fails TH-13. Missing TSH still requires review.

Require the learner to identify both removed orders and retained orders. Ask why
a fall in order volume would also occur if all reflexes were disabled, and why
that would not demonstrate success. Ask for the denominator: 13 selected records,
including nine eligible final adult reflex results, two review exceptions, and
two outside the protocol. This is not a random sample or a full-day utilization
study.

Discuss clinical approval separately from software maintenance. Reference
intervals do not automatically define optimal reflex thresholds. The case
explicitly states that the director approved these order criteria for the new
assay and adult protocol. Do not generalize this sequence to all patients,
pregnancy, pediatric testing, or suspected pituitary disease.

The final handoff should name the approved revision, every dependent object,
expected and observed actions, affected production accessions, monitoring
denominators, and the person responsible for follow-up. Completed FT4 results
are not discarded solely because the order rule was incorrect.
