# Trace a local reflex order

## Who to meet

An assay director and an LIS or middleware analyst.

## Time

30–60 minutes.

## Steps

1. Choose an approved reflex protocol. Note the order type, the patients it applies to, the first test and the tests that can be added.
2. Find the written criteria and the saved rule. Ask which report definitions, result statuses and order codes the rule uses.
3. Follow an accession you are allowed to review through its first result, the rule's decision and the added or existing order.
4. Look at test examples just below, exactly at and just above each threshold, plus missing results and follow-up orders that already exist.
5. Set a monitoring period and decide which accessions count. Explain how the team would spot a missed addition, an unnecessary addition and a duplicate.

## Bring back

A one-page diagram linking the first order, result, rule and follow-up test. Add a small table of expected actions and one monitoring measure, with its numerator and denominator.

## Discuss with faculty

Which separate definition could be missed during an assay change? What would you check before acting on an order that the old rule already created?

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor allows you to review. Keep patient identifiers and screenshots in the hospital's own systems, and bring back a description with no patient identifiers.
