# Updating a Thyroid Reflex Rule

On Monday morning, the chemistry supervisor finds an free thyroxine (FT4) order added to an
accession whose thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) is within the interval printed on its report. You are the
pathology resident working with the laboratory information system (LIS) analyst to investigate whether the new
thyroid assay configuration was implemented as approved.

## What changed

The laboratory began using a new TSH assay at 06:00. Before the change, the
laboratory director reviewed the adult reference interval and separately approved
the adult TSH-with-reflex protocol. The approved lower threshold for adding FT4
changed from 0.40 to 0.27 mIU/L; the upper threshold remained 4.20 mIU/L.

The LIS analyst updated the report's reference-interval definition. The reflex
process uses a separate rule entry to decide whether to place another order.
Your investigation begins with accession TH-01: TSH **0.30 mIU/L**, finalized at
07:18, displayed interval **0.27–4.20 mIU/L**, and an FT4 order created at 07:18.
No earlier FT4 was linked to that accession.

## Your task

Compare the report definition, approved protocol, configured rule, and order
record. Revise the order rule and replay 13 selected accession records from the
morning review. Account for every added order, retained order, exception, and
case outside the rule's approved scope before recommending the change.


## What you will be able to do

- Check dependent reports after changing a reflex rule.
- List every result that depends on the reflex assay.
- Explain why “hide the unexpected comment in the chart only” is an incomplete approach.

Start by reading the case records. Make a choice before checking the explanation; you can revise it and test again.
