## Debrief: check dependent reports after changing a reflex rule

### What happened in this case


Changing the assay requires reviewing every separate rule that uses its result.

The report definition used 0.27–4.20 mIU/L, while the order rule retained 0.40 as
its lower threshold. TH-01, TH-02, and TH-04 therefore received additional FT4 orders
under the original rule. Correcting the lower threshold preserves FT4 orders at
TH-03 and TH-07 and reuses the orders already linked to TH-08 and TH-09.

The exact boundary matters: changing “below” to “at or below” adds an order at
0.27, and changing “above” to “at or above” adds one at 4.20. Removing scope or
final-status restrictions changes other records even when both numeric thresholds
are correct. This is why the full action list matters more than the new-order
count alone.

The replay compares 13 selected records, including records needing review and
those outside the rule. The fall from five new FT4 orders to two identifies this
configuration's effect in that set. It is not a measured hospital-wide utilization
rate. Broader monitoring requires a defined population, period, and denominator.

The analyst changes configuration; the pathologist approves clinical thresholds; laboratory operations monitors consequences.

### Who does what

Work with the assay director and LIS analyst. The person making a technical change should document the test evidence; the responsible clinical or operational lead approves the intended use.

### Connection to PIER 4.3, 4.5, 4.2, 8.2

The case asks you to check dependent reports after changing a reflex rule using laboratory records and explain the consequence of your choice.
