## Debrief: evaluate the pathologist and algorithm working together

### What happened in this case


At 0.50, the 12-location set has three true positives, two false negatives, three false positives, and four true negatives. Sensitivity is 3/5 (60%); specificity is 4/7 (57.1%); three of six marks are reference mitoses (50%). M03 at 0.60 is included at a threshold of 0.60 and excluded above it.

Lower thresholds can reveal more reference figures while adding false marks. Higher thresholds reduce review of marks but can leave more figures unmarked. Neither effect tells us the final pathologist's accuracy or the time required for a complete search.

The separate reader study found 36/40 figures without assistance, 38/40 with assistance and full-area review, and 34/40 with marks-only review. Mean times were 6.2, 8.1, and 4.4 minutes per slide. These observations illustrate why model sensitivity, speed, and final human performance must all be assessed; they do not show a recommended clinical threshold.

A pathologist must confirm proposed marks, inspect unmarked tissue, and use an appropriate counting area. The team needs evidence for the intended population, scanner, stain, interface, and reader workflow. Investigate failures and site differences, preserve accountable denominators, and assess changed versions or thresholds before clinical release. The concepts extend Topic 2's AI work to an image-analysis workflow without turning detection into automatic diagnosis or prognosis.

### Who does what

Work with a pathologist using image-analysis tools. The person making a technical change should document the test evidence; the responsible clinical or operational lead approves the intended use.
