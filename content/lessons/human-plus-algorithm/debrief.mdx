## Debrief: evaluate the pathologist and algorithm working together

### What happened in this case

At a threshold of 0.50, the 12-location set has three true positives, two false negatives, three false positives and four true negatives. Sensitivity is 3/5 (60%); specificity is 4/7 (57.1%); three of the six marks are real mitoses (50%). M03, with a score of 0.60, is marked at a threshold of 0.60 and not marked above it.

Lowering the threshold can show more real mitoses but adds false marks. Raising it means fewer marks to check but can leave more mitoses unmarked. Neither tells you how accurate the pathologist's final count will be, or how long a full search takes.

The separate reader study found 36 of 40 mitoses without the tool, 38 of 40 with the tool plus a search of the whole area, and 34 of 40 when readers only checked the marks. Mean times were 6.2, 8.1 and 4.4 minutes per slide. These results show why the tool's sensitivity, the speed and the pathologist's final accuracy all have to be measured; they do not point to a threshold to use clinically.

The pathologist has to confirm the marks, search the unmarked tissue and count in the right area. The team needs evidence for the patients, scanner, stain, screen and way of reading the service will actually use. Look into failures and differences between sites, keep denominators someone is answerable for, and check any new version or threshold before clinical release. These ideas carry the earlier AI lesson into image analysis, without turning detection into automatic diagnosis or prognosis.

The tool's marks change where a reader looks first. Lowering the threshold adds both useful marks and distracting ones. The final count depends on how the pathologist checks the marks and searches the rest of the tissue.

Compare reading with the tool plus a full search against checking only the marks. The faster way missed more mitoses. To understand why, look at differences between readers, the cases each one saw, the order of sessions and the time spent searching. Keep the tool's results for each location separate from the pathologists' final counts.

Later checks need someone to review unmarked tissue independently, as well as the marked areas. If the tool's suggestions become the answers used to judge its next version, its earlier misses get carried into the next comparison.

**When planning the audit:** How would you find a missed mitosis that no reader had been prompted to inspect?

### Who does what

The pathologist using the tool and the technical team test it and record the results. The responsible pathologist reviews the reader study and the monitoring reports and decides whether the tool can be used, and when it should be paused.
