# Look at one image-analysis tool at your institution

## Who to meet

A pathologist who supervises an approved image-analysis tool.

## Time

30–60 minutes.

## Steps

1. Find out exactly what the tool does, how images are prepared for it, what area or cells it counts, the software version, the threshold, what it outputs and what the pathologist must check.
2. Ask how mitoses or other findings the tool missed can be found, and what happens when an analysis fails.
3. Look at the approved monitoring plan for errors in final readings, the mix of cases in each group, the extra work and how changes to the tool are controlled.
4. If no tool is in use, sketch instead how you would evaluate one independently using approved local training images.

## Bring back

A one-page summary of the tool and how its use is checked. Include one open question and the person who can answer it. Do not install a tool or change a clinical threshold as part of this exercise.

## Discuss with faculty

How would you find a finding that neither the tool nor the reader noticed? Why is the share of correct marks on a hand-picked list not the tool's clinical sensitivity?

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor allows you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots in the hospital's own systems, and bring back a description with no patient identifiers.
