# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet a pathologist using image-analysis tools.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Review the complete reader-study design.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Design an audit of marked and unmarked areas with a reference review and workload measures.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: design an audit of marked and unmarked areas with a reference review and workload measures. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “use candidate precision as clinical sensitivity” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum

With a pathologist supervising an approved image-analysis workflow, identify the actual task, input preparation, area or cell denominator, software version, threshold, output, and human review responsibilities. Ask how false negatives can be found and how failed analyses are handled.

Review an approved monitoring plan for final reader errors, subgroup case mix, workload, and change control. If no tool is deployed, write a proposal for an independent evaluation using approved local training images. Do not install a tool or change a clinical threshold for this exercise.
