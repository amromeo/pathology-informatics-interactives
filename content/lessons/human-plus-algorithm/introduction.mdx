# Reviewing a Mitosis Detection Tool

You are the pathology resident helping Dr. Rao, a breast pathologist, evaluate a tool that detects mitoses.

The tool marks possible mitotic figures within a chosen tumor area. The pathologist has to check those marks, search the rest of the area and record the final count. The tool does not give a diagnosis, a grade or a prognosis. The vendor's sensitivity figure does not tell Dr. Rao how many false marks someone will have to check, or what happens when a reader sees no marks at all.

The list has 12 locations picked from a development review: five that are mitotic figures and seven that are not. Two pathologists agreed the right answer for each one. All 12 stay in the exercise. This small, hand-picked set is for working through the arithmetic and looking at errors; it does not estimate how accurate the tool is on whole slides in practice.

## Terms used in this case

- **Mitotic figure:** a cell caught in division. Pathologists count them in a set area to help grade a tumor.
- **Threshold:** the score at or above which the tool marks a location.
- **Reference answer:** what two pathologists agreed each location was, used to judge the tool.
- **Reader study:** a study of pathologists reading slides with and without the tool.
- **Automation bias:** trusting a system's output too much, for example taking a slide with no marks as having no mitoses.

## Your task

Look at the marked locations and change the marking threshold. Compare the errors at two sites. Then review a separate study of pathologists using the tool, and a monthly monitoring report, and choose what to keep checking once the tool is in use.

## What you will be able to do

- Work out sensitivity, specificity and the share of correct marks at a chosen threshold, and see how they change.
- Explain why checking only the marked spots misses mitoses the tool never flagged.
- Read a reader study for what it shows about the pathologist and tool working together.
- Choose what to monitor once the tool is in use, and what to do about a problem at one site.
