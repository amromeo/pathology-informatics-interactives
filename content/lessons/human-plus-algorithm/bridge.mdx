## Evaluate the reader as well as the marks

The reader study is separate from the 12-location exercise. In a crossover evaluation, four trained pathologists each reviewed the same five slides in separate sessions with reordered slides and a three-week interval. There are 20 slides and 40 consensus reference mitotic figures. The table pools one interpretation per slide per condition. All slides, including difficult areas, remain included.

The three conditions were unassisted review, assistance with a full-area search, and an exploratory marks-only workflow. Mean minutes are total review minutes divided by 20 slides within each condition. Individual readers and slides vary. The observed counts and times do not change when you move the candidate threshold: no threshold-to-reader-performance model was measured.

Assistance with full-area review found more reference figures but took longer. Marks-only review was faster and missed more. This suggests a workflow concern; a small crossover study with repeated slides cannot establish general clinical benefit. Review recall, order effects, reader variation, false final counts, uncertainty, and independent validation before approval. Missing figures or counting false marks could affect a grading task, but this study has not measured patient outcomes or grade changes.

**Automation bias** is excessive reliance on a system's output. A negative overlay can falsely reassure a reader. Training and an interface that supports searching unmarked tissue should be tested, not merely stated in a policy.

## Monitor what the deployed system actually does

The monthly report counts submitted slides and analysis failures for all submissions. The reference audit covers selected areas within a separate sample of successfully analyzed slides. Its figure denominator is neither submitted slides nor all figures in all submitted tissue. Do not silently drop failed analyses or assume an absent result is negative.

Month 2 includes a new site and staining workflow. Investigate input quality, version, threshold, errors, and reader actions before attributing the difference to drift. **Drift** means a change over time in inputs, relationships, or performance; a changed population can also alter measured performance. Preserve a reference audit of unmarked areas, subgroup counts, failures, review time, and final reader errors. Assign a responsible pathologist and technical contact, a review schedule, and conditions for pausing assistance.

If disease prevalence changes while sensitivity and specificity stayed fixed, the fraction of positive results that are true positives would still change. In practice, sensitivity and specificity can also change with case mix. Do not transfer the selected list's predictive value to routine signout.
