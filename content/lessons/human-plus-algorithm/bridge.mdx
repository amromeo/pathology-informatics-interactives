## Evaluate the reader as well as the marks

The reader study is separate from the 12-location exercise. Four trained pathologists each reviewed their own set of five slides (20 slides in all, with 40 mitotic figures agreed by consensus). Each read their five slides three ways, in sessions three weeks apart, with the slides in a different order each time. The table adds up the 20 slide reviews for each way of reading. Every slide is included, difficult areas and all.

The three ways were: reading without the tool; reading with the tool's marks plus a search of the whole area; and, as an experiment, checking only the marks. Mean minutes are the total reading time divided by the 20 slides for each way. Readers and slides vary. These counts and times do not change when you move the threshold, because nobody measured how readers perform at different thresholds.

Using the tool and still searching the whole area found more mitoses but took longer. Checking only the marks was faster and missed more. That is a warning about how the tool is used; a small study in which readers saw the same slides several times cannot show a general clinical benefit. Before approval, think about readers remembering slides, the order of sessions, differences between readers, wrong final counts, uncertainty and a validation on separate cases. Missing mitoses, or counting false marks, could change a grade, but this study did not measure grades or patient outcomes.

**Automation bias** is trusting a system's output too much. A screen with no marks can falsely reassure a reader. Training, and a screen that helps readers search unmarked tissue, should be tested, not just written into a policy.

## Watch what the tool actually does once it is in use

The monthly report counts every slide sent for analysis and every analysis that failed. The audit against reference answers covers chosen areas in a separate sample of slides that were analyzed successfully. Its denominator is not all the slides sent, and not every mitosis in all the tissue. Do not quietly drop failed analyses, or assume a missing result means no mitoses.

Month 2 adds a new site and a new staining process. Check image quality, the software version, the threshold, the errors and what readers did before blaming drift. **Drift** means a change over time in the images coming in, in how they relate to the answer, or in performance; a different mix of patients can also change the numbers. Keep auditing unmarked areas against reference answers, and keep counts by group, failures, reading time and final reader errors. Name a responsible pathologist and a technical contact, set a review schedule and decide when the tool should be paused.

If how common mitoses are changed while sensitivity and specificity stayed the same, the share of marks that are real mitoses would still change. In practice, sensitivity and specificity can change with the case mix too. Do not carry the hand-picked list's predictive value over to routine sign-out.
