### Start with the laboratory's approved policy

This laboratory's troponin policy requires a final numeric result from 2 through
10,000 ng/L, accepted **quality control (QC)** for the run, and no unresolved
instrument flag. Results at or above 1,000 ng/L enter its critical-result review
and communication workflow. These are the case laboratory's configured limits,
not diagnostic cutoffs or a release policy for every troponin assay.

The comparison must use the same patient's prior result from the same assay,
obtained no more than 24 hours earlier. Missing, zero, or otherwise unusable prior
values are sent for review in this laboratory. A blank field is not a measured
zero. A zero denominator cannot support a percentage calculation, and results
from different assays cannot simply be substituted as a prior comparison.

### Calculate the two changes

The **absolute difference** is the size of the current-minus-prior difference,
regardless of direction. For AV-01, it is 48 minus 12 = **36 ng/L**. The
**relative difference** divides that size by the prior value: 36 / 12 × 100 =
**300%**. Both increases and decreases are evaluated by their size.

The director approved a hold when **either** of these branches is met:

1. The absolute difference is **at least 50 ng/L**.
2. The relative difference is **at least 200%**, **and** the absolute difference
   is **at least 20 ng/L**.

The 20 ng/L requirement prevents a large percentage caused by a small denominator
from being sufficient on its own. A change from 2 to 8 ng/L is also 300%, but its
absolute difference is only 6 ng/L. Under this policy, that change alone does not
require a hold. In AV-01, the 36 ng/L difference is large enough for the relative
branch, even though it is below the separate 50 ng/L limit.

### AND and OR change which results are held

The two requirements **within** the relative branch use AND: both must be true.
The absolute and relative branches are combined with OR: either branch can hold
the result. Combining the branches with AND would let AV-01 release because its
absolute difference is below 50 ng/L. Keeping only the absolute branch has the
same effect for this accession, but the defects are different configurations.

The controls start with the transferred rule. Change one setting at a time and
inspect its effect below. The numeric eligibility, final-status, and critical
gates remain in place while you edit the delta, history, QC, and flag settings.
The trace shows every gate; release requires all gates to pass. A skipped delta
calculation does not override a failed eligibility or history check.

### A hold is a request for review

An unexpected change can arise from a real clinical change, specimen problems,
patient identification problems, or analytical issues. A hold does not determine
which explanation is correct. Staff investigate according to the laboratory's
procedure, preserve the original result, and communicate urgent findings promptly.
Likewise, automatic release does not mean that a result is within the reference
interval or that the patient is well. Reference intervals, reportable ranges,
critical-result procedures, and automatic-release criteria serve different purposes.
