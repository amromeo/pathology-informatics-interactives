## Debrief: explain why AV-01 was released

AV-01 rose from 12 to 48 ng/L. The absolute difference was **36 ng/L**; dividing 36 by the prior value of 12 gave a **300%** relative difference.

The rule as moved to the middleware used only the absolute branch. Because 36 was below its 50 ng/L limit, that branch let the result through. The other checks passed, and the middleware released the result.

The approved policy also contains a relative branch. AV-01 meets both of its conditions: at least 200% and at least 20 ng/L. Joining the branches with OR gives that branch a way to hold the result. The [branch comparison](#release-branch-examples) explains why joining them with AND would still let AV-01 continue.

### What the other records add

The original rule matches **20 of 22** expected decisions. It misses the holds for AV-01 and AV-03. With the approved OR configuration and the other approved settings intact, all **22** match.

AV-04 also has a 300% change, but it only rises from 2 to 8 ng/L. The relative branch's 20 ng/L minimum keeps it released, as intended. Take that minimum away and it is held for no reason.

AV-06 has no usable prior result. Its delta cannot be calculated, and the serial-order history check sends it to review. AV-09's QC was not accepted, and a small change in the result does nothing about that separate problem. Open their entries in the [complete check list](#release-replay-heading) to connect each outcome with its reason.

### What staff review before use

The analyst records the settings and the expected and actual outcomes for the whole set. Chemistry staff and the director review the policy, how held results are handled, how results are reported and the monitoring plan.

A hold asks staff to look into the result. A real change in the patient, a problem with the specimen and a problem with the analysis each need something different. Staff follow the local procedure and pass on urgent findings quickly. The exercise checks whether the software applies that procedure the same way every time.

To apply this in your own laboratory, start with the order type, the assay, the prior result needed and the other release checks. Those details decide which records belong in the validation set and how missing information should be handled.
