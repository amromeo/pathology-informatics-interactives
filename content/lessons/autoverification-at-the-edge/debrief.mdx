## Debrief: test a release rule with both held and releasable results

### What happened in this case


The serial troponin rule must hold unusable history and release eligible results at the stated boundaries.

AV-01 had a 36 ng/L absolute difference and a 300% relative difference. The
absolute-only configuration released it because 36 is below 50. The approved
relative branch requires at least 200% and at least 20 ng/L; both are met.
Combining the branches with OR restores the intended hold. Combining them with
AND still permits the reported result to release.

Compare that result with the 2-to-8 ng/L record. It has the same percentage change
but does not meet the 20 ng/L minimum. Removing that minimum causes an unintended
hold. Missing history, older or different-assay results, QC failure, instrument
flags, and result status each need their own decision; none can be inferred from
the delta alone.

The replay demonstrates the configured behavior against the laboratory's policy.
It does not determine whether a held troponin represents disease, an analytical
problem, or a specimen problem. Staff investigate holds and avoid delaying urgent
communication. A stable higher result may release under this policy without being
clinically normal.

The LIS or middleware analyst implements the rule. Technical and medical leadership approve criteria, validation, monitoring, and change control.

### Who does what

Work with a chemistry supervisor and middleware analyst. The person making a technical change should document the test evidence; the responsible clinical or operational lead approves the intended use.

### Connection to PIER 4.3, 4.5, 5.4, 8.2

The case asks you to test a release rule with both held and releasable results using laboratory records and explain the consequence of your choice.
