# Faculty guide: Checking Automatic Result Release

Allow 35 minutes. The resident should translate the written serial-troponin policy into conditions, explain the omitted relative branch and test the correction. The lesson addresses PIER 4.3, 4.5, 5.4 and 8.2.

## Run of show

Use about 7 minutes for the case and order scope, 10 for arithmetic and branch logic, 12 for the trace and complete checks, and 6 for implementation and local discussion.

## Establish the order scope first

This rule covers the laboratory's serial troponin pathway. A missing same-assay prior result prompts review under that policy. Initial troponins follow a separate rule without a delta comparison.

Ask the resident why those pathways need different handling. A first sample often has no prior result; a requested serial sample calls for checking whether the expected prior sample exists and is linked correctly. Preserve the other release checks in both discussions.

The numerical thresholds and critical-result pathway are the case laboratory's approved policy. They are not universal troponin recommendations. Laboratories may use different validated criteria, including automatic release with a separate critical-result notification process.

## Work through the two branches

For AV-01, ask the resident to calculate 48 − 12 = 36 ng/L, then 36 ÷ 12 × 100 = 300%. Ask why 300% describes the change while the current concentration is four times the prior concentration.

Have the resident answer three questions aloud: is 36 at least 50; is 300 at least 200; is 36 at least 20? The answers are no, yes and yes. The relative branch calls for a hold.

The required configuration is OR between branches, absolute limit 50 ng/L, relative limit 200%, relative-branch minimum 20 ng/L, same-assay history within 24 hours, unusable history held, QC and flag checks enabled. The original configuration matches 20/22; the correction matches 22/22.

Compare AV-04, a 2-to-8 change. Its 300% relative difference accompanies only a 6 ng/L absolute difference. Removing the minimum introduces an unnecessary hold. Then use AV-16 to show that a decrease can meet the absolute branch.

## Read the trace and test the change

The trace follows the selected record through each check and updates when settings change. The complete check list compares all 22 records with fixed expected outcomes. The learner should explain both a missed hold and a correct release.

Useful challenges include AND between branches, a zero minimum for the relative branch, disabled QC checks, missing history, a result exactly at a boundary and a preliminary result. Each setting change clears the old replay and final recommendation.

Ask what remains after a passing replay. Staff must check the review worklist, manual verification, LIS/EHR reporting and response to a problem after use begins. The director reviews the policy and test record before the analyst schedules the change.

## Common misconceptions

- **A 300% change means the current value is three times the prior value.** Work through the change and the total separately.
- **AND makes the rule more cautious.** With AND between hold branches, fewer results meet the hold condition. Use AV-01 to demonstrate.
- **Holding everything proves the rule is safe.** Include results expected to release and discuss the effect of delaying them.
- **A missing prior result is zero.** Ask what measurement zero would assert and what happens when a percentage calculation divides by zero.
- **Passing creatinine checks supplies every eGFR input.** Review the additional calculation question. Age must be resolved from an authorized source before the configured equation can be used.

## Teaching sources

[CLSI AUTO15](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/auto15/) provides professional guidance on designing and validating autoverification for specified laboratory disciplines. Its scope does not supply this lesson's troponin thresholds.

For an optional example of practice, [Krasowski and colleagues' core chemistry study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4023033/) describes rules involving middleware, instrument information and patient-result comparisons. Ask residents which checks their own laboratory uses; do not transfer the study's algorithm into local policy.

[NIDDK's adult eGFR equations](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/research-funding/research-programs/kidney-clinical-research-epidemiology/laboratory/glomerular-filtration-rate-equations/adults) identify inputs used in the calculation, including age. Use this optional reference to discuss why accepting a creatinine result does not resolve a missing calculation input.

[API's system implementation and management slides](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/assets/lms/jimharrison/PathInformatics%209%20Impl-Mgmt%20slides.pptx), by James H. Harrison, Jr., support discussion of testing and controlled changes. Use session 9, slides 7–10, with the LIS configuration concepts from session 3, slides 7–13. Ask the resident to name an unaffected release case that must be retested.
