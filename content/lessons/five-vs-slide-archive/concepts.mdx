### Why size is only one question

The archive expects about 1.2 million slides a year at about 1.6 gigabytes (GB) each, which rounds to 1.9 petabytes (PB). One PB is one million GB. That is the **volume**, before counting backup copies or how long the images have to be kept.

**Velocity** is how fast records arrive or have to be delivered. Together, the 18 scanners can write 0.5 GB a second during the overnight run. The target for viewing is under two seconds until the first field of view appears on screen, not until the whole slide has downloaded.

**Variety** is the range of data types: image pixels, a protein staining score, gene variants and a written stage. **Veracity** asks whether the records, and the links between them, can be trusted. Last quarter, 2,160 of 120,000 scanned slides (1.8%) had no confirmed case, part or block link. **Value** asks whether the combined information answers the clinical question. The five Vs affect one another, but each one needs its own check.

### What each identifier points to

A patient identifier links the encounters for one person. A case number identifies one accession. A part is one specimen submitted within that case, and a block is material prepared from that part. An image identifier points to one digital file. None of these can stand in for the others. A staging encounter can also refer to a different tumor or an earlier episode of care.

PD-L1 is a protein measured by immunohistochemistry. Here the report gives a tumor proportion score: the percentage of the tumor cells assessed that show the staining. Keep the assay, specimen and block attached to that score. Sharing a patient number is not a reason to combine it with a genomic result from a different specimen.
