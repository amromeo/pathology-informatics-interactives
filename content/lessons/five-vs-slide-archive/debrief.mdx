## Debrief: keep each finding attached to the right tumor

### What happened in this case

TB-01 needs a block-link correction: the image label identifies A2, while the view says A1. TB-02 is already linked to the current lung specimen and should remain unchanged. TB-03 contains a documented transposition of the patient identifier. TB-04 belongs to a prior colon specimen, and TB-05 contains a stage from that earlier episode. Keep those historical records separate while obtaining current findings.

The record repairs address veracity. They do not increase storage or throughput. The workload test addresses volume and velocity; it must run peak scanning alongside clinical retrieval. Variety requires preserving the meaning and provenance of each data type. Value depends on answering the board's current clinical question.

A tumor-board view brings together information recorded for different purposes. Staff may link an image to a block, a genomic report to a specimen and a stage to a clinical encounter. All three records can contain the correct patient identifier while referring to different tumors or episodes of care.

Trace one finding from the combined view back to its source. For an image, inspect the slide label, block and accession. For a genomic result, inspect the submitted specimen and report date. Ask the clinical team which encounter supplied the stage. Preserve a correctly linked current result while correcting a different link.

**At tumor board:** Which source record would you open first if the genomic finding seemed inconsistent with the slide under review?

### Who does what

The LIS analyst and archive team investigate the import and image links. The pathologist confirms specimen relationships and report meaning. The clinical team confirms the current staging encounter. Research staff use approved copies and document selection criteria. Record every correction rather than silently changing source identifiers.
