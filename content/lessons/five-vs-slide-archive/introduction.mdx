# Five Vs in the Slide Archive

You are the pathology resident helping Dr. Rao get ready for the lung cancer tumor board. A new combined view puts whole-slide images, a PD-L1 staining result, a genomic report and the stage from the electronic health record (EHR) on one screen. While checking it, the team finds records that belong to the right patient but to the wrong specimen or the wrong episode of care.

Each item comes from a different place. The laboratory information system (LIS) records the case, part and block. The slide archive stores the image files and their identifiers. An outside genomic laboratory sends its reports. The clinical team records the stage in the EHR. Before the board uses the combined view, every item has to stay linked to the right specimen.

The archive also has to cope with its size. It takes in about 1.9 petabytes of images a year, most of them scanned overnight, while pathologists and clinicians open slides during the day.

## Terms used in this case

- **The five Vs:** a common way to describe a large body of data. **Volume** is how much there is; **velocity** is how fast it arrives and has to be delivered; **variety** is how many different kinds there are; **veracity** is whether it can be trusted; **value** is whether it answers the question being asked.
- **Patient, case, part and block:** the patient identifier links everything for one person. The case (accession) is one set of specimens sent to the laboratory; a part is one specimen in that case; a block is material prepared from a part.
- **Encounter:** one episode of care in the EHR, such as the visit when a cancer was staged.
- **PD-L1 tumor proportion score:** the percentage of tumor cells that stain for the PD-L1 protein. It belongs to one specimen and block.

## Your task

Match five facts about the archive to the five Vs. Then check five records from the tumor-board view against their sources and choose what to do with each one. Finally, choose how to test the archive. You can open the debrief at any time.

## What you will be able to do

- Use the five Vs to describe what this archive has to handle.
- Tell patient, specimen, block and encounter links apart.
- Fix the wrong links without disturbing a link that is right.
- Choose a test that runs overnight scanning and clinical viewing at the same time.
