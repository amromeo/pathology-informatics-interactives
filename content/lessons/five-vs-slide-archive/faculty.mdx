# Faculty guide: Five Vs in the Slide Archive

## At a glance

Allow 30 minutes, alone or in a small group. PIER 2.3: apply the five Vs, connect pathology data sources and review use of a combined dataset. Project the five records before displaying feedback.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Activity | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Establish the tumor-board question | Which tumor and clinical episode are being discussed? |
| 5–12 | Match the five archive observations | Which problem would more storage actually solve? |
| 12–23 | Review record links | What independent source supports each correction? |
| 23–30 | Choose a workload test and debrief | What must remain correct while the scanners are busy? |

## Expected answers

Archive observations map to volume, velocity, variety, veracity and value in order. Record actions: TB-01 block; TB-02 keep; TB-03 patient; TB-04 specimen; TB-05 case/encounter. Choose the peak-traffic test with concurrent clinical retrieval. A blanket hold does not repair the valid TB-02 link or establish a working service.

The rounded annual rate is 1.9 × 10^15 bytes / (365 × 24 × 3600 seconds), about 60 MB/s. The overnight peak is about 500 MB/s. Neither figure includes all storage copies. The missing-link proportion is 2,160 / 120,000 = 1.8%.

## Common misconceptions

- A patient match proves the specimen match. Ask which cancer and collection the finding belongs to.
- An image identifier names the block. Ask where the file-to-block association was recorded.
- Enough disk guarantees responsive retrieval. Ask what happens during simultaneous scanning.
- Every mismatch should be fixed by changing the source label. Ask what evidence authorizes the correction.

## Discussion prompts

1. When should the view show a missing finding? A strong answer includes keeping unresolved records separate and naming who will resolve them.
2. Which performance measure matters to a pathologist opening a slide? A strong answer includes the first field of view and concurrent clinical work.
3. What must accompany a research copy? A strong answer includes access controls, provenance, versions and selection criteria.

## Group variant

Assign a pathologist, LIS analyst, archive administrator and tumor-board clinician. Each explains the evidence their system can supply before the group agrees on corrections.

## Assess transfer

Ask the resident to identify a different example where the right patient's record contains evidence from the wrong specimen or episode.

Explain why archive capacity alone does not establish that clinical retrieval will remain usable during scanning. Have residents support their answer with a specific record or result from this lesson before comparing it with the slides.
