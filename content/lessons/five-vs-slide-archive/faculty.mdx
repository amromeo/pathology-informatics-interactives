# Faculty guide: Five Vs in the Slide Archive

Ask learners to connect every proposed component to a specific workload. Challenge solutions that merely add storage or compression without addressing peak flow, linkage, retrieval targets, and governance.

## Preparing discussion with API slides

Use [Association for Pathology Informatics teaching slides: Digital Imaging in Pathology (PowerPoint)](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/assets/lms/jimharrison/PathInformatics%205%20Digital%20Imaging%20slides.pptx) by James H. Harrison, Jr., slides 13–16, 23, to prepare the discussion after residents have explained their case decision. Ask: Follow how an image pyramid supplies only the part of an image being viewed. Explain why archive capacity alone does not establish that clinical retrieval will remain usable during scanning. Have residents support their answer with a specific record or result from this lesson before comparing it with the slides.

These slides provide teaching background. Use the lesson's primary sources and current local policies for clinical criteria, legal requirements, and approval decisions; do not turn a slide example or numerical recommendation into a local requirement. If the PowerPoint link changes, locate the session on the [API teaching-slide page](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/teaching-slide-sets). The lesson and discussion remain usable without the external file.
