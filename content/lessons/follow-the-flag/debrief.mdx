## Debrief: distinguish message receipt from faithful result meaning

### What happened in this case


FL-01 retained its value, unit, and final status, but CH reached a display table
that did not recognize it. Translating CH to HH restores critical-high meaning.
FL-02 tests the independent CL-to-LL mapping. Mapping CH to H makes the code
recognizable while still losing severity.

The unchanged configuration matches four of six selected category outcomes.
Both critical translations yield six of six, provided unknown XH still enters
review. Converting unknown codes to normal creates a new failure even after
the reported result is repaired.

The laboratory remains responsible for the complete report and its verified
appearance in the EHR. The analyst changes the interface; clinical and
laboratory staff approve interpretation and assess already affected reports.
The documented call and the electronic display are separate records of work.

The next lesson applies the same distinction between arrival and meaning to a
newborn-screening report. There, the receiving program must recognize both
which observation is reported and what its answer means. Repairing a flag
mapping does not show those separate observation and answer mappings.

### Who does what

Work with an interface analyst. The person making a technical change should document the test evidence; the responsible clinical or operational lead approves the intended use.

### Connection to PIER 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 8.1

The case asks you to distinguish message receipt from faithful result meaning using laboratory records and explain the consequence of your choice.
