## Debrief: distinguish message receipt from faithful result meaning

### What happened in this case

FL-01 kept its value, unit and final status, but its CH flag reached an EHR table that did not recognize it. Translating CH to HH brings back the critical-high meaning. FL-02 tests the separate CL-to-LL translation. Translating CH to H makes the code recognizable but still drops the word "critical."

The original settings match four of the six flag categories. Translating both critical flags gives six of six, as long as the unknown XH flag still goes for review. Turning unknown flags into normal creates a new failure, even once the critical results are fixed.

The laboratory is still responsible for the complete report and for checking how it appears in the EHR. The analyst changes the interface; laboratory and clinical staff approve what the flags mean and look at the reports already affected. The phone call and the flag on the screen are two separate records of the laboratory's work.

The next lesson applies the same idea, that arriving is not the same as arriving with the right meaning, to a newborn screening report. There, the public health program has to recognize both which result is being reported and what the answer means. Fixing a flag translation does not show that either of those is right.

<summary>A closer look: carry the flag’s severity through the whole report</summary>

A result can keep its number and unit and still lose the severity its flag carried. The analyst has to compare what the sending laboratory meant with what the receiving screen shows. Recognizing the code is only part of that.

For CH, the screen has to keep the critical-high meaning. Translating it to H keeps "high" but loses "critical." Check the critical-low translation on its own, and send an unfamiliar code for review instead of calling it normal.

The same check should cover comments, status and reference information in the complete report. Different screens in the chart can show different parts of the message, so open the screen clinicians actually use to read the official laboratory report.

**When documenting the fix:** What would you compare on the sending and receiving reports to show that the critical flag came through?

### Who does what

The interface analyst changes the translation and records the test results. Laboratory and EHR staff check the report clinicians see. The laboratory director approves what the flags mean and decides what follow-up the affected reports need.
