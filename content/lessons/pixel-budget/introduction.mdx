# Choosing Image Settings for a Pathology Service

You are the pathology resident on the digital pathology committee.

Dr. Rao, a surgical pathologist, asks you to review an archive proposal with Elena Park, the digital pathology technologist, and Malik from infrastructure. The service scans fixed-tissue hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) slides for hospital reporting. Malik proposes smaller files because the storage forecast exceeds the current allocation. Elena Park also reports locally blurred tissue in a few scans.

Your question is whether the proposed savings preserve information needed for the intended use. Compare the same tissue field after two different changes. Then estimate sampling, compression, focal-plane, and storage-copy tradeoffs. Recommend the evidence needed before changing the service's approved settings.

The comparison uses a small H&E-style field, not a whole-slide acquisition. It demonstrates visible raster-image changes; it cannot confirm diagnostic equivalence, scanner resolution, a physical tissue scale, or an acceptable clinical compression ratio.


## What you will be able to do

- Relate image detail to storage and delivery.
- Check the intended viewing task.
- Explain why “choose the smallest file regardless of detail” is an incomplete approach.

Start by reading the case records. Make a choice before checking the explanation; you can revise it and test again.
