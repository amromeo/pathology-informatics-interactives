## Separate acquisition from enlargement

A scanner samples light from a slide on a grid. **Pixel spacing** is the physical distance represented by adjacent pixels. Smaller spacing gives more samples across the same tissue area. Optical resolution also depends on optics, focus, preparation, and sensor; spacing alone does not show the smallest resolvable structure.

A scanner's “20×” or “40×” label describes an acquisition setting. Viewer zoom describes enlargement on a screen. They are not interchangeable measurements. Enlarging an image does not acquire new detail. Additional focal planes may help with thick material but add acquisition time and data; they do not make every preparation suitable for digital diagnosis.

**Downsampling** replaces a larger grid with a smaller one. **Lossy compression** discards information to encode fewer bytes. **Lossless compression** permits exact recovery of the pixels supplied to that encoder. Saving an already blurred or lossy image losslessly does not restore earlier information.

### Relating sampling to familiar magnification labels

About 0.5 micrometers per pixel is commonly called 20× equivalent; 0.25 micrometers per pixel is commonly called 40× equivalent. These are useful comparisons, not a guarantee of identical optical information. A multiresolution image pyramid adds roughly one third to the uncompressed base-level size when each level halves both dimensions. The calculator's base size does not include every pyramid, metadata or filesystem cost.
