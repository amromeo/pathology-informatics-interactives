# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet a digital pathology technologist.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Check the intended viewing task.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Estimate a scan workload from dimensions, channels and bit depth, then compare measured file sizes.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: estimate a scan workload from dimensions, channels and bit depth, then compare measured file sizes. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “choose the smallest file regardless of detail” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum

With digital pathology and infrastructure staff, trace one approved training slide from accession and barcode to scan, archive, retrieval, viewer, and reporting workstation. Record spacing, compression, focal planes, encoded size, time to first image, and the authoritative slide identity. Keep identifiers out of your notes.

Compare a month's actual volume and file sizes with this estimate. Add pyramids, rescans, copies, retention, growth, and recovery. Ask who approves a setting change and how restored images are checked for identity, completeness, and readability. Do not alter a clinical archive for this exercise.
