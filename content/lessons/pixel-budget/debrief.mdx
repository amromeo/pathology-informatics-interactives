## Debrief: relate image detail to storage and delivery

### What happened in this case


At 0.5 µm/pixel, one plane contains 600 million pixels and 1.8 GB before compression. At 0.25 µm/pixel it contains 2.4 billion pixels and 7.2 GB. Three planes triple those amounts. At a modeled 10:1 ratio and two copies, one year at 0.25 µm/pixel requires 180 TB for one plane. None of these calculations shows an acceptable diagnostic setting.

Downsampling and strong compression each change displayed detail. Neither fixes the blurred source region. A lossless copy preserves the pixels it receives; it cannot reverse earlier loss. Cropping can preserve detail within its borders while omitting the rest of the specimen.

<summary>A closer look: distinguish sampling from enlargement</summary>

A scanner records tissue on a grid. Smaller pixel spacing gives more samples across the same area. The optics, focus and preparation also affect the detail recorded. Enlarging the displayed image changes its size on screen without collecting additional information from the slide.

A whole-slide file often contains smaller versions of the image as well as the largest one. The viewer can request tiles from a level suited to the displayed area and zoom. Those additional levels and the file’s metadata contribute to its stored size.

Compression, focal planes, extra copies and retrieval needs add further questions to the service’s storage estimate. Measure representative files and viewing performance before using the estimate to choose a system.
