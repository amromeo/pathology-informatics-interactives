## Debrief: relate image detail to storage and delivery

### What happened in this case


At 0.5 µm/pixel, one plane contains 600 million pixels and 1.8 GB before compression. At 0.25 µm/pixel it contains 2.4 billion pixels and 7.2 GB. Three planes triple those amounts. At a modeled 10:1 ratio and two copies, one year at 0.25 µm/pixel requires 180 TB for one plane. None of these calculations tells you which setting is good enough for diagnosis.

Downsampling and heavy compression both change the detail you see. Neither fixes the blurred patch in the original scan. A lossless copy keeps exactly the pixels it is given; it cannot undo earlier loss. Cropping can keep the detail inside its borders while leaving out the rest of the specimen.

Elena Park checks preparation, focus, whether all the tissue was scanned, slide identity and rescans. Malik measures real file sizes, how long images take to open, how long they must be kept and how they would be recovered. Dr. Rao and the director decide what pathologists need to be able to see, and review how the whole proposed system performs on typical cases. How long images are kept, and replacing the archive, need the hospital's own review and any regulatory review that applies.

The next lesson looks at clinical validation. A patch of tissue that looks similar on this page does not approve a scanner, a compression setting, a viewer, a screen or any diagnostic use.

<summary>A closer look: distinguish sampling from enlargement</summary>

A scanner records tissue on a grid. Smaller pixel spacing gives more samples across the same area. The lenses, focus and preparation also affect the detail recorded. Enlarging the image on screen makes it bigger without collecting any more information from the slide.

A whole-slide file often holds smaller versions of the image as well as the full-size one. The viewer can ask for tiles from whichever level suits the area and zoom on screen. Those extra levels, and the information stored about the file, add to its size.

Compression, focal planes, extra copies and how fast images must open all affect the storage estimate. Measure typical files and how quickly they open before using the estimate to choose a system.
