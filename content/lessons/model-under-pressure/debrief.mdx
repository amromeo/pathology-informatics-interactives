## Debrief: verify the model in its intended setting

### What happened in this case

The four proposals are a programmed rule, supervised image detector, unsupervised clustering and generative summary. Their first reviews differ: boundaries and exceptions, independent local performance, clinical meaning of clusters, and agreement with source records.

The detector's initial local gap is a generalizability failure. Scanner A detects 23/25 positive slides; scanner B detects 19/25. Across scanners, micrometastasis detection is 17/25 while macrometastasis detection is 25/25. The latter result does not justify unreviewed restriction to a new intended use. September's decline from 17/25 to 12/25 micrometastases requires investigation and the agreed response to a safety threshold.

The prevalence slider changes positive predictive value because false positives come from the much larger negative population when disease is uncommon. In this model sensitivity stays fixed when only prevalence changes. Increasing separation improves both sensitivity and specificity. Those modeled changes do not rewrite the study data.

### Who does what

The laboratory director defines the intended use and acceptance criteria. Pathologists confirm independent reference diagnoses and the human review workflow. Analysts retain model, scanner and preparation versions. The regulatory team checks device status and scope. Clinical staff agree on reporting, escalation, pause criteria and patient follow-up after a detected failure.

### Connection to PIER 2.4

The learner distinguishes model categories, interprets local and longitudinal performance, and assigns the clinical and organizational reviews needed before use.
