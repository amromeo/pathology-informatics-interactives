### Each proposal needs its own check

The programmed rule needs tests at its limits and for exceptions. The image detector needs testing on local material with independent diagnoses. The gene-expression clusters need an explanation of what the groups mean. The generated summary needs comparison with the source reports, including anything important it left out.

### Before the tool is used for patient care

The laboratory director decides what the tool will be used for and what performance is acceptable. Pathologists decide how they will check its marks and review areas it leaves unmarked. Analysts keep a record of the model version and the scanner and stain settings.

The hospital's regulatory team checks whether the proposed use fits the product's current device status. The laboratory and treating clinicians also need to agree on:

- What will appear in the report.
- Whom staff call when the tool appears to be wrong.
- When use must stop and who can stop it.
- How to review affected patients if a problem is found.

When you lowered prevalence with the slider, positive predictive value fell even though sensitivity stayed fixed. More of the positive calls came from negative cases. That is why the laboratory needs to know both what the detector misses and what it marks incorrectly.
