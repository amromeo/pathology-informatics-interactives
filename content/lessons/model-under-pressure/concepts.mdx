### How was the tool made?

A programmer can write a rule that holds a result when quality control fails. The programmer supplies the conditions directly. Test the limits and exceptions to find out whether the rule does what the laboratory asked.

Other programs learn patterns from examples. This is **machine learning**, one group of methods within **artificial intelligence (AI)**. AI includes computer methods for tasks such as recognizing images and generating language.

The examples used for training make a difference:

- **Supervised learning:** pathologists label the training slides, and the model learns patterns associated with those labels. The metastasis detector uses a **convolutional neural network**, which learns patterns in neighboring parts of an image. Test it on local slides with diagnoses made independently of its marks.
- **Unsupervised learning:** the program groups similar measurements without being given diagnoses. Tumors with similar gene-expression patterns may cluster together. Someone still has to find out what those groups mean and whether staining, preparation or other differences explain them.
- **Generative models:** the program produces text or images. A tumor-board summary may read well while leaving out a qualification or adding a finding that is absent from the reports. Compare its statements with the reports before using it.

The name of the method helps you understand how it works. The first check still depends on the job: holding a result, finding a lesion, grouping tumors or summarizing a record.
