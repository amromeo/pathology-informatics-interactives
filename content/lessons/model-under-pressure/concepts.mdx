### Start with the task and the training data

Artificial intelligence describes computer methods used for tasks such as recognizing images or generating language. Machine learning fits parameters from examples. An explicit if/then laboratory rule is programmed directly; its behavior still needs validation, but it does not learn from cases.

In **supervised learning**, training examples have labels, such as a pathologist's annotation of a metastasis. A convolutional neural network learns spatial patterns in image neighborhoods. **Unsupervised learning** groups or represents observations without supplied outcome labels; a molecular cluster still needs biological and clinical interpretation. A **generative model** produces new text or images. A fluent summary can contain unsupported statements and must be checked against the source reports.

Choose the review according to the actual proposal. Test programmed boundaries and exceptions. Verify a detector on representative local material with independent reference labels. Investigate what clusters mean. Check generated assertions and omissions against the underlying record.
