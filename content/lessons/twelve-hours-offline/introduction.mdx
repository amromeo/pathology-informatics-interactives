# Twelve Hours Offline

> **Story so far:** North River University Hospital installed its laboratory middleware server before the network had been separated and before anyone had practiced restoring it from backup. The server has only one network connection.

## Case setup

North River University Hospital is a Level I trauma center. On April 18, 2026, construction severs the hospital's primary network at 06:20. For the next twelve hours, orders, registration messages, results and patient identifiers stop moving between the laboratory and the rest of the hospital's systems.

The middleware server loses its only working network connection. Staff have never restored it from backup or practiced recovering from an outage. The unfinished work did not cause the cable damage, but it leaves the team less prepared to recover.

Network separation limits which computers can connect to each other. An alternate network route provides another way for messages to travel. A restricted network boundary would not repair the severed cable. What this outage needs is a working connection and a check of all the work done while it was down. Nothing in the case says the server's stored data were destroyed.

Dr. Maya Chen is working with Jordan Ellis, a laboratory information system (LIS) analyst, the core laboratory and nursing units. Dr. Elena Ruiz is the laboratory medical director. They need to keep testing on paper, phone critical results and keep each specimen linked to the right patient. When the network comes back at 18:20, they have to match the paper work to the electronic orders and results.

## Terms used in this case

- **Middleware:** software that routes instrument messages to the LIS and holds messages waiting for delivery.
- **Temporary patient identifier:** a number assigned during downtime to connect a patient with their orders and specimens. Registration records who received each number.
- **Medical record number (MRN):** the identifier for a patient's hospital chart. Staff must verify which MRN belongs to each temporary identifier.
- **Paper accession number:** the laboratory's identifier for a specimen and its testing. It is separate from the patient identifier.
- **Reconciliation:** comparing paper and electronic records so that every order, result and call ends up in the right chart.
- **Back-entry:** entering earlier paper work into the restored system. Its entry time is recorded separately from specimen collection time.
- **Recovery time objective (RTO):** the longest a service is planned to be down before the effect becomes unacceptable.
- **Recovery point objective (RPO):** how much recent data the hospital is prepared to lose, measured as time back from the moment of failure.
- **Backup and standby:** a backup is a saved copy of the data, used to restore it. A standby is a second system kept ready to take over.

## Your task

Choose how the laboratory starts downtime, works through the day and goes back to electronic records. Your early choices change which records you have during recovery. Then match six paper records to patient charts and decide how to deal with the problems that are left.

At 06:20 the incident board shows 17 trauma patients being treated and 9 critical tests waiting; the first number counts people, the second counts tests. The six records you will match are examples picked for this exercise, not every test done that day. Your choices about identifiers and the call log change those six records. The other choices teach, through their feedback, how to coordinate the work.

## What you will be able to do

- Choose how staff assign temporary identifiers and record paper orders and critical-result calls.
- Match a downtime specimen to the correct patient chart.
- Sort out a duplicate order, a missing critical result and a wrong collection time before routine work restarts.
- Explain what Dr. Ruiz needs checked before she approves going back to normal testing.
