# Walk one specimen through your downtime procedure

## Who to meet

Meet the downtime coordinator, bench supervisor and an LIS analyst who helps reconcile records after an outage.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes. Ask them to have the current downtime forms and recovery procedure available.

## Steps

1. Find when the procedure was last used or practiced. Ask who declares downtime and tells registration and the clinical units.
2. Follow a specimen through the blank forms. Find where staff assign the temporary patient identifier and paper accession number.
3. Have the supervisor show how a critical result and read-back would be recorded. Identify how that call would be linked to the later chart entry.
4. Ask the analyst to show how staff would match the paper record to the patient and order when systems return. Include a pending order for a test already completed on paper.
5. Find the local limits for service interruption and possible data loss. Ask who checks the recovered records and who can approve routine work restarting.

## Bring back

Prepare a one-page example using the blank forms. Mark where the temporary identifier, accession number, collection time and call record would be entered. Add the job titles of the people who match the chart and approve restarting. List one missing instruction you found, or one step you needed someone to explain.

## Discuss with faculty

The worklist says Pending, but the bench has a completed paper result. Show which records you would compare before asking for another specimen. How would you preserve the original collection time?

## Privacy

Use blank forms for your notes. Review any actual downtime records only with your supervisor in approved hospital systems. Leave patient identifiers and confidential incident details there.
