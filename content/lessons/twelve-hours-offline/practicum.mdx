# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet the downtime coordinator and bench supervisor.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Locate the downtime reconciliation procedure.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Walk through one downtime specimen and record recovery time, acceptable data loss and reconciliation steps.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: walk through one downtime specimen and record recovery time, acceptable data loss and reconciliation steps. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “use the latest backup time as proof of recovery” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum

With a supervisor, review your laboratory's current downtime procedure.

1. Record the date when the procedure was last exercised in a drill or used during an actual interruption.
2. Identify who declares laboratory downtime and who coordinates with registration, clinical units, the LIS team, and hospital IT.
3. Determine how temporary patient identifiers and accessions are assigned and how staff prevent two identifiers from being created for the same patient.
4. Find the required method for documenting a phoned critical result and its read-back.
5. Identify who owns reconciliation after restoration and what must be checked before normal electronic workflows resume.

Do not copy patient identifiers, results, credentials, or confidential incident details into teaching notes. Follow local policy and perform the review under appropriate supervision.
