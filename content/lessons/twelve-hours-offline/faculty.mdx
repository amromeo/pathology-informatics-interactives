# Faculty guide: Twelve Hours Offline

## At a glance

Allow 30 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: reconcile downtime work before routine release resumes; locate the downtime reconciliation procedure; justify the first local check. PIER: 3.1, 3.3, 4.5.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Activity | Ask the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6 | Read the case and records | What decision has the laboratory been asked to make? |
| 6–21 | Make, test and revise a choice | Which record supports your decision? What could fail? |
| 21–30 | Debrief and retrieval questions | How would you verify this in your own laboratory? |

## Expected answers


Run the exercise as a tabletop. Reveal evidence in stages and require learners to name an owner, communication channel, documentation method, and failure check for each decision.

The most important transition is recovery: every temporary identity, order, result, critical-value call, and repeat test must be accounted for.

Run the controlled path first: controlled identifiers and incident command, a numbered call log and central accession log, then staged recovery and comparison before recollection. The six paper records match six established charts. There are three recovery hazards: the duplicate order for DT-204, missing critical result for DT-202, and collection/entry timestamp conflict for DT-205. A complete check resolves nine items: six identity matches and three recovery actions.

Then change the identifier and communication decisions. Separate unit identifiers add chart MRN-490118 for the patient already represented by MRN-410118; they do not add a seventh paper record. Unit-specific communication removes call-log entry CL-02 for DT-202. Ask learners to distinguish restoring a documented critical result from investigating a call that cannot be verified in the laboratory log. Do not invent a read-back record during recovery.

Have learners deliberately leave one identifier unmatched or accept the duplicate order, check reconciliation, and choose the preferred resumption condition. Approval must still be withheld while an item remains unresolved. Changing an earlier tabletop decision clears the reconciliation selections and approval choice because the records and required actions may change.

Close with the learner's actual path: which early decision created extra recovery work, which source record supports each correction, and who must review unresolved discrepancies. IT restores the service; laboratory, registration, and LIS staff reconcile the episode together, with medical leadership approving resumption under local procedures. Discuss how the team preserves original collection times, documents later entry, and avoids unnecessary recollection.

These slides provide teaching background. Use the lesson's primary sources and current local policies for clinical criteria, legal requirements, and approval decisions; do not turn a slide example or numerical recommendation into a local requirement. ## Common misconceptions

- “Confirm that the login screen appears.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? Restored connectivity does not resolve paper orders, duplicate identities or results already communicated. Reconcile them using the approved recovery process.
- “Enter all paper results as new orders.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? Restored connectivity does not resolve paper orders, duplicate identities or results already communicated. Reconcile them using the approved recovery process.
- “Use the latest backup time as proof of recovery.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? Identify how the team tracks unresolved work, verifies entries and prevents duplicate orders or reports.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: reconcile downtime work before routine release resumes.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: locate the downtime reconciliation procedure, because identify how the team tracks unresolved work, verifies entries and prevents duplicate orders or reports.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes the downtime coordinator and bench supervisor and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Walk through one downtime specimen and record recovery time, acceptable data loss and reconciliation steps. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.
