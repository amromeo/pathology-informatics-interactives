# Faculty guide: Twelve Hours Offline

## At a glance

Allow 30 minutes. Run this as a discussion of what staff would do through the outage. Have residents name the person and record for each action. The lesson covers PIER 3.1, 3.3 and 4.5.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Activity | Ask the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Read the outage and follow the work moving to paper | How will the specimen stay linked to the patient? |
| 5–13 | Choose actions in Activate, Operate and Recover | What record will the next shift need? |
| 13–23 | Match the six patients and choose recovery actions | Has this pending order already been tested? |
| 23–30 | Change an earlier choice and discuss restarting | Which additional check does that choice create? |

## Expected answers

Begin by having the resident explain how registration's identity index, accessioning's paper log, bench results and the restored worklist were combined. Work through DT-201: Amina Yusuf, birth date 1958-09-22, maps to MRN-410052. Names alone cannot resolve the two Liam Brooks charts; registration confirms the existing MRN before entry.

First use one numbered identifier block, a named laboratory incident lead, a numbered call log and a central accession log. On recovery, return to electronic work in stages and compare completed paper tests with pending electronic orders before recollection.

On this path, the six paper records match six existing charts. Three further actions make **nine checks** in all:

- **DT-204:** compare ORD-R-8821, entered by laboratory staff at 18:28, with ORD-R-8840, entered by Cardiac ICU staff at 18:32. Both name paper accession D26-0418-004 and the 09:44 specimen. The nurse's 18:46 confirmation identifies the second order as another request for that same testing. Enter the verified result on ORD-R-8821 and cancel ORD-R-8840. Review a separate request for a later specimen individually.
- **DT-202:** enter the original critical potassium result and link call-log entry CL-02.
- **DT-205:** preserve 11:06 as collection time and 18:48 as entry time.

Then change the identifier choice. Separate unit identifiers add chart MRN-490118 for Liam Brooks, who already has MRN-410118. There are still six paper records. Liam's match is disabled until the resident chooses the hold-and-confirm action. That choice immediately displays registration's comparison of the pre-outage encounter and downtime forms. It identifies MRN-410118 as the existing chart and flags MRN-490118 for correction. Have the resident follow the return link and select the confirmed MRN. A guessed match cannot earn credit before that confirmation. Changing the registration action clears the match and removes the response.

Changing the critical-result choice removes CL-02. The unit reports a call, but the laboratory has no matching record. Residents should choose verification with the clinical team and referral of the missing record for follow-up. They should not invent a read-back.

Separate identifiers increase the total from nine to ten checks. The call-log choice changes an action without changing that total. The other four phase choices provide feedback about coordination but do not alter this selected record set. Preserve the fixed 18-patient privacy export in the next lesson; it does not depend on this exercise's choices.

The cable damage causes the outage. Segmentation limits network access, an alternate route supplies another connection, and a restore drill verifies recovery procedures. The case does not say stored data were destroyed or require staff to restore an older backup over intact records. Use the example two-hour RTO and 15-minute RPO to distinguish planned recovery targets from the observed twelve-hour interruption.

Leave one patient unmatched or choose recollection for DT-204. Even with the best approval condition selected, the laboratory should not resume normal work. Changing an earlier choice clears the recovery selections because the available records and required actions may change.

## Common misconceptions

- **“We can log in, so downtime is over.”** Ask: where are the paper results in the chart?
- **“Every pending order needs a specimen.”** Ask: what has already been tested for DT-204, and what do the two orders actually request?
- **“A paper result makes the electronic order a duplicate.”** Ask: which order should receive the result, and which second request was confirmed as redundant?
- **“Either Liam Brooks chart will do.”** Ask the resident to obtain registration's response before selecting an MRN.
- **“We should use the time the result was entered.”** Ask: would 18:48 describe when Grace Kim's blood was collected?
- **“The nurse remembers a call, so we can mark read-back complete.”** Ask: which record confirms the recipient, time and read-back?
- **“The duplicate order and duplicate chart are the same problem.”** Ask: which repair prevents another collection, and which keeps all results in one patient's chart?

## Discussion prompts

1. Which early choice created more recovery work? *A strong answer:* separate unit identifiers created a second chart for Liam Brooks. Unit-specific call notes left the laboratory unable to verify the potassium call from its own log.
2. Why should the team compare records before recollecting DT-204? *A strong answer:* both electronic orders request the already tested 09:44 specimen, and the nurse confirms the second request repeats the downtime test. Completing ORD-R-8821 preserves the result; cancelling ORD-R-8840 removes the extra collection request.
3. What does Dr. Ruiz need before agreeing to restart? *A strong answer:* completed patient matches and checks of orders, results and calls, including repeats and timestamps. The laboratory and LIS team should identify any problem still needing follow-up.

## Group variant

Assign registration, bench technologist, nurse, LIS analyst and Dr. Ruiz. Have each person say what record they need from the previous person. After recovery, ask the nurse whether another collection is needed and ask Dr. Ruiz what remains to be checked.

## Assess transfer

Give the resident the local blank downtime forms. Ask them to show where one specimen's collection time, critical call and later chart match would be recorded. Look for a named person who performs each check.

## Using the API teaching slides

[NIST SP 800-34 Revision 1, updated November 2010](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/34/r1/upd1/final) explains contingency planning, recovery objectives, backups and alternate processing arrangements. Use it as background for the worked backup example; local downtime procedures define the hospital's actual targets and restart checks.

After residents explain their recovery choices, use James H. Harrison, Jr.'s [System Implementation and Management](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/assets/lms/jimharrison/PathInformatics%209%20Impl-Mgmt%20slides.pptx), slides 11–12 and 25. Ask which paper records still need checking after stored data are restored. Use the hospital's current downtime procedure for local practice. If the link moves, find the session on the [API teaching-slide page](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/teaching-slide-sets).
