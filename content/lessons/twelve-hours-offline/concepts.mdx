### Keep a record staff can match later

When registration cannot supply the usual medical record number, staff need another way to label the patient and specimen. A **temporary identifier** serves that purpose during downtime. Registration records each assignment in a shared index so staff can later find the correct chart.

Accessioning records the specimen when it arrives and gives it a paper accession number. The paper accession log connects the order and specimen to testing and repeats. A separate critical-result call log records who called, who received the result and when. It also records the recipient's read-back of the result.

When the systems return, staff compare these paper records with the electronic orders and results. This is **reconciliation**. A result collected earlier in the day keeps its original collection time; the time someone enters it later is recorded separately.

### How much time and work can the laboratory recover?

The laboratory first decides how long a service can be unavailable. That limit is its **recovery time objective (RTO)**. It also decides how much recent work could need recovery after a data loss. That interval is its **recovery point objective (RPO)**. Staff need records that let them identify and recheck work within that interval.

A backup copies data so staff can recover it later:

- A **full backup** copies the complete selected dataset.
- An **incremental backup** copies changes since the previous backup.
- A **differential backup** copies changes since the last full backup.

These methods require different sets of files during restoration. Ask staff to restore a backup and check the recovered records before relying on it during an outage.

### What is kept ready if the main system fails?

A **hot standby** is kept ready for rapid use. A **warm standby** needs more recovery steps. A **cold standby** needs more setup before staff can use it.

Copying data continuously to another system can also copy corruption. The laboratory still needs protected older backups. Rotating backup media and keeping an offsite copy help if one location is damaged. A downtime drill should test restoration, another network path and reconciliation of the work done on paper.

A **data warehouse** is a separate reporting copy used for analysis. Check when it was last updated and who can use it. It may be missing recent orders and results, so staff should not treat it as the current patient-care record.
