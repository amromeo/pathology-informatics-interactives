### The recovery record starts during activation

A temporary identifier is the controlled label used when the usual registration and medical record number are not available. It must be unique, written on the requisition and specimen, and recorded in an assignment index so staff can later connect the paper record to one verified patient chart.

During the interruption, the paper accession log records the order, specimen, testing, repeats, and result. A separate critical-result call log records who received a phoned result, when it was called, and whether the recipient read it back correctly. Both records are needed during recovery.

When electronic systems return, staff must preserve the time the specimen was actually collected. The later time when someone enters the paper result is a different event. Connectivity has been restored, but normal laboratory operation should not resume until identities, orders, results, calls, repeats, and timestamps have been reconciled.

### Recover the records as well as the connection

First define how long the service can be unavailable and how much recent work could be lost. These are the recovery time objective (RTO) and recovery point objective (RPO). The laboratory must be able to identify and reconcile any work inside the possible loss interval.

A full backup copies the complete selected dataset. An incremental backup copies changes since the previous backup; a differential copies changes since the last full backup. Restoration requirements differ. A backup is useful only if staff can restore it and verify the recovered records.

A hot standby is kept ready for rapid use; a warm standby needs additional recovery steps; a cold standby requires more setup. Replication can copy corruption, so it is not a substitute for protected backup history. Rotating media and an offsite copy reduce shared-site risks. Test restoration, alternate network paths and reconciliation under the planned downtime procedure.

Use governed reporting copies in a separate data warehouse for analysis. Document the extraction schedule, permitted users and missing recent records rather than treating the copy as the live patient-care system.
