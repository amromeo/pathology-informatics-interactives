## Debrief: put the paper work in the right charts

The network came back at 18:20, but the laboratory still had to account for all the testing done during the outage. The [six paper records](#downtime-records) needed patient matches. DT-201 matched Amina Yusuf's name and birth date to MRN-410052; the remaining rows required the same comparison. Three problems also needed attention:

- **DT-204:** the [two troponin orders](#recovery-duplicate-order-DT-204) refer to the same paper accession and 09:44 specimen. The nurse confirms that ORD-R-8840 repeats the downtime request. Enter the verified result on ORD-R-8821 and cancel ORD-R-8840. Any separately requested later specimen needs its own review.
- **DT-202:** the critical potassium result was absent from the chart. Verify the patient, enter the original result and link the call record.
- **DT-205:** the specimen was collected at 11:06 and the result entered at 18:48. Keep those times in their separate fields.

### Earlier choices change the recovery work

If registration issued identifiers from one numbered block, the team had one index to use for patient matching. If units created their own sequences, Liam Brooks had two charts. Choosing the hold-and-confirm action displayed registration's response: MRN-410118 is his existing chart, and MRN-490118 was opened as a duplicate during recovery. That response supplied the information needed to complete DT-202's match. Registration also flagged the duplicate chart for correction.

The numbered laboratory call log records the potassium call. If units kept only their own notes, the laboratory had no matching entry. The team then needed to verify the call with the clinical unit, including its recipient, time and read-back. Staff cannot fill in a read-back that nobody can confirm.

Registration checks the identity records and arranges correction of a duplicate chart. Laboratory and clinical staff compare the test requests to find out whether an order repeats testing that is already done. Each problem needs its own check, and a record of what was done.

With controlled identifiers there are nine checks: six patient matches plus three recovery actions. Separate unit identifiers add a duplicate-chart action, making ten. The call-log choice changes the action needed for DT-202, without changing the number of checks. The other four decisions teach how staff coordinate the response; they do not add records to the six used here.

The construction damage caused the interruption. A backup copy cannot carry messages across the broken route. Once connectivity returns, the team still has to compare the work on paper with the electronic records. A recovery drill should practice both restoring a usable service and completing that comparison.

### Before Dr. Ruiz approves the return

Hospital information technology (IT) staff restore the connection. Laboratory, registration and LIS staff compare the paper work with the electronic records. Dr. Ruiz needs to know which checks are finished and which problems still need attention before approving the return to normal work.

Choosing the best approval condition cannot make an unmatched paper record safe to enter. In this exercise, the score checks the actions you chose. In the laboratory, staff must carry out those actions and record the checks before the section resumes routine work.

The paper records also protect patients from unnecessary recollection. A pending status in the restored worklist may describe testing that was already completed during downtime. Check the specimen and order before sending someone back to the bedside.
