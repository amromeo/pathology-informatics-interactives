## Debrief

**Informatics principle:** High reliability depends on rehearsed degraded workflows and controlled recovery.

Your Activate and Operate choices determine what the recovery team can verify. A controlled identifier block provides one assignment index. Separate unit sequences add a duplicate-chart pair for the same patient. A numbered call log provides a record of the critical potassium communication; unit-specific notes leave no matching laboratory log entry and require verification and escalation.

The reconciliation exercise asks you to match six paper records to established charts, resolve a duplicate order before recollection, account for the missing critical result, and preserve the collection time rather than substituting the later entry time. If your identifier choice created a duplicate chart, that chart requires a separate hold and reconciliation action. A duplicate order and a duplicate patient chart are different problems.

Compare the unresolved items with your return-to-service decision. Selecting the preferred approval condition cannot make an incomplete reconciliation safe. Restored connectivity establishes that systems can communicate; it does not establish that each patient's orders, results, and critical-result communication are complete.

The exercise scores your selected recovery actions. In practice, staff must complete and document the verification or escalation those actions require before medical leadership approves resumption.

Incident command coordinates operations. Laboratory and clinical teams execute downtime procedures. IT restores services. LIS staff reconcile records. Medical leadership approves return to normal operation.
