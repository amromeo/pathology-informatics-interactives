# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet a section director and LIS build analyst.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Review the approved build specification.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Complete a change record and a validation matrix for a proposed test, including manual updates.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: complete a change record and a validation matrix for a proposed test, including manual updates. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “copy a similar test without a specification” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum: follow one test change

With faculty, choose a recently introduced or revised test. Trace the request to the approved specification and the exact deployed configuration. Identify who supplied each fact and who approved its clinical meaning.

Compare the order, specimen requirements, components, permitted values, units/reference information, comments, statuses, and codes across the laboratory procedure, LIS, and designated EHR report. Select one normal case and two exceptions that could expose a copied setting.

Review the change-control record, evidence of destination testing, procedures, training, downtime plan, and post-release checks. Prepare a short list of gaps with a proposed responsible person and verification method. Discuss the list with the laboratory team before proposing a production change.
