## Debrief: build the clinical meaning, then verify it

The starting report combined three targets into one answer and attached copied quantitative units. Successful transmission did not make that report clinically useful.

The repaired definition preserves three named qualitative components, withholds final values for pending tests, handles failed controls as invalid, and routes specimens outside the local procedure for review. It distinguishes a correction from a new report and a retransmission from another test.

The local specimen rule is part of this case's approved procedure. It is not a statement about every respiratory assay. Similarly, selecting “no units” is correct for this qualitative result, not a general rule for laboratory tests.

Eight passing checks establish the expected behavior of eight cases. They do not cover every patient identifier, timestamp, permission, interface, interruption, or downstream use. The readiness review adds clinical approval, destination verification, procedures/training, and controlled promotion with monitoring.

### Maintain the definition after go-live

Keep the request, approved specification, configuration version, test evidence, signoffs, and release record together. Record subsequent changes to names, codes, specimen requirements, comments, result values, and routing. Review which reports, procedures, and training materials each change affects.

A laboratory IT procedure manual should explain routine operation and exception handling, interfaces and reports, access control, quality checks, downtime and recovery, and change control. Assign a reviewer and review schedule. Retiring an old order also requires attention to saved order sets and historical report retrieval.

U.S. CLIA reporting requirements include reliable delivery to the final destination and appropriate report content and correction handling. Consult the current requirements and the laboratory's applicable accreditation checklist; this exercise is not a checklist compliance determination.
