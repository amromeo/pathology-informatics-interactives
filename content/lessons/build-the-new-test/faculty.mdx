# Faculty guide: Building and Validating a Laboratory Test

## At a glance

Allow 40 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: validate the complete report before releasing a build; review the approved build specification; justify the first local check. PIER: 8.1, 8.2, 8.4, 4.2.

## Outcomes and sequence

This lesson addresses PIER 8.1, 8.2, and 8.4 through order/report responsibility, test definition, validation and information maintenance. Allow 40 minutes: 5 for the request and report, 10 for the definition, 15 for testing, and 10 for the release discussion.

Ask learners to explain what “Detected copies/mL” implies before showing them the procedure. Require them to identify the missing organism identity and the unsupported concentration unit. They should distinguish the laboratory's report responsibility from the analyst's technical configuration work.

## Expected behavior

The initial configuration fails all eight result checks. Selecting separate components, invalid-run handling, qualitative reference information, pending status, the locally validated specimen, linked corrections, and accession/component/version matching passes all eight.

Correcting the result fields and units alone fixes routine positive, negative, and multiple-detection reports but leaves invalid, pending, specimen, correction, and retry problems. Use this partial success to ask why regression testing matters.

The preview is a result-section abstraction, not a complete EHR implementation. Ask learners to name fields and workflows outside its scope. Do not claim that it verifies analytical performance, terminology assignments, complete identity matching, or production interfaces.

The four readiness selections are elements of a recommendation. Learners cannot approve a real clinical release by checking them. Good explanations identify evidence, responsible people, and the exact version being promoted.

## Assess transfer

Ask how a changed reference comment, a corrected report, an interface outage, and an old order in a favorite list would affect the plan. A strong answer connects the test dictionary to reports, procedures, training, and downstream consumers.

Discuss manufacturer instructions, analytical verification, and local information-system testing as distinct sources of evidence. Do not invent a regulatory classification for the unnamed case assay. The specimen limitation is supplied by the local case procedure.

## Common misconceptions

- “Approve because the numeric result arrived.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? A technically delivered result is not a complete verified report. Correct the build and rerun affected and unaffected cases before clinical approval.
- “Remove the interval from the LIS too.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? A technically delivered result is not a complete verified report. Correct the build and rerun affected and unaffected cases before clinical approval.
- “Copy a similar test without a specification.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? Compare the requested assay, units, intervals, flags, comments and chart destination with the implemented report.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: validate the complete report before releasing a build.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: review the approved build specification, because compare the requested assay, units, intervals, flags, comments and chart destination with the implemented report.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes a section director and LIS build analyst and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Complete a change record and a validation matrix for a proposed test, including manual updates. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.
