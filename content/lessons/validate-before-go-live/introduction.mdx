# Reviewing a Whole-Slide Imaging Validation

You are the resident on the digital pathology team. Dr. Anita Shah, director of surgical pathology, has been asked to approve three things for next month: reporting fixed-tissue hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) slides on screen at the hospital, frozen-section consultation on screen, and reporting from home. Elena Park, the digital pathology technologist, put together the study log after trained pathologists read each case both on glass and on screen.

The packet holds a finished fixed-tissue comparison, an early frozen-section series, two fixed-tissue cases where the readings disagreed, and a list of ways of working that have not been tested. Decide what the evidence supports before the director signs. A good overall percentage cannot make up for a use that was never tested, a scanning problem nobody has fixed, or a reporting location nobody has tried.

## Terms used in this case

- **Intended use:** the specimens, purpose, readers, equipment, location and way of working that an approval covers.
- **Intraobserver concordance:** how often a pathologist's reading on screen agrees with the same pathologist's reading on glass.
- **Time between readings:** the gap, usually at least two weeks, between a pathologist's glass and screen readings, so they do not simply remember the case.
- **Discordance:** a case where the two readings disagree.

## Your task

Start with the actual study counts. Build a plan you could defend, look into the two disagreements, decide what the approval should cover, and sort later changes by how much checking they need. The image comparison in [Choosing Image Settings for a Pathology Service](../pixel-budget/) cannot take the place of this clinical evidence.

## What you will be able to do

- Read a validation packet and say which uses it covers and which it does not.
- Plan a glass-versus-screen comparison with suitable cases, readers and time between readings.
- Keep disagreements in the results while working out their cause.
- Decide how much checking a later change to the system needs.
