## Debrief: review validation evidence for each intended use

### What happened in this case

Fixed tissue has 58 agreements out of 60 paired cases, or 96.7%. Frozen sections have 14 out of 17, or 82.4%, with too short a gap between readings for the guideline and too few cases for a finished study. These denominators describe this packet; they are not the chance of a future error for a patient.

V12 needs a review of how the slide was scanned, and evidence that the problem was fixed. V44 needs a clinical look at why the two readings differed. Both stay in the original calculation. Agreement reached after the two readings are reconciled is not the same as the original agreement between a pathologist's own readings.

A reasonable recommendation is to consider approving fixed-tissue reporting at the hospital once the fix for V12 has been reviewed and signed off. Keep frozen-section consultation and home reporting on hold until there is proper evidence for them. Do not mistake a good plan for work that has actually been done. The exercise itself cannot approve anything.

Elena Park and the technical team look into scanning, slide identity, retrieval and versions. The pathologists in the study judge whether images can be read, how serious each disagreement is and what training is needed. The director approves the clinical scope with the relevant hospital teams. Ongoing quality review, a way back to glass and control of later changes are still needed after launch.
