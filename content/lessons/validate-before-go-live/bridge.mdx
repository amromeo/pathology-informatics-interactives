## Interpret the packet without deleting failures

The fixed-tissue set contains 60 cases: 20 gastrointestinal biopsies, 15 skin cases, 15 breast cases, and 10 resections from other organs. The selection includes small foci, low- and high-grade lesions, benign mimics, and difficult preparations. Rao read 30 and Chen read 30; each compared their own digital and glass interpretations after 14 days. No cases were excluded after reading. The table counts one paired interpretation per case, not slides or fields.

The exploratory frozen set contains 17 separate cases with an eight-day interval. It has three disagreements and is not a completed validation. Do not combine it with the fixed series to justify approval. Home workstations H1 and H2 and the remote network pathway have no completed study records.

The two fixed-tissue disagreements remain in the 60-case denominator after review. A rescan can test a correction, but the corrected image must not silently replace the original study result. Preserve the acquisition version, original reads, investigation, repeat checks, and approval decision.

## Separate device authorization from laboratory approval

In the United States, review the exact device's current labeling and regulatory status as well as laboratory accreditation requirements and local responsibilities. FDA's original DEN160056 decision illustrates a defined system and specimen scope: fixed paraffin-embedded surgical tissue, with frozen sections, cytology, and non-FFPE hematopathology outside that original indication. This historical example is not a claim about every current device.

Local validation does not expand a manufacturer's authorized claims. Device authorization does not show performance in every local workflow. Check the actual scanner, software, display configuration, location, and use; obtain institutional regulatory advice where the proposed use differs. Other jurisdictions have their own requirements.

After approval, retain versions, settings, users, training, quality records, and fallback instructions. A change affecting image rendering or acquisition needs impact review and appropriate verification or revalidation. A new clinical application requires more than repeating a login check.

### Remote review and the tested workstation

CMS memo QSO-23-15-CLIA, revised October 14, 2026, continues conditional enforcement discretion for remote review of digital laboratory materials under the primary laboratory's CLIA certificate, including review from a private residence. The primary director remains responsible, reports identify the remote site, and the memo's other conditions apply. Remote digital cytology review requires a separate appropriate CLIA certificate following the March 23, 2026 end of the earlier discretion.

That federal policy does not supply validation evidence for this home workstation, network, display or fallback. The lesson's hold remains in place until the proposed workflow has the required local evidence. Recheck the current memo and applicable state requirements before implementation.
