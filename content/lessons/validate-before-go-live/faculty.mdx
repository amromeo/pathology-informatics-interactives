# Faculty guide: Reviewing a Whole-Slide Imaging Validation

## At a glance

Allow 45 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: review validation evidence for each intended use; review intended use and local validation protocol; justify the first local check. PIER: 7.1, 7.4.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Activity | Ask the group |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Expected answers


Allow 45 minutes. PIER 7.1 addresses applications and the complete imaging workflow; 7.4 addresses validation and regulatory responsibilities. Harrison/API Session 5, slides 20–29, supports the validation framework. Open the debrief at any time.

Ask what the director was asked to approve before interpreting any percentage. The fixed set is 58/60 (96.7%); the frozen exploration is 14/17 (82.4%). They differ in application and read interval. Pooling them would obscure the unsupported frozen use. The original pilot's 96.1% aggregate had no complete denominator; it has been replaced with accountable study counts.

The strongest plan selects the representative fixed-tissue set, same-reader comparison, at least 14 days, and the complete intended workflow. V12: investigate local blur and rescan without deleting the original failure. V44: assess interpretive variation and clinical significance without assigning an unsupported scanner cause. Even a seven-check plan needs completed corrective evidence before approval.

The guideline's exact concordance recommendation is investigation and attempted remedy when below 95%, not “approve every system above 95%.” CAP's FAQ uses a different shorthand; follow the formal summary. Recommendations differ from enforceable local accreditation or regulatory requirements. The 60-case and additional-20 guidance is not a rule that every monitor or reader always requires an independent 60-case series; justify coverage for the actual system and application.

Change answers: rendering update → targeted impact review and revalidation as indicated; frozen application → new-application validation and regulatory review; phone-number correction → documented administrative verification. Avoid both automatic full revalidation after every edit and automatic exemption for every software patch.

## Common misconceptions

- “Approve every reader from the pooled rate.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? Pooling can conceal a reader-specific or workflow-specific problem. Review causes and readiness for the intended use before clinical approval.
- “Exclude the reader from the denominator.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? Pooling can conceal a reader-specific or workflow-specific problem. Review causes and readiness for the intended use before clinical approval.
- “Reuse another institution validation unchanged.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? The validation must represent the system, users and clinical use being approved.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: review validation evidence for each intended use.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: review intended use and local validation protocol, because the validation must represent the system, users and clinical use being approved.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes the digital pathology director and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Annotate a validation plan with intended use, readers, case mix, washout and discordance review. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.

## PIER coverage

- 7.1: Bound the requested scope to the application and system supported by the packet.
- 7.4: Build a representative paired comparison, retain and investigate disagreements, recommend an approval boundary, and classify subsequent changes.
