## Say exactly what is being validated

An **intended use** names the specimens, the clinical purpose, who will read the images, the scanner, the viewer, the screen and how the work is done. Routine fixed-tissue reporting, urgent frozen-section consultation and reporting from home carry different risks and need different evidence. The checks should include slide identity, whether all the tissue was captured, retrieving the image, moving around the slide, linking to the report, and getting the glass slide when the image is not good enough.

The College of American Pathologists (CAP) guideline, updated in 2021, recommends at least 60 typical cases for one use, plus 20 more for each further use, such as immunohistochemistry or special stains, that the first set does not already cover. These are recommendations; any convenient set of 60 does not prove safety. Include the expected range of diagnoses, difficult cases, small findings and hard preparations, and write down any cases you leave out before looking at the results.

**Intraobserver concordance** compares a pathologist's reading on screen with the same pathologist's reading on glass. Comparing a new reader with the original report mixes up differences between people with differences between glass and screen. The guideline recommends at least two weeks between the two readings. Separate sessions, the order of cases, whether readers can see earlier interpretations, and training all need planning so that readers do not simply remember the case or improve with practice during the study.

Record each original reading before anyone reconciles the two. Agree beforehand how clinically important disagreements will be classified and what will trigger an investigation. The guideline says to investigate, and try to fix the cause, if agreement is below 95%. That does not mean a serious disagreement can be ignored when the overall figure is higher.

This laboratory's director also requires that scanning problems are either fixed and recorded or put on hold, that every disagreement is reviewed, that every step of the proposed way of working is checked, and that the scope is signed off. Those are this case's conditions, not a general rule for every laboratory.

Agreement is worked out for each pathologist. A combined percentage can hide one reader below the 95% line. Rao and Chen each agreed on 29 of their 30 cases (96.7%); V12 is Rao's disagreement and V44 is Chen's.
