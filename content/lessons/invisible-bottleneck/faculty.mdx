# Faculty guide: Investigating Delayed Result Release

## At a glance

Allow 35 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: choose a denominator that includes unfinished work; check the extract inclusion rule; justify the first local check. PIER: 5.5, 2.1.

## Run of show

| Minutes | Activity | Ask the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–7 | Read the case and records | What decision has the laboratory been asked to make? |
| 7–25 | Make, test and revise a choice | Which record supports your decision? What could fail? |
| 25–35 | Debrief and retrieval questions | How would you verify this in your own laboratory? |

## Expected answers


Allow 35 minutes. Let the learner first explain what the 20-minute median
actually describes. Inspect the refresh log before selecting the current
extract; then link unfinished accessions. This sequence separates a stale
dataset from exclusion inherent in a finalized-result table.

Expected current all-record summary: 14 received, 12 completed, two pending;
total median 40 and nearest-rank p90 80 minutes. Peak-only summary: eight
received, six completed, two pending; total median 72.5, p90 85. Peak analysis
interval is 15 throughout; post-analysis median 57.5, p90 70. Pending total
ages at noon are 114 and 113 minutes, not completed turnaround times.

Require the learner to state the start, end, source, extract time, population,
and treatment of missing endpoints. A passing final recommendation requires
current linked data and the post-analysis view, along with a coverage pilot.
Ask which balancing measures would detect rushed review, displaced work, or
increased costs. The data support a mechanism to investigate, not proof of a
causal staffing effect or a hospital-wide rate.

PIER alignment: 5.5 and 2.1. Harrison/API: Session 0 slides 10, 15, 18–20;
Session 2 slides 12, 24–26; Session 3 slides 20–24; Session 4 slides 13–15.
The original record set makes data source and processing history, integration, freshness, query
performance, and operational interpretation observable. Statistical definitions
follow the explicit formulas in the lesson, so a learner can reproduce them
without external software.

## Preparing discussion with API slides

Use [Association for Pathology Informatics teaching slides: Databases (PowerPoint)](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/assets/lms/jimharrison/PathInformatics%202%20Databases%20slides.pptx) by James H. Harrison, Jr., slides 12, 24–26, to prepare the discussion after residents have explained their case decision. Ask: Compare an operational database with a database used for analysis. Explain how delayed updates or a query's selection rules can make a dashboard disagree with current accession records. Have residents support their answer with a specific record or result from this lesson before comparing it with the slides.

These slides provide teaching background. Use the lesson's primary sources and current local policies for clinical criteria, legal requirements, and approval decisions; do not turn a slide example or numerical recommendation into a local requirement. If the PowerPoint link changes, locate the session on the [API teaching-slide page](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/teaching-slide-sets). The lesson and discussion remain usable without the external file.


## Common misconceptions

- “The final times of completed cases.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? Completed-case turnaround and current queue age answer different questions. A queue analysis must include unfinished eligible work.
- “The number already finalized.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? Completed-case turnaround and current queue age answer different questions. A queue analysis must include unfinished eligible work.
- “Sort the completed cases by longest time.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? Establish whether pending specimens are present before interpreting workload or delays.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: choose a denominator that includes unfinished work.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: check the extract inclusion rule, because establish whether pending specimens are present before interpreting workload or delays.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes an operations manager and reporting analyst and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Write a dashboard definition with its population, timestamps, pending cases and an hourly summary. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.
