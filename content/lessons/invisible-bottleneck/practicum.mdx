# Read one laboratory dashboard at your institution

## Who to meet

A laboratory operations manager and the analyst who builds the dashboard.

## Time

30–60 minutes.

## Steps

1. With your supervisor, choose one turnaround-time dashboard you are allowed to review.
2. Find out where its data come from, how often they are copied and how users can tell when the last copy was made.
3. Ask which records are included. Are specimens that are still pending in the data, or only final results?
4. Write down the start and end times the dashboard uses, and how it calculates any median or percentile.
5. Ask who would test a change to the dashboard and who would approve it.

## Bring back

A one-page definition of the dashboard: which specimens it covers, the start and end times, how pending work is shown and when the data were last updated. Include one open question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What would change your view of where the delay is? Why might sorting the finished cases by longest time mislead the team?

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor allows you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots in the hospital's own systems, and bring back a description with no patient identifiers.
