### Define the interval before reading the number

**Turnaround time** needs a start and an end. This dashboard starts the clock at laboratory receipt and stops it at final release. It does not cover collection to receipt, the clinician reading the report, or anything done about the result. A different question needs different start and end points.

The chosen records also have an analysis-complete time from the analyzer log. Receipt-to-analysis covers any waiting or preparation before the specimen is run, as well as the run itself. Analysis-to-release covers waiting in a queue, review and anything else after the run. Neither one on its own measures staff effort or the analyzer's own processing time.

Join these records on the accession number, keeping one row per accession. If every message or rerun is joined without sorting out repeated events, records multiply and the times come out wrong. Before subtracting one time from another, check what each event means, the time zones, whether the clocks agree, how duplicates are handled and whether any times are missing.

### Check when the data were last updated

The page viewed at noon does not show noon's data. Each hour, a scheduled query copies final results from the LIS into a separate reporting database, and the dashboard summarizes that copy. The last copy that worked was made at 09:00; the next two timed out. That copy holds only six finished routine records, each taking 20 minutes. It cannot tell you anything about the 10:00 delivery.

Reporting databases get copies of data from the systems used in daily work. **Business intelligence** tools summarize those copies to help people make decisions. A record of where each field came from and how it was changed tells you whether to trust it, and the time of the last update tells you how current it is. Both matter even when the dashboard's arithmetic is right.

The analytics team should look at the failed query, how the tables are joined, how the query runs and whether it could copy only new records. Any change needs testing for missing and duplicate records. Running a bigger query more often against the live LIS could slow it down for everyone. Users should always be able to see whether the last copy worked and what time the data are from.

### Include work that is not finished

A table of final results leaves out accessions still waiting for release. At noon, Q-13 and Q-14 have been run but are still in the middleware review queue. Q-13 and Q-14 are being held because their hemolysis indices are above the local limit for potassium, and recollection has been requested. Their total turnaround times are not known yet. Show how long they have been waiting, separately. Do not enter zero, pretend noon is their release time, or drop them without saying so.

The linked view keeps those records, but calculates the summary numbers only from finished results. That makes the denominator clear: 12 finished and two pending, out of 14 received in the chosen period. These records were picked for this investigation; they are not a checked estimate of how the whole laboratory performs.

### Compare the typical time with the long delays

The **median** is the middle finished time, or the average of the two middle times when there is an even number. The six earlier records finish in 20 minutes. The six finished records from the 10:00 delivery take 60–85 minutes, so the median for all 12 is 40 minutes. Reporting only the earlier median hides the group that changed.

The **90th percentile** shown here uses the nearest-rank method: sort the finished times, multiply their number by 0.9, round up and take that position. For 12 records that is the 11th value, 80 minutes. With small numbers, other ways of calculating percentiles can give different answers, so the individual records and the waiting times of the pending ones still matter.

Select the 10:00 group and compare the two intervals. Receipt-to-analysis stays at 15 minutes, while the time after analysis runs from 45 to 70 minutes for the finished records. The worklist log shows that reviewers were late to arrive. That points to a closer look at staffing and how review is organized, not to the analyzer being too slow.
