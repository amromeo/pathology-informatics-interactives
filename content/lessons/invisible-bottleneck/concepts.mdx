### Define the interval before interpreting a number

**Turnaround time** requires a start and an end. This dashboard begins at
laboratory receipt and ends at final release. It does not measure collection
to receipt, clinician review of the report, or action on the result.
A different question requires different events.

The selected records also contain an analysis-complete time from the analyzer
log. Receipt-to-analysis includes any wait or preparation before analysis as
well as processing. Analysis-to-release includes queueing, review, and other
post-analysis work. Neither interval alone measures staff effort or pure
instrument processing time.

Join these records by the accession key, preserving one intended row per
accession. Joining every message or rerun without resolving repeated events
can multiply records and distort durations. Check event definitions,
time zones, clock synchronization, duplicate handling, and missing timestamps
before subtracting them.

### Check when the data were actually refreshed

The noon page view does not establish noon data. The last successful extract
was at 09:00; the next two scheduled queries timed out. The earlier snapshot
contains only six completed routine records, all with 20-minute total times.
It cannot describe the 10:00 delivery.

An analytic store receives copies from operational systems through extracts or
interfaces. **Business intelligence** tools summarize these copies for decisions.
Data lineage records where fields came from and how they were transformed.
Freshness indicates how recent those copies are. Both matter even when a
dashboard's arithmetic is correct.

The analytics team should examine the failed query, join keys, execution plan,
indexes, and incremental extraction strategy. Changes require testing for
completeness and duplicates. Running a larger query more frequently against
the production LIS may worsen operational load. An extract-health indicator
and an explicit data timestamp should remain visible to users.

### Include work that has not finished

A finalized-result table excludes accessions still awaiting release. At noon,
Q-13 and Q-14 have completed analysis but remain in the middleware review
queue. Their total turnaround times are not yet known. Show their elapsed
ages separately; do not insert zero, pretend noon is their release time, or
drop them without explanation.

The linked view retains those records while calculating completed-result
statistics only from completed results. Its denominator is therefore explicit:
12 completed and two pending out of 14 received in the selected interval.
These are records chosen for investigation, not a validated estimate of the
whole laboratory's performance.

### Compare typical values with the long delays

The **median** is the middle completed duration, averaging the two middle
values when the number is even. Six earlier records finish in 20 minutes.
The six completed peak records take 60–85 minutes, so the combined median is
40 minutes. Reporting only the earlier median conceals the changed group.

The displayed **90th percentile** uses the nearest-rank convention: sort the
completed durations and take rank rounded upward from 0.9 times their count.
For 12 records it is the eleventh value, 80 minutes. Small samples and other
percentile conventions can yield different summaries. The individual records
and pending ages remain necessary evidence.

Select the peak group and compare intervals. Receipt-to-analysis stays at
15 minutes, while the completed post-analysis delays range from 45 to 70
minutes. The worklist log documents delayed reviewer availability. This
supports a targeted review of staffing and workflow, rather than concluding
that analyzer capacity caused the observed delay.
