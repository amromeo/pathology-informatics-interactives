### Try a change without removing safety checks

First, deal with the two accessions still open at noon. Then try having trained reviewers available when the courier arrives, keeping the laboratory's review rules as they are. Fix the data copy and keep an eye on it, so the trial can be judged on current data.

Decide what to measure. The main result is receipt-to-release time, reported by delivery group, with the long delays and the waiting times of pending work. A measure of the process is the time from analysis to review. Measures that catch side effects include results released wrongly, delays in phoning critical results, problems left open, overtime and staffing cost. Speeding up one queue by slowing down another bench is not an improvement.

Compare similar groups of specimens, schedules and periods before and after the trial, and record staffing and how many specimens arrived. This exercise shows where to look; it does not prove what caused the change or predict any savings. The final review needs current linked data, a look at the time after analysis, and a plan that keeps the clinical safety checks in place.
