## Debrief: choose a denominator that includes unfinished work

### What happened in this case


The original 20-minute median described six early completions in a stale
09:00 extract. At noon the full set contains 12 completed and two pending
accessions. The completed total median is 40 minutes and the nearest-rank
90th percentile is 80 minutes. The pending records are still unresolved.

The completed peak group has a 15-minute receipt-to-analysis interval but
45–70 minutes after analysis. The middleware log supplies the workflow context:
review coverage was delayed. Another analyzer would not address that observed
interval. Disabling review rules would remove a clinical safeguard without
establishing that the affected results could safely release.

A useful improvement plan therefore combines queue investigation, appropriate
coverage, resolution of open accessions, and reliable analytic refresh.
Laboratory operations owns the work; middleware and analytics staff supply
configuration and trustworthy measurements. Monitor clinical and financial
consequences alongside speed.

<summary>A closer look: look at the work still waiting</summary>

A report based on completed results describes the specimens that reached completion by its extraction time. The oldest work can be absent if it is still waiting. Staff need a separate view of pending accessions to understand the current queue.

For each accession, define the intervals from receipt to analysis and from analysis to release. The delay after analysis directs attention to review coverage and release conditions. Buying another analyzer would require a different finding about where work accumulated.

Write down the report’s refresh time and the population used for each measure. Keep a pending result visible as pending; assigning it a completed turnaround time would change what the number means.

**At noon:** What would you show beside the completed-result median so the manager could see both finished work and the remaining queue?
