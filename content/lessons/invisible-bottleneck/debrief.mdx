## Debrief: choose a denominator that includes unfinished work

### What happened in this case

The 20-minute median came from six early results in a copy of the data made at 09:00. At noon, the full set has 12 completed and two pending accessions. The completed total median is 40 minutes and the 90th percentile is 80 minutes, using the nearest-rank method. The two pending accessions are still waiting.

The finished records from the 10:00 delivery took 15 minutes from receipt to analysis, but 45–70 minutes after analysis. The middleware log explains why: reviewers arrived late. Another analyzer would do nothing about that part of the delay. Switching off the review rules would remove a clinical safety check, with nothing to show that those results were safe to release.

So a useful plan combines looking at the queue, having reviewers there when they are needed, finishing the open accessions and making the data copy reliable. The laboratory runs that work; middleware and analytics staff supply the settings and trustworthy numbers. Keep an eye on clinical and cost effects as well as speed.

<summary>A closer look: look at the work still waiting</summary>

A report built from finished results only describes the specimens that were finished when the data were copied. The oldest work can be missing because it is still waiting. Staff need a separate view of pending accessions to see the current queue.

For each accession, work out the time from receipt to analysis and from analysis to release. A long delay after analysis points to review coverage and release conditions. Buying another analyzer would need evidence that work was piling up somewhere else.

Write down when the report was last updated and which specimens each measure covers. Show a pending result as pending; giving it a finished turnaround time would change what the number means.

**At noon:** What would you show next to the median for finished results so the manager could see both the finished work and the queue still waiting?

### Who does what

The reporting analyst fixes the data copy and records how it was tested. The chemistry supervisor and laboratory manager decide on reviewer coverage and approve any change to how work is done.
