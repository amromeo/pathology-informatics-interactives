# Investigating Delayed Result Release

At noon, the chemistry supervisor reports that specimens delivered around 10:00 are taking much longer than usual to come out as final results. The operations dashboard still shows a median of **20 minutes** from receipt to release. A manager asks whether the laboratory needs another analyzer.

You are the resident helping the supervisor and the analytics team. The dashboard is built from final results in the laboratory information system (LIS). It opens in a web page whose clock shows the current time, but its numbers come from a copy of the data made on a schedule. The middleware's review records, and accessions that are not finished yet, are kept in other systems.

The review set has 14 accessions received between 08:00 and 10:07 the same day: six earlier routine ones and eight from the 10:00 delivery.

## Terms used in this case

- **Turnaround time:** the time between two set events. Here the clock starts at laboratory receipt and stops at final release.
- **Data copy:** a scheduled copy of LIS data into a separate reporting database. The dashboard reads the copy, not the live LIS.
- **Pending:** received by the laboratory but not yet released.
- **Median and 90th percentile:** the median is the middle finished time; the 90th percentile is the time that 90% of finished specimens beat.

## Your task

Can the dashboard answer the manager's question? Check when its data were copied and which records it includes. Link the timestamps, choose a group of specimens that can answer the question, and compare the time before and after analysis. Then choose a change to try and say how you would judge it.

## What you will be able to do

- Check when a dashboard's data were last copied and whether pending work is included.
- Work out a median and 90th percentile, and show pending work alongside them.
- Split turnaround into the time before and after analysis to find where the delay is.
- Choose a change to try, with measures for speed and for side effects.
