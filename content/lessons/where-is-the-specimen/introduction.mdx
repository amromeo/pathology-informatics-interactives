# Where Is the Specimen?

At 08:40 on Tuesday, a pathologists' assistant checks the operating room's delivery list against the gross-room worklist. A tissue excision is on the delivery list, but its accession, **S26-1042**, is on the cytology worklist instead.

You are the pathology resident working with the surgical pathology supervisor and a **laboratory information system (LIS)** analyst. Accessioning received container **C-1042-A** at 08:11 and checked its patient and part against the requisition. After that, the records show no further receiving scan. Staff need to find the container while the analyst works out why the case went to the wrong worklist.

On Monday, the analyst added an entry for the tissue-excision procedure by copying an existing entry in the LIS. That entry tells the LIS which worklist an excision goes to. The destination the supervisor had approved was the gross room. The testing done before the change covered routine biopsies and cytology, but not the new excision code.

## Terms used in this case

### Records that connect the order and specimen

- **Order catalog:** the list of examinations that can be ordered, with their names, specimen requirements and collection instructions.
- **Accession:** the laboratory record that ties an order to its patient and specimen. Here, S26-1042 has one part, A, in container C-1042-A.
- **Procedure code:** a short code the LIS uses to look up an examination's settings. In this laboratory, SP-EX is the tissue-excision procedure.
- **Procedure dictionary:** the stored settings for each procedure code. An entry can set the worklist, preparation instructions and report settings.

### Assignment and tracking

- **Worklist:** an electronic list of cases assigned to a team. The gross-room team uses its list to see which cases they need to process.
- **Routing:** the step that uses an examination's settings to assign the work. Here, the LIS looks up SP-EX and reads where it should go.
- **Audit trail:** a dated history of events, showing the person or program that recorded each one. It lets staff piece together what happened to a case.
- **Receiving scan:** a barcode scan recorded when staff receive the actual container at a named place.
- **Regression check:** after a change, a test that things that already worked still work. A fix to one route should leave the cytology and frozen-section routes as they were.

## Your task

Read the four events and find the last place the container was recorded. Explain how the procedure entry put the case on the cytology worklist. Then fix the routing settings, test five procedures, and choose how staff should find the container and hand it over.

## What you will be able to do

- Use an event's time, author and action to say what it shows.
- Follow a procedure code from the accession to the destination stored for it.
- Test a fixed route, the routes that were not changed, and a code the LIS does not recognize.
- Describe how staff should find the existing specimen and record its receipt.
