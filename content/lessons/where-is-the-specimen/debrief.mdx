### What the routing tests show

The original settings pass **three of five** checks. SP-EX sends the excision to cytology, and SP-NEW falls through to the default gross-room destination.

Changing SP-EX to the gross room produces **four of five** passing checks. The unrecognized code still needs attention. Changing the unmatched-code destination to accessioning review gives **five of five**, while preserving the biopsy, cytology and frozen-section routes.

Sending every procedure to the gross room would create new problems for cytology and frozen-section work. The [routing results](#specimen-routing-results) make those consequences visible one case at a time.

### What staff still need to do

A passing replay supports review of the proposed configuration. The LIS analyst should retain the tested settings and results with the change record. The laboratory supervisor reviews the destinations and agrees on when the correction will be used.

For S26-1042, staff also need to correct the existing worklist assignment under the local procedure. They should record gross-room receipt when that team actually receives the verified container. Creating a replacement accession would introduce another record for a case whose identity was already established.

When reporting this incident to the supervisor, name the procedure entry that caused the assignment, the last documented physical location, the proposed correction and the person following up on the container.

<summary>A closer look: check what a scan actually recorded</summary>

A successful barcode read identifies the record encoded on the label. Staff still need to know what happened when the code was read: receipt at accessioning, assignment to a worklist or a physical handoff to another section.

The SP-EX case illustrates why those events need separate names and times. The accessioning scan documents receipt. The cytology worklist entry documents a software assignment. Locating the container requires a later observation or handoff record. Keep the original events so the team can reconstruct the delay.

Standardized labels can help laboratories exchange identifiers, but the receiving software must still interpret the identifier and apply the correct local routing rule.

**When reviewing a tracking screen:** Which event tells you that someone physically received the container, and who recorded it?
