## Debrief: explain the assignment and locate the container

The [audit events](#specimen-audit-title) link C-1042-A to the right accession and part. J. Lee recorded receipt at accessioning at 08:11. One minute later, the LIS routing service used SP-EX in dictionary revision 17 to send the case to cytology.

Those events explain why the case showed up on the cytology worklist. They say nothing about where the container went after accessioning. The assistant and accessioning staff need to find it, check that it is the right container and record the handoff that actually happens.

### What the routing tests show

The original settings pass **three of five** checks. SP-EX sends the excision to cytology, and SP-NEW falls through to the default destination, the gross room.

Changing SP-EX to the gross room gives **four of five** passes. The unrecognized code still needs fixing. Changing the destination for unmatched codes to accessioning review gives **five of five**, and the biopsy, cytology and frozen-section routes still work.

Sending every procedure to the gross room would cause new problems for cytology and frozen-section work. The [routing results](#specimen-routing-results) show those effects one case at a time.

### What staff still need to do

A passing replay is a reason to review the proposed settings, not to switch them on. The LIS analyst should keep the tested settings and results with the change record. The laboratory supervisor reviews the destinations and agrees when the fix will go live.

For S26-1042, staff also need to correct the worklist assignment following the local procedure. They should record receipt in the gross room when that team actually receives the checked container. Creating a new accession would add a second record for a case whose identity was never in doubt.

When reporting this to the supervisor, name the procedure entry that caused the problem, the last place the container was recorded, the proposed fix and the person following up on the container.

<summary>A closer look: check what a scan actually recorded</summary>

A good barcode read tells you which record the label points to. Staff still need to know what happened when it was read: receipt at accessioning, assignment to a worklist, or a handoff to another section.

The SP-EX case shows why those events need their own names and times. The scan at accessioning records receipt. The entry on the cytology worklist records a software assignment. Finding the container needs a later sighting or handoff record. Keep the original events so the team can work out what caused the delay.

Standard labels can help laboratories share identifiers, but the receiving software still has to read the identifier and apply the right local routing rule.

**When reviewing a tracking screen:** Which event tells you that someone actually received the container, and who recorded it?
