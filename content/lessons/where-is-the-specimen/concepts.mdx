### A worklist is not a location record

The **accession** connects the patient, requested examination, specimen, and parts
under one laboratory case number. Here S26-1042 is the case and C-1042-A identifies
its container. A barcode scan retrieves that record; it does not decide the
clinical meaning of the procedure code.

One accession may contain several separately labeled parts. Matching only the
patient is therefore insufficient: staff also compare the accession, part, and
requested examination. In this case, the patient and part agree at receipt, so
there is no evidence that changing the barcode or creating a replacement
accession would solve the worklist problem.

A **procedure dictionary** stores configuration for each examination. The routing
process reads its destination and adds the case to a **worklist**, the electronic
list a team uses to find assigned work. A receiving scan records a different fact:
who documented physical receipt, where, and when. The **audit trail** keeps these
events distinguishable.

For this case, the sequence is: staff scan C-1042-A, the LIS retrieves S26-1042
part A, and the routing process reads the stored SP-EX procedure code. It then
looks up the destination in revision 17 of the procedure dictionary. This
lookup succeeds technically even though its configured destination is wrong
for a tissue excision. A valid code is not evidence that the associated
configuration has the intended clinical meaning.

### Compare three different questions

1. **Which patient and part does the container represent?** Compare the requisition
   with the accessioning identity check. Agreement supports the identity, but
   does not tell you where the container went after receipt.
2. **Which team was assigned the case?** Inspect the procedure entry and worklist
   assignment event. These establish the electronic destination, but do not tell
   you whether that team received the container.
3. **Where was receipt last documented?** Find the named receiving scan with its
   time and workstation. This establishes the last recorded location, but not
   necessarily the present location if no later handoff was recorded.

A missing scan is an information gap. It may reflect an undocumented transfer,
a container still at accessioning, or another handling problem. Contacting the
staff involved and checking the container resolves that uncertainty. Inferring
a location from the worklist does not.

### Repair a rule, then challenge it

The cards below show the supervisor's approved routing plan for this
laboratory beside the editable configuration. Change individual destinations,
then replay the reported case and neighboring procedures. An unrecognized code
must remain available for accessioning review rather than being silently assigned
to a work area.

Start by replaying the unchanged configuration. Then fix only SP-EX and replay
again. The additional unknown-code case asks what should happen when an incoming
procedure has no approved entry. Review staff must clarify that examination and
document the destination; a default gross-room assignment would conceal the gap.

The neighboring tests are **regression checks**: they ask whether a correction
changed behavior that was already correct. Cytology and intraoperative specimens
need their own work areas in this laboratory. Moving every route to the gross
room would correct the reported excision while disrupting those workflows.

Configuration changes apply to future routing decisions in this simulation. They
do not relocate the existing container or reconstruct an unrecorded handoff.
The LIS analyst documents the proposed change and test results for laboratory
review; accessioning and receiving staff separately correct the existing worklist
assignment under the approved procedure and document the physical handoff.
