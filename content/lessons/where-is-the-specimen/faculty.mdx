# Faculty guide: Where Is the Specimen?

Allow 25 minutes. The resident should explain how the LIS connects an order, specimen and worklist, then propose a tested routing correction and a documented physical handoff. The lesson addresses PIER 4.1, 4.2 and 4.4.

## Run of show

Spend about 5 minutes on the case and terms, 7 on the events, 8 on the routing tests and 5 on recovery and local application. The explanation before the event controls teaches the lookup process; the explanation before the routing controls teaches the replay.

## Read the events together

Ask the resident to describe the 08:11 record aloud: J. Lee received C-1042-A at ACC-02 and linked it to S26-1042, part A. Then ask who authored the 08:12 event. It is a routing-service entry, produced when the LIS read the SP-EX destination in revision 17.

A complete answer separates the identified case, the assigned team and the last documented receipt. The current physical location remains to be confirmed. Ask whom the resident would call and what observation should be recorded after the container is found.

Use the procedure lookup to explain the informatics step. The barcode retrieves the accession; the accession supplies SP-EX; the dictionary supplies its destination. Copying an entry can retain a setting inappropriate for the new procedure. The change review omitted a test of the newly added code.

## Work through the routing checks

Run the unchanged settings first: three of five pass. Changing SP-EX from cytology to gross gives four of five. Holding unmatched codes for accessioning review gives five of five.

Then deliberately route all cases to gross. Ask the resident to name the consequences for cytology and frozen-section processing. Return to the approved configuration and repeat the complete test set.

Changing a setting clears the preceding test result. Ask why the previous result can no longer support the new configuration. The expected destinations come from the supervisor's approved plan; they remain fixed as learners edit the settings.

After a passing replay, select the option that records receipt from the queue entry. The recovery review still fails. The correct plan requires staff to locate and verify the container, correct its worklist assignment and document its actual handoff.

## Common misconceptions

- **The worklist proves the container reached that department.** Ask for the receiving event, person and timestamp. The cytology assignment was generated by software.
- **A barcode problem explains any routing error.** Here the scan retrieved the intended patient, accession and part. Inspect the SP-EX dictionary entry.
- **Fixing the reported route completes the repair.** Ask about SP-NEW and the unaffected routes, then ask who is locating the existing container.
- **One correct patient identifier is enough to connect all work.** Have the learner follow the specific order, accession, container and part through the records.

## Extend the discussion

The additional questions compare how anatomic and clinical pathology organize records. Parts, blocks and slides describe the AP example; individual chemistry tests and numeric results describe the CP example. Both benefit from specimen instructions, order-time questions and review of possible duplicate orders.

Ask how the receiving section sees information entered during ordering. This makes the connection between an order catalog and the worklist concrete. A record can contain the right answer while placing it on a screen the responsible staff do not use.

## Teaching sources

[API's LIS and health-information-system teaching slides](https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/assets/lms/jimharrison/PathInformatics%203%20LIS-HIS%20slides.pptx), by James H. Harrison, Jr., provide background on specimen-centered records and LIS functions. Use session 3, slides 6–10 and 16–18, after the resident has explained this case.

For optional reading about labeling, [CLSI AUTO12](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/auto12/) addresses specimen-label content and placement. [CLSI AUTO14](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/auto14/) addresses two-dimensional barcoding in clinical and anatomic pathology, including encoded content and its use. These are sources for discussing local label design and validation. The case's procedure codes and routing destinations are local examples.
