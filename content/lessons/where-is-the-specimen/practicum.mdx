# Trace an order and a specimen at your institution

## Who to meet

An accessioning supervisor and an LIS analyst.

## Time

30–60 minutes.

## Steps

1. Ask the supervisor to pick an accession you are allowed to review. Start with the examination requested, the specimen requirements, the accession and the container or part identifiers.
2. Have the analyst show you which procedure entry sets the worklist destination. Note the entry's name, the setting that matters and how the team tells one version from another.
3. Follow the receipt and handoff events you can find. For each one, note who or what recorded it, where and when, and what it shows happened.
4. Ask how staff handle a procedure the LIS does not recognize, and a handoff nobody recorded. Look at how a proposed routing change is tested before it goes live.
5. Compare the records for one anatomic pathology case and one clinical pathology test. Note which links they share and which records belong only to one or the other.

## Bring back

A one-page diagram linking the order, the specimen, the procedure entry and the receiving worklist. Show physical receipts separately from software assignments. Include one open question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

Which record would you use to find where the container was last recorded? Which cases would you include when testing a fix to one route?

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor allows you to review. Keep patient identifiers and screenshots in the hospital's own systems, and bring back a diagram with no patient identifiers.
