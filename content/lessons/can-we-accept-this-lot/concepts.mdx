## Describe the 18 paired differences

The statistics below use the 18 specimens near the clinical decision concentration. They summarize **new lot minus current lot**, not the troponin concentrations themselves.

### Mean: the average shift

Add up the 18 differences in the [paired-results table](#lot-results-table). They come to +21.6 ng/L. Divide by 18 specimens: 21.6 ÷ 18 = **+1.2 ng/L**. On average, the new lot reads 1.2 ng/L higher in this group.

An average does not describe every specimen. TNI-012 has a difference of −0.8 ng/L, while TNI-016 has a difference of +3.5 ng/L.

### Median: the middle of the ordered differences

Sort the differences from lowest to highest. With 18 values, the middle falls between the 9th and 10th. Both are +0.8 ng/L, so the **median is +0.8 ng/L**.

The larger positive differences pull the mean above the median. If the biggest difference grew even larger while the middle values stayed put, the mean would rise and the median would not move.

### Standard deviation: how much specimens differ from the average

The **standard deviation**, or SD, describes how widely the individual differences are spread around their mean. Here it is about **1.2 ng/L**. That tells you the shift varies from specimen to specimen; it does not mean every specimen differs by 1.2 ng/L.

To calculate it, take each difference's distance from the mean, square those distances, add them up, divide by 17 and take the square root. You divide by 17, one less than the 18 specimens, because the mean was worked out from the same data. You do not need to do this by hand to read the plot.

More spread also makes the average less precise for the same number of specimens. If the SD were 4.0 ng/L instead of 1.2 with the same 18 specimens, a different set of specimens could easily give a quite different average, and the confidence interval for the mean would be wider.
