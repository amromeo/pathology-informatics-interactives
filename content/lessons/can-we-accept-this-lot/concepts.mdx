## Describe the 18 paired differences

The statistics below use the 18 specimens near the clinical decision concentration. They summarize **new lot minus current lot**, not the troponin concentrations themselves.

### Mean: the average shift

Add the 18 differences in the [paired-results table](#lot-results-table). Their sum is +21.6 ng/L. Divide by 18 specimens: 21.6 ÷ 18 = **+1.2 ng/L**. The new lot therefore reads 1.2 ng/L higher on average in this group.

An average does not describe every specimen. TNI-012 has a difference of −0.8 ng/L, while TNI-016 has a difference of +3.5 ng/L.

### Median: the middle of the ordered differences

Sort the differences from lowest to highest. There are 18 values, so the middle lies between positions 9 and 10. Both are +0.8 ng/L; their average is the **median, +0.8 ng/L**.

The larger positive differences pull the mean above the median. If the largest difference increased further while the middle values stayed in place, the mean would rise and the median would stay the same.

### Standard deviation: how much specimens differ from the average

The **standard deviation**, or SD, describes the spread of the individual differences around their mean. Here it is about **1.2 ng/L**. This says the shift varies between specimens; it does not mean every specimen differs by 1.2 ng/L.

To calculate the sample SD, find each difference's distance from the mean, square those distances, add them, divide by 17 and take the square root. The divisor is one less than the 18 observations because their mean was estimated from the same data. You do not need to calculate it by hand to interpret the plot.

A larger spread also makes the group's average less precise at the same sample size. If the SD were 4.0 rather than 1.2 ng/L with the same 18 specimens, another group could give a more variable average. The confidence interval for the mean would widen.
