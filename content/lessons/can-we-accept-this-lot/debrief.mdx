# Debrief

The same 30 patient specimens were tested with both reagent lots, so the results were paired. The main statistical analysis used the 18 specimens near the laboratory's assay-specific clinical decision concentration. Very low and markedly elevated specimens were reviewed separately because one summary of absolute differences would not describe the full measuring range well.

For the 18 primary specimens, the new lot averaged 1.2 ng/L higher than the current lot. The median difference was 0.8 ng/L, and the standard deviation was 1.2 ng/L. Several larger positive differences pulled the mean above the median, but there was no single markedly discordant specimen.

The 95% confidence interval for the mean paired difference was approximately +0.6 to +1.8 ng/L. The paired t-test produced p < 0.001. That small p-value supports the conclusion that the lots have a measurable average difference. It does not determine whether the difference is medically acceptable.

Before testing began, this synthetic laboratory set an acceptable average shift of −2 to +2 ng/L near the clinical decision concentration. The entire confidence interval remained inside that range. The six very low specimens had the same reportable category with both lots, and the six elevated specimens remained within the laboratory's separate percentage limit.

The laboratory can therefore accept the lot after the laboratory director reviews the complete study. The small positive shift should be documented, and the chemistry lab should monitor patient-result comparisons after the lot change.

The central statistical lesson is broader than this assay. Mean, median, and standard deviation describe the specimens tested. Confidence intervals and hypothesis tests help the laboratory draw a conclusion beyond those specimens. The final medical decision must also consider the size of the effect and criteria established before the data are examined.
