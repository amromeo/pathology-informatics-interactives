## Debrief: What supports accepting this lot?

The [paired-results table](#lot-results-table) contains 30 specimens measured with both lots. The main numerical comparison uses the 18 near the laboratory's clinical decision concentration.

Their differences add up to +21.6 ng/L. Dividing by 18 gives a mean of +1.2 ng/L. The median is +0.8 ng/L and the SD is about 1.2 ng/L. The larger positive differences explain why the mean is higher than the median.

The confidence interval is approximately +0.6 to +1.8 ng/L. The paired t-test gives p < 0.001, which is good evidence that the average difference is not zero. That tells you a difference can be detected. The laboratory still has to decide whether a difference of that size is acceptable.

Before testing, the director set an acceptable average shift of −2 to +2 ng/L. The whole confidence interval falls inside that range. The six very low specimens all stay in the same less-than-2 ng/L category, and all six elevated specimens meet the separate ±10% criterion.

Together, these results support accepting the lot once the director has reviewed them. The chemistry team should record the positive shift and carry out the planned monitoring after the change. A small p-value on its own, or a mean inside the limit on its own, would not show that every required check had passed.

## What to take from this case

Keep measurements from the same specimen paired. Say which concentration group each summary describes. Keep the spread of individual differences separate from the uncertainty in their mean. Set the acceptance criteria before looking at the results.

The ±2 ng/L and ±10% limits belong to this laboratory's case. They are not general troponin limits, and this case is not a complete reagent-lot procedure. A local protocol has to cover the assay, the concentration ranges, individual differences, how specimens are chosen, and the risks that matter for how the test is used.

The statistics inform the director's decision; they do not make it.
