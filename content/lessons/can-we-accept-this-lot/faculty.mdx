# Faculty guide: Can We Accept This Lot?

Allow 32 minutes. Learners compare two reagent lots, explain the difference between statistical evidence and the laboratory's acceptance criteria, and choose tests for five short study designs. This addresses PIER 2.2.

## Run of show

Spend 6 minutes inspecting paired results, 8 minutes on descriptive statistics, 8 minutes on the confidence interval and p-value, and 10 minutes on test selection and the lot decision.

## Keep the pairing and concentration groups explicit

The worksheet contains 30 specimens tested with both lots: six very low, 18 near the clinical decision concentration and six elevated. Ask the learner to calculate TNI-013's difference: 18.7 − 18.0 = +0.7 ng/L. Then inspect a negative difference.

Use the [paired-results table](../../lessons/can-we-accept-this-lot/#lot-results-table) and dot plot before revealing the interpretation. Most differences are positive; several larger differences form a modest right tail. No single isolated observation explains the whole shift.

## Work through the numerical argument

For the 18 main specimens, the differences sum to 21.6 ng/L, giving a mean of +1.2. The ninth and tenth ordered differences are both +0.8, so the median is +0.8. The sample SD is approximately 1.2 ng/L.

Ask what changes if a large positive difference becomes larger. The mean rises; the middle positions may remain unchanged. Ask what a larger SD would do at the same sample size: it would increase uncertainty in the estimated average.

The standard error is approximately 1.2/√18 = 0.28. Using 17 degrees of freedom, the two-sided 95% t multiplier is approximately 2.1098. The resulting interval is about +0.6 to +1.8 ng/L; p < 0.001. Keep rounding for explanation separate from the calculation's full precision.

A useful misconception to test is that the interval contains 95% of individual differences. TNI-016 is +3.5 ng/L, outside the interval for the mean. Ask which quantity the interval actually estimates.

## Review the decision

The director's criteria were set before testing: an acceptable average shift within −2 to +2 ng/L near the assay-specific 99th-percentile limit of 20 ng/L, matching less-than-2 ng/L categories for the six low specimens, and differences within ±10% for the six elevated specimens.

All three checks pass in this case. Rejecting solely because p is small confuses detectable with unacceptable. Accepting without checking the other ranges omits part of the protocol. Document the shift and the planned follow-up after director review.

These are case-specific criteria, not a complete CLSI EP26 protocol or universal troponin limits. Discuss the additional local decisions about individual differences, sampling, imprecision and clinical risk rather than presenting this calculation as sufficient for every assay.

## Test-selection key

The answers are paired t, Wilcoxon signed-rank, independent-samples t, ANOVA and Fisher exact, in the order of the scenarios.

The signed-rank example uses paired numerical differences that are roughly symmetric but not normally distributed. Symmetry is an assumption of that test; skew alone does not establish that it is suitable. Ask what else the learner would need to know before applying it to markedly skewed differences.

The fixative example explicitly uses independent samples. If aliquots from each specimen were assigned to every fixative, the analysis would need to account for those related measurements. Ask learners to identify the design before naming a test.

## Further reading and local work

[CLSI EP26](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/ep26/) explains the use of patient samples and a protocol established before routine lot evaluations. The practicum asks learners to inspect their own laboratory's approved procedure and identify its criteria and approver.

[NIST's confidence-interval explanation](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda352.htm) supplies the formula and repeated-sampling interpretation. Its [p-value explanation](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section1/prc131.htm) clarifies the zero-difference question. The [signed-rank reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/signrank.htm) describes the symmetry assumption. These are optional readings for more depth; the learner has the reasoning needed in the lesson.

PIER R5 provides the curriculum objectives. The API/Harrison data-literacy sessions support discussing how source results become an analysis; they are not the source of the assay's acceptance limits.

## Common misconceptions

A small p-value does not mean a lot must be rejected. A confidence interval for the mean is not a range containing 95% of individual differences. Passing the main concentration group does not establish that the other ranges passed. Have learners explain which question each calculation answers.
