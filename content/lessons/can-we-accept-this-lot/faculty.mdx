# Faculty guide

## PIER alignment

This lesson addresses all three parts of PIER objective 2.2:

- Descriptive statistics: the learner interprets the mean, median, standard deviation, distribution, and modest right-sided tail of paired lot differences.
- Inferential statistics: the learner interprets a confidence interval, hypothesis test, and p-value without confusing statistical significance with medical importance.
- Common tests used in pathology research: the learner matches five short pathology questions to paired t, Wilcoxon signed-rank, independent-samples t, ANOVA, and Fisher exact tests.

## Case mechanism

The chemistry lab has a limited amount of the current high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I reagent lot remaining. A technologist tests 30 leftover patient plasma specimens with both the current and new lots. Because both measurements come from the same specimen, the analysis uses paired differences.

The primary group contains 18 specimens near the laboratory's assay-specific clinical decision concentration. The paired differences have a mean of +1.2 ng/L, median of +0.8 ng/L, and sample standard deviation of approximately 1.2 ng/L. Using a two-sided paired t procedure with 17 degrees of freedom gives a 95% confidence interval of approximately +0.6 to +1.8 ng/L and p < 0.001.

The laboratory's −2 to +2 ng/L limit is an original teaching assumption, not a universal troponin criterion. It is deliberately stated before the statistical result is interpreted. Real lot-change protocols use assay-specific acceptance criteria and may evaluate individual differences, concentration ranges, analytical imprecision, and the risk of incorrect acceptance or rejection in greater detail.

## Facilitation

Ask learners to describe the dot plot before showing the summary. If they begin with the p-value, return to the clinical question: “How large is the difference, how certain are we, and is that range acceptable?”

The small test-selection exercise is not intended to replace a statistics course. Its purpose is to make learners identify paired versus independent data and numerical versus categorical outcomes before selecting a test.

## Roles

The medical laboratory scientist performs the comparison according to the laboratory's approved procedure. The chemistry director or laboratory director establishes or approves the medical acceptance criteria, reviews unexpected findings, and approves the new lot for patient testing. A statistician may help design or analyze a difficult study, but the statistical result does not replace laboratory medical judgment.
