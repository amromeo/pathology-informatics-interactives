## Choose a test that fits the question

A statistical test should match both the kind of result and the way the specimens were selected.

- A **paired t-test** compares the mean difference when the same specimens are measured twice and the paired differences have no marked outlier or severe skew.
- A **Wilcoxon signed-rank test** is a common alternative for paired numerical results when the differences are markedly non-normal.
- An **independent-samples t-test** compares numerical results from two unrelated groups.
- **Analysis of variance**, commonly called **ANOVA**, compares a numerical measurement across three or more groups.
- **Chi-square** and **Fisher exact** tests compare counts or proportions. Fisher exact is especially useful when the groups are small.

The examples below are intentionally brief. They teach how study design and result type guide the initial choice; a real analysis also requires checking the assumptions of the selected test.
