## Start with one pair

Each row in the [paired-results table](#lot-results-table) holds two measurements from the same specimen. The worksheet subtracts the current-lot result from the new-lot result.

For TNI-013, the current lot gives 18.0 ng/L and the new lot gives 18.7 ng/L. The difference is 18.7 − 18.0 = **+0.7 ng/L**. The plus sign means the new lot reads higher. TNI-012 goes the other way: 16.7 − 17.5 = **−0.8 ng/L**.

The table starts by showing the 18 specimens near the clinical decision concentration, and the dot plot below shows their differences. A dot at zero means the two lots gave the same result. Dots to the right mean the new lot read higher; dots to the left mean it read lower.

Look at the pattern before you answer. Are most differences on one side of zero? How spread out are they? Does any one specimen sit far away from the rest? You can use the table's filter to look at the six very low or six elevated specimens; the main statistics still use the same 18.
