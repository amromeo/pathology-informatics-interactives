# Apply it at your institution

## Who to meet

Meet a clinical service representative and informatics lead.

## Time

Allow 30–60 minutes with a supervisor.

## Steps

1. Observe the ordering workflow.
2. With your supervisor, select one approved record or workflow to examine.
3. Sketch one decision-support intervention with its recipient, timing, exception and safety measures.
4. Compare the proposed change with local policy and identify who would test it and who would approve it.

## Bring back

Bring a one-page annotated diagram, table or checklist that documents this task: sketch one decision-support intervention with its recipient, timing, exception and safety measures. Include one unresolved question and the person who can answer it.

## Discuss with faculty

What observation would change your conclusion? Explain why the shortcut “add a warning without observing ordering” could mislead the team.

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor authorizes you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots within approved institutional systems; bring back a workflow description without patient identifiers.

## Further local discussion

#### Local practicum: examine one ordering intervention

With the supervising faculty member, choose an existing laboratory ordering suggestion. Use approved local access to inspect its clinical specification and demonstration workflow.

Record the clinical question, relevant data source, rule criteria, recipient, format, channel, timing, and available exception. Identify who approves the criteria and who maintains the build. Confirm whether the displayed report status and timestamp mean what the rule assumes.

Prepare four test encounters: one that should receive guidance, one that should not, one at a boundary, and one with a reason to proceed. Include the expected displayed information and permitted action for each.

Propose a small evaluation with unique decision counts, review of clinical appropriateness, staff burden, and delayed indicated tests. Present the plan to faculty before proposing a local change.
