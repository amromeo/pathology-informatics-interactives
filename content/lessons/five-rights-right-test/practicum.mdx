# Look at one ordering suggestion at your institution

## Who to meet

A representative of the clinicians who use the suggestion and the informatics lead who looks after it.

## Time

30–60 minutes.

## Steps

1. With your supervisor, choose an existing laboratory ordering suggestion. Watch a clinician, or a demonstration, place the order it applies to.
2. Find its clinical specification. Note the clinical question, where its data come from, the rule's criteria, who sees it, its format, where it appears, when it appears and how a clinician can override it.
3. Find out who approves the criteria and who maintains the build. Check that the report status and time it shows mean what the rule assumes.
4. Write four test encounters: one that should get the guidance, one that should not, one exactly at a limit and one with a good reason to go ahead. For each, note what should be shown and what the clinician should be able to do.
5. Sketch a small evaluation: count each order decision once, review whether decisions were clinically appropriate, and look at the extra work for staff and at any needed tests that were delayed.

## Bring back

A one-page summary of the suggestion, your four test encounters and your evaluation sketch. Include one open question and the person who can answer it. Show it to faculty before proposing any local change.

## Discuss with faculty

What would you need to see to change your design? Why might adding a warning without watching how clinicians order mislead the team?

## Privacy

Use only records your supervisor allows you to review. Keep identifiers and screenshots in the hospital's own systems, and bring back a description with no patient identifiers.
