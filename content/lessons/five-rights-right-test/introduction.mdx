# Designing Repeat-Test Guidance

You are the pathology resident working with the laboratory director and the electronic health record (EHR) team. Last month, the team added guidance for repeat hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) orders. Clinicians say the message arrives after they have already signed the order. Collection staff get it too, even though they did not order the test.

The laboratory director asks you to look at the ordering audit, compare it with the earlier laboratory report and redesign the guidance. The clinical question is whether the information already available should be looked at before another test is ordered. The rule cannot answer that without the clinician's judgment.

The local specification asks for a review when there is a usable final result collected within the last 90 days, or when another order is still pending. That window is the trigger for review in this case, not a general recommendation on how often HbA1c should be checked. A documented clinical reason must always allow a repeat.

Start with the records. Find out who got the message, where it appeared and whether the order could still be changed. Then build a different design and test it against eight encounters.

## What you will be able to do

- Put decision support at the point where it can change the decision.
- Watch how ordering actually happens before designing support for it.
- Explain why adding a warning without watching how clinicians order can backfire.
