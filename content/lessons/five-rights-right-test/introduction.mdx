# Designing Repeat-Test Guidance

You are the pathology resident working with the laboratory director and the electronic health record (EHR) team. Last month, the team added guidance for repeat hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) orders. Clinicians say the message arrives after they have already signed the order. Collection staff get it too, even though they did not order the test.

The laboratory director asks you to look at the ordering audit, compare it with the earlier laboratory report and redesign the guidance. The clinical question is whether the information already available should be looked at before another test is ordered. The rule cannot answer that without the clinician's judgment.

The local specification asks for a review when there is a usable final result collected within the last 90 days, or when another order is still pending. That window is the trigger for review in this case, not a general recommendation on how often HbA1c should be checked. A documented clinical reason must always allow a repeat.

## Terms used in this case

- **Clinical decision support (CDS):** information or an action, built into the EHR, that helps someone make a decision about care.
- **Five rights of CDS:** the right information, to the right person, in the right format, through the right channel, at the right time.
- **Order composer:** the screen where a clinician chooses and edits an order before signing it.
- **Inline suggestion, soft stop and hard stop:** an inline suggestion sits beside the order; a soft stop interrupts but lets the clinician continue; a hard stop blocks the order.
- **Order audit:** the record of who saw a message, when, and what they did with the order.

## Your task

Start with the records. Find out who got the message, where it appeared and whether the order could still be changed. Then design different guidance and test it against eight encounters. Finish by sorting six other examples of decision support by type.

## What you will be able to do

- Use an order audit to find who received guidance and whether it arrived before the order was signed.
- Choose the information, person, format, place and timing for a piece of ordering guidance.
- Test a design against encounters that should and should not get guidance, including a justified repeat.
- Name the kind of decision support used in six laboratory examples.
