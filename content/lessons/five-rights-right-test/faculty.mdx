# Faculty guide: Designing Repeat-Test Guidance

## At a glance

Allow 35 minutes. Use individually or in a small group. Project the case records and the exercise controls; keep the answer key out of view until learners commit to a decision.

Learning objectives: place decision support at the decision it can improve; observe the ordering workflow; justify the first local check. PIER: 6.1, 6.2, 4.2.

## Learning goals and facilitation

PIER 6.1–6.2 are addressed through identifying CDS approaches and matching support to a clinical workflow. Allow about 35 minutes: 5 for the audit, 10 for teaching and design, 10 for exception testing, and 10 for discussion.

Before learners change anything, ask: “Who can change this order at 09:14:20, and what extra work is now required?” Require reference to the ordering audit. Then ask them to distinguish the format from the channel and to propose a non-alert form of support for another laboratory task.

The specification is institution-specific. Do not teach 90 days as a clinical guideline for HbA1c. The exercise concerns review of available information and preservation of clinical judgment.

## Expected observations and alternative choices

The original configuration delivers late, to an inbox, without patient-specific context, and to collection staff. Three encounters without qualifying history correctly show no guidance even before repair; learners should preserve these behaviors.

The reviewed design selects the ordering clinician, contextual information, the order composer, before signing, and an inline suggestion. All eight checks then meet the specification. A soft stop also passes because it preserves discretion; ask what additional risk or usability evidence would justify its interruption. A hard stop fails for all displayed review cases and particularly for the documented exception.

The action buttons record a response for the selected encounter. Ask learners what additional chart evidence they would need before judging either action appropriate. They should not claim that cancellation itself improves care.

## Assess transfer

Ask learners to describe an order set, a result display, and a reflex protocol, and explain why none is necessarily an interrupting alert. A useful response identifies the user, decision, data source, clinical authority, and point in the workflow.

Ask what happens when a final report is corrected after the suggestion was generated. Expect re-evaluation of the source, display, and active decision rather than indefinite suppression.

## Common misconceptions

- “Send a weekly list after discharge.” Ask: what does the record actually establish? Timely patient-specific information can prevent an avoidable repeat while preserving justified exceptions. Test workflow and safety before broad deployment.
- “Interrupt every order regardless of history.” Ask: which conclusion would still need evidence? Timely patient-specific information can prevent an avoidable repeat while preserving justified exceptions. Test workflow and safety before broad deployment.
- “Add a warning without observing ordering.” Ask: what must be checked before making the change? Identify who decides, what information is available and whether the proposed support reaches that person at the useful moment.

## Discussion prompts

1. What supports the case decision? A strong answer includes a specific record and the distinction: place decision support at the decision it can improve.
2. What would you check locally? A strong answer includes: observe the ordering workflow, because identify who decides, what information is available and whether the proposed support reaches that person at the useful moment.
3. Who needs to participate? A strong answer includes a clinical service representative and informatics lead and separates technical work from clinical approval.

## Group variant

Assign laboratory director, LIS analyst, laboratory operations and clinical service roles. Add clinical informatics and a patient advocate when considering ordering or chart use. Ask each person to state one requirement, one risk and the evidence needed before approval. The director then summarizes what can proceed and what must be resolved.

## Assess transfer

Sketch one decision-support intervention with its recipient, timing, exception and safety measures. A strong response names the record, responsible person, first check and an unresolved risk. Use the three learner retrieval questions to distinguish an unsupported shortcut from a reasoned decision.
